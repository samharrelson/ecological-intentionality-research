**Field Notes** is the public research notebook for this project. Its first and currently central series, **Black Walnut Field Notes**, emerges from a practice of repeatedly sitting with a black walnut tree in Spartanburg, South Carolina and attending to what happens there.

The practice began in graduate work in ecology, spirituality, religion, and conservation and continued through later work on vegetal life, phenomenology, empathy, ecological intentionality, and the theology I have come to call the Ecology of the Cross.

The larger Field Notes project remains open to other creatures, places, watersheds, and ecological encounters as the research develops. The black walnut remains the primary philosophical test case.

The private field journal is intentionally messy. It includes audio transcripts, research questions, unfinished interpretations, observations, images, citations, and things that turn out to be wrong. Those notes remain private.

What appears here is a curated public layer: selected observations and reflections that are useful in their own right and that allow the shape of the research to remain visible while it develops.

The workflow is:

**sit → private field note → philosophical analysis → research integration → curated public field note → verified public archive**

After publication, the curated public layer is also versioned in the project’s GitHub repository. The repository records what has already crossed the public boundary; it does not decide what becomes public.

The workflow is part of the method, not only a way to move finished ideas onto a website. It is designed to preserve provenance, keep encounter distinct from inference and stronger interpretation, make revision visible, and require verification before a claim or file crosses into the public layer. In that limited sense, the workflow is designed to exert methodological resistance on the researcher.

One larger question of the project is whether the form of scholarship can embody some of the same epistemic virtues the inquiry recommends: attention to relation and difference, awareness of limits, corrigibility, and openness to changed conditions. The public archive and its version history are therefore an experiment in making the genealogy of an argument inspectable rather than presenting only its finished form.

The goal is not to turn every encounter into an argument. It is to practice attention carefully enough that the encounter can resist the categories brought to it.

Over time, the notes gather around recurring questions: attention and possession, presence and remainder, place and agency, vegetal life, creaturely otherness, and the possibility of relation without mastery.

**Open research:** A versioned public research layer for this project is available on [GitHub](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research), including curated field notes, concept pages, methodological documentation, and the revision history of the inquiry. The repository records material that has already been deliberately made public; it is not a mirror of the private research vault.

## Who has a say in the record?

*November 16, 2026*

I want this notebook to make the development of an argument available to other readers. That requires an account of what the public record leaves out, how other people enter it, whose knowledge it draws on and what publication might do to the inquiry itself. These are part of the question pursued in [issue #7](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/7).

### Selection and the limits of an inspectable record

I choose what crosses from the private journal into these pages. The public notes preserve selected observations, interpretations, challenges and revisions. They are not a complete sample of what happened in the yard or of every difficulty in the research.

The export boundary helps keep private files out of the repository. It cannot decide whether somebody is recognizable in a paragraph, whether an account is fair or whether I have permission to share it. Those questions remain part of my editorial responsibility. The [public source map](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/PUBLIC-SOURCE-MAP.md) describes the technical boundary; a successful export does not establish the adequacy of every editorial decision.

The two correction chains traced in [issue #7](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/7#issuecomment-6801761406) show different degrees of access. Readers can compare the November 6 mowing account with its November 7 correction because both are public. The November 11 correction of my description of the ring reports wording from a private recording. Readers can inspect the published correction and its reasons, but cannot independently compare it with that recording.

Both can contribute to an argument's history, provided I state the difference. A repeatable export, an inspectable revision and a reproducible research result are different achievements. Public correction also selects failures I can responsibly describe. Readers should not infer that these are all the failures, or that my willingness to publish a correction establishes the adequacy of the corrected account.

### Participation and public representation

Taking part in a shared activity and agreeing to its public representation are different matters. A report of agreement about care in the yard cannot itself establish permission to recount somebody's reasons. A note's silence about discussion of publication also cannot establish that no such discussion occurred.

I can describe my own reasoning while keeping clear that my report gives no direct access to another person's experience. Leaving out names helps, but recognizable relationships and circumstances can still matter. The relevant questions concern the particular account, its audience, what the people involved understand about it and whether they can decline or seek changes.

Adams, Holman Jones and Ellis describe process consent as continuing consultation through a project's design, fieldwork, drafting and sharing. They do not reduce that work to obtaining a form once. Their account gives me a resource for considering how representation can remain answerable to the people it involves. It does not establish what any person in this notebook has agreed to, or classify ordinary household conversation as a formal research role.[^practice-consent]

Version history introduces a further tension. It can preserve a failed interpretation so that a later reader can follow the revision. It can also preserve personal material after its removal from a current page. A responsible account of versioning needs room for privacy correction, including changes to history where warranted. An explanation of a redaction should protect the person concerned, and removing material within my control cannot guarantee its removal from copies elsewhere. The value of an argument's history does not give me an unrestricted claim on someone else's story.

### Community knowledge and editorial authority

My proposed use of a place is only one contribution to understanding it. People who use, tend or remember that place may know things I do not. A garden I would like to propose is not already a congregation's purpose. Learning how the ground is used and how decisions are made may change the question before a plan is adopted.

The same discipline matters when I read sources. Watts's Place-Thought challenges the human-centered starting terms of this inquiry within particular Indigenous intellectual and political contexts. I cannot make it a generic endorsement of my preferred language of relation.[^practice-watts]

Smith describes reporting back and sharing knowledge as continuing reciprocal work within Indigenous research. On her account, making a report available does not complete the relationship; sharing includes making the reasoning and theory accessible to the people concerned.[^practice-smith] This challenges any assumption that a public link is, by itself, an adequate return. Her account does not license me to describe my yard practice as an Indigenous methodology.

Harris's ecowomanist method gives another situated challenge. Honoring Black women's experience and ecomemory can recover knowledge and make the telling itself an act that strengthens agency.[^practice-harris] My interpretation of a place cannot stand in for histories and experiences I have not gathered. These sources differ in their commitments, but each requires more than adding another quotation to a framework I control.

Community knowledge may be shared under particular conditions. Its usefulness to my argument does not settle what I may reproduce. I retain editorial control over which challenges become public corrections and how I describe them. That control is a limit readers should be able to see, even when the challenge originated with another person, a text or the encountered place.

### What publishing can do to the inquiry

A daily series and a roadmap can encourage a finding every morning. They can also make a published correction look like evidence of my own carefulness. Bateson's discussion of self-validating contextual habits gives me a reason to examine how the form of this practice may help organize what I recognize as a result. It does not establish that every confirmation is rationalization, or that each reversal is progress.[^practice-bateson]

Publishing a plan can also affect the conditions under which I carry it out. Announcing a plan might make it harder to abandon; a genre that rewards surprises might create the opposite pressure. The order of publication and action cannot establish either effect. I need to distinguish what occurred from a causal story about what publicity made me do. The same applies to other people's participation: anticipating an audience could affect what someone feels free to say, but that possibility is not evidence that it happened in a particular conversation.

Preparation is part of this question too. I use AI tools to help prepare scripts, check sources, clean transcripts and draft the public notes. I review the resulting accounts and remain responsible for their claims and their publication. A prepared distinction read aloud at the tree does not become an independent discovery by entering the recording. The notes therefore distinguish what preparation supplied from what the encounter added, contradicted or left unsettled.

The useful response is to keep the evidential demand attached to the claim. An unchanged decision, an unresolved identification or a morning without a new finding can belong in the record. A criterion should be checked for the bounded work it names, with the public evidence linked. It should not stand in for proving the whole theory or for finishing an ongoing obligation of care.

This analysis makes the public record's limits more explicit. It does not yet establish the method's novelty, its effectiveness or its transferability. The comparison with other methodologies and the methodological paper remain separate work.

## What this practice inherits

*November 19, 2026*

Returning to one tree, recording my reflections and publishing corrections brings together practices that already have histories. I want to say what I inherit, what this particular arrangement makes available, and what remains beyond it. I compare three approaches through the same questions: what counts as evidence, how an interpretation can be challenged, whose account enters, and what another reader can use.

### Environmental and field phenomenology

David Seamon describes first-person environmental phenomenology as inquiry through the researcher's situated experience. Walking, looking, writing and attending to a place supply material for interpretation. His account of reliability already includes public scrutiny and intersubjective corroboration. He also questions descriptions that attend to visible activities while leaving out what places mean to their inhabitants.[^practice-seamon]

My repeated walnut sits belong within that inheritance. I describe an encounter, distinguish what I perceived from what I inferred, and let subsequent encounters trouble the account. A reader can ask whether my description supports the concept I bring to it. Publishing revisions makes that relationship easier to follow, but it doesn't invent the demand for criticism.

The limit becomes apparent when I write about the walnut's good. My experience can disclose how the tree matters to me without establishing what benefits it. That question requires ecological evidence. An account of how the yard appears to me also leaves its other inhabitants' experiences and its particular history partly inaccessible.

### Reflexive and relational autoethnography

Adams, Holman Jones and Ellis describe autoethnography through personal experience examined in relation to cultural beliefs, practices and relationships. Reflexivity includes the researcher's position and participation in those relationships. Their account of process consent follows a project through design, research, writing and sharing.[^practice-adams-method] I use this particular approach within the wider ethnographic family as my second comparison.

My position matters to this inquiry. I have access to a yard, time to return, commitments to a congregation, and responsibility for what I publish. The language of care and the expectation of a productive sit can organize what I notice. Other people's questions have changed what I investigate. Their presence deserves an account beyond its usefulness to my argument.

This approach asks how relationships enter my interpretation and what obligations arise when I write about them. My notebook can examine my participation without representing the household or congregation as a whole. A reported conversation leaves questions about another person's experience, continuing agreement and authority within an undertaking. Making my perspective explicit is a beginning; those questions may require further conversation and a different research design.

### Open notebook science

Jean-Claude Bradley's account of open notebook science makes raw experimental records, including failed and aborted experiments, available for inspection. Readers can examine the evidence behind a report beyond its condensed conclusions.[^practice-bradley] My public record is curated. It does not release full recordings, private conversations or every preparatory document, so it does not meet that unrestricted notebook model.

I take from it the value of preserving an unsuccessful attempt alongside the reason it failed. In my case, the attempt may be an interpretation. Another reader should be able to identify the claim, locate its challenge and assess the revised conclusion. That is a possible use for an argument's history.

The limits matter to that use. Philosophical correction and experimental replication make different demands. When a source remains private, readers can examine my published reasoning but cannot independently check every part of its basis. A retrievable history also doesn't establish that anyone has examined or reused it. I need to describe the access I actually provide.

### Two corrections across the three approaches

The November 6–7 mowing sequence gives the comparison something definite. I first described a change from tidying the yard to caring for the lives supported by fallen leaves. The next day's account complicated that contrast: soil care and safe access had already belonged to my earlier purposes. The correction changed what the practical revision could establish about the formation of a new end.[^practice-corrections]

The November 12–13 account of return supplies a different failure. I had assigned a necessary role to coming back after an act. A question during planning or contact with the plants during action could also have brought the ring into consideration. Those were counterexamples to my general claim, rather than additional events I observed. The reported return still mattered in that particular sequence.

For each case, phenomenological description helps specify the experience being interpreted. Reflexive inquiry asks how my preferred story of ecological learning shaped its telling and how others enter it. A public history of revision lets a reader trace the narrowing of the claim. These approaches contribute different questions to the same material. The public links make selected reasons for revision available; they do not supply missing ecological evidence or other participants' accounts.

### What today's disruption adds

In [November 19's reflection](../field-notes/2026-11-19%20-%20What%20Holds%20a%20Purpose%20Together.md), I describe a congregational dinner changing to pickup during a water outage. Food provision continued, while the conversations I valued at the tables became unavailable. I also report a first shared leaf observation after the preparation described the day before.

The three approaches give me different work to do with those accounts. I can describe the changed practical situation, examine which purposes I take to be central, and preserve the difference between my report and a dated public notice. I can also distinguish a reported observation from an inference about what lives in the litter. My perspective doesn't settle what the changed meal meant to everyone, and our first look does not establish what the place supports. The method has to keep those questions available.

### What I am proposing

My contribution is a procedure for making changes in an argument inspectable: an initial claim, a challenge, a decision, the uncertainty retained, and a retrievable public source. A failed interpretation could help another reader avoid an inference, question my revision or adapt the procedure to different material.

That proposal needs a test beyond my account of its promise. Someone else should be able to recover what changed and why, identify an uncertainty I preserved or missed, and explain what they could reuse. The procedure would fall short if my selection made every correction look like progress or left the necessary evidence unavailable. This comparison locates the proposal among established practices. The fuller methodological paper still has to examine its effectiveness and transferability.

# Contributing to Ecological Intentionality

Thank you for your interest in the Ecological Intentionality public research layer.

This repository is a versioned public record of an ongoing inquiry in ecology, phenomenology, religion, and creaturely relation. It is downstream from a private research environment and intentionally contains only material that has already crossed a deliberate public boundary.

Contributions are welcome, but they should preserve that distinction.

## What kinds of contributions are especially helpful

Useful contributions include:

- factual corrections;
- broken-link reports;
- citation or bibliographic corrections;
- source suggestions;
- accessibility improvements;
- documentation improvements;
- fixes to export, privacy, or publication scripts;
- corrections to metadata or repository structure.

Substantive philosophical disagreement is also welcome, but it is usually better raised first as an issue rather than as a pull request that rewrites an argument.

## Public-boundary rule

Please do not submit or request material from the private Obsidian vault.

The repository should never be treated as a mirror of the private research environment. Public material enters this repository only after an intentional editorial decision.

Do not include:

- private notes;
- unpublished family or personal information;
- private correspondence;
- local filesystem paths;
- private vault structure;
- credentials or secrets;
- copyrighted source PDFs or substantial excerpts that are not licensed for redistribution.

## Field notes

The dated field notes are preserved as records of specific public research moments.

Corrections to obvious errors, broken links, metadata, or formatting are welcome. Proposed changes to interpretation, argument, or wording should normally begin as an issue so the historical character of the record can be preserved.

## Concept and method pages

Concept, method, and project-orientation pages are more explicitly revisable and may change as the inquiry develops.

If proposing a substantive change, explain:

1. what problem the change addresses;
2. which public note, source, or argument motivates it;
3. whether the change corrects an error, clarifies a claim, or proposes a new interpretation.

## Sources

Source suggestions are welcome.

Please provide enough bibliographic information to identify the work and briefly explain its relevance to the inquiry.

Do not upload copyrighted articles, book scans, or other source files unless redistribution is clearly permitted.

## Pull requests

For non-trivial changes, opening an issue first is encouraged.

Pull requests should:

- remain focused;
- explain what changed and why;
- avoid unrelated formatting changes;
- preserve existing links and provenance where possible;
- pass the repository privacy guard;
- avoid introducing private-path markers or non-public material.

## Questions

If you are unsure whether a proposed contribution belongs in the public research record, open an issue first.

*October 14, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 28 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What would I have to encounter or learn to change my mind?

## Field Notes

A clear, sunny morning, with cool air that makes it comfortable to stay beneath the walnut. Sunlight catches the leaves overhead. The canopy is still green and yellow, and I notice more yellow leaves on the ground than I remember from a few days ago.

That comparison already reaches beyond the present view. I see leaves here now; I remember a different distribution. I have not counted them or watched each one fall. Some familiar branch sections are visible, while others remain hidden behind foliage.

## Reflection

Autumn makes it tempting to turn every difference into a story of development. There is a story here, but this morning's view does not tell all of it. The leaves on the ground do not show me their particular falling, and remembering the canopy does not make that missing event something I witnessed.

[Attention](../concepts/Attention.md) includes recognizing how much a familiar view carries: bodily comfort, remembered mornings, expected change. None of that makes the encounter false. It does mean that I need to know what supports each claim.

[Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) changes with the question. A branch hidden by leaves might become visible from elsewhere. The history of a fallen leaf requires something more than another angle. The question of the walnut's experience is different again; greater visibility would not by itself answer it.

## Research Connection

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) helps resist the idea that I first receive isolated sensations and then assemble a tree. Familiarity, horizons, bodily position, and temporal continuity already belong to how one walnut appears. A later observation can correct a judgment within that experience without overturning the account of its structure.

During the sit I bring one hand against the other and ask about touching and being touched. Husserl's *Ideas II*, §36, already distinguishes bodily contact from localized sensation and describes the doubling involved when bodily parts touch. The exercise focuses a question; it does not establish what the walnut feels.

[Maurice Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) develops the sensing body's belonging to the sensible world through flesh and reversibility. In *The Visible and the Invisible*, “The Intertwining—The Chiasm,” reversibility remains incomplete. Difference belongs within the relation. But moving from bodily self-relation to that broader account requires an argument; an exercise with my hands cannot carry the whole claim.

This leaves [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) with work to earn. A richer description may educate my attention without discovering a new structure. The useful question is what a further encounter could require me to revise—and how far that revision would reach.

## Question Carried Forward

**What would change my understanding of this walnut, and what further argument would be needed to change my understanding of encounter itself?**

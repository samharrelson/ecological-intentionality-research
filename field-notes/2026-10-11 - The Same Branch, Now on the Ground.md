*October 11, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 28 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What has changed in this encounter, and what does that change allow me to say?

## Field Notes

Heavy overnight rain was easing. Light showers continued, but the clouds were beginning to open and sunlight was returning. The walnut stood above wet ground and scattered leaves, its canopy still mostly green with yellow appearing among the leaflets.

I returned to the marked leaves photographed yesterday. They remained attached, their wet surfaces showing holes, dark spots, and brown patches. Then I noticed the branch with shelf-like fungi that I had observed on October 4. It was now lying on the ground below its former position. The earlier photograph, its distinctive arrangement, and remembered location gave me grounds to recognize the same branch.

## Reflection

A week ago, the visible fungi prompted questions about the branch's condition. Today, one thing is better established: its physical attachment has ended. That does not settle the questions raised last week. I do not know whether the wood was already dead or weakened, what part the weather played, or whether several processes contributed to the break.

The distinction matters for [Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md). Earlier attachment can be remembered and documented; present detachment can be observed. The falling belongs between those encounters. Recognizing the branch more confidently does not make that missing event something I watched happen.

Its position has also changed what I can do. The wood is now within reach. I could examine or move it, but access alone does not decide what care requires. Nor would possible later benefits to other organisms establish that the break was good for the walnut. The October 4 question persists: lives can belong within one field without sharing one good.

## Research Connection

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) provides a strong account of recognizing a thing across changing appearances. Memory, temporal horizons, familiarity, and motivated attention help the earlier encounter bear on the present one. His distinction between retention and recollection also keeps remembering last week's branch separate from the immediate continuity of this morning's perception. Neither supplies an experience of the fall itself.

[Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) sharpens another distinction in *Finite and Eternal Being*. A dead branch can remain a material part shaped by the living whole without remaining a member permeated by its life. Applying that distinction here means that physical separation need not mark the moment living membership ceased. Her metaphysical account gives me a question to investigate, not a diagnosis of this branch.

[Maurice Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) helps describe how the branch's changed position alters bodily possibilities. His later account of flesh also makes difference internal to belonging. Yet shared belonging does not by itself determine the good of the fungi, branch, or whole tree. What late flesh adds beyond the earlier accounts remains a question for close reading.

[Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) is being tested at these transitions: from recognition to documented change, from change to causal explanation, and from practical access to warranted care. Today's encounter strengthens some claims while leaving others open. It has not established a new intentional structure.

## Question Carried Forward

**What further evidence would connect this branch's observed detachment to its biological history and to an adequate response?**

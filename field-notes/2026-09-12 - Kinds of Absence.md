*September 12, 2026 · Black walnut · 20 minutes*

## Prompt

> What kind of absence am I encountering when the walnut exceeds what is presently given?

## Field Notes

Clouds, light rain, and wind moved through the yard during this sit. From where I stood and sat, one side of the walnut’s trunk was visible while the opposite side remained concealed by the tree’s own opacity. Some branches and compound leaves appeared clearly, others only partially, and many were hidden behind other leaves, branches, and fruit.

*September 12, 2026. The walnut appears through overlapping profiles: trunk, canopy, leaves, fruit, and the absences produced by position and opacity.*

Movement changed what was available to perception. Walking around the trunk revealed profiles that had been hidden. Wind shifted the canopy so that leaves concealed a moment before became visible without my moving at all.

I tried to notice when I knew something was there because I had seen it before rather than because it was actually given in the present moment. That distinction kept returning: perception, memory, anticipation, and inference do not all disclose the walnut in the same way.

## Reflection

The question today was whether all hiddenness is the same.

The unseen reverse side of the trunk is one kind of absence. It is not visible from my present position, but I can walk around the tree and perceive it directly. This is a perspectival or horizonal absence: what is absent now can become present through another profile.

Other dimensions of the walnut are absent differently. Its earlier growth, future development, most of its root system, and many physiological processes are approached through memory, anticipation, repeated encounter, inference, or scientific knowledge.

But there is a stronger possibility that should not be confused with either of these. If the walnut is a living center whose life includes an interiority of its own, then no multiplication of my perspectives would become the walnut’s own mode of being.

That stronger claim is not something I want to assume simply because the tree is alive.

A methodological distinction emerged from the sit:

> **living ≠ interiority ≠ subjectivity ≠ empathically accessible other**

The word “interiority” itself needs care. There is physical interiority beneath bark and within tissues; biological or organizational interiority in the coordinated processes of a living organism; and the much stronger claim of experiential interiority: a lived perspective belonging to the being itself.

The first two do not automatically establish the third.

## Research Connection

This sharpens the meaning of [Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md). Remainder cannot simply mean everything I have not yet perceived or learned. Some absence can be overcome through movement. Some can be reduced through memory, repeated encounter, or scientific knowledge. A genuinely irreducible otherness, if there is one, would be different in kind.

That distinction matters for [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md). The practice may not give privileged access to another being’s hidden interiority. Its philosophical work may instead lie in training attention to distinguish what is perceptually given, what is inferred, and what must remain unresolved rather than filled in by projection.

The black walnut continues to function as a [Vegetal Encounter](../concepts/Vegetal%20Encounter.md) in precisely this sense: not an illustration of a finished philosophy, but a test case that can refuse the concepts brought to it.

This also keeps a question from Edith Stein in view. Non-primordiality names a form of access to another’s experience without that experience becoming primordial for me. Whether anything analogous occurs across the human–plant difference remains an open question, not a conclusion.

## Question Carried Forward

**Does the walnut give any phenomenological warrant for distinguishing the alterity of another living center from the ordinary horizonal incompleteness of a perceptual object?**

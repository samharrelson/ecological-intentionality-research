*October 6, 2026 · Black walnut · recorded reflection: at least 21 minutes*

## Prompt

> What changes when I describe a walnut as “still attached” rather than “attached”?

## Field Notes

Sunlight returned after the overcast mornings. The air was cool, around 55°F, with blue sky and a light breeze. A couple of walnuts hung among mostly green leaves; some leaves were yellow. Birds, squirrels, and ants were present around the tree.

The fruit caught my attention because I remembered watching other walnuts fall in recent days. Today's attachment was visible. The earlier falls belonged to memory, and the prospect of these fruits detaching belonged to expectation. I did not record a walnut falling during this sit.

## Reflection

“Still” does quiet work. It places the fruit against what has already happened and what I expect next. Removing the word does not change the visible walnut, but returning it makes the temporal dimension of the encounter explicit. Familiarity with this tree already helps determine what stands out.

This is a small exercise in [Attention](../concepts/Attention.md): learning to distinguish the sources of a description without pretending that ordinary perception begins free of memory or knowledge. A few attached fruits do not establish a complete count, and green and yellow leaves do not by themselves explain the timing or cause of each leaf's change.

Repeated [Vegetal Encounter](../concepts/Vegetal%20Encounter.md) makes another question harder to avoid. How do these differing conditions belong to one developing life? The canopy does not undergo seasonal change all at once. Understanding its colors and fruit as phases of development draws on previous encounters and biological knowledge as well as what is visible today.

[Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) therefore acquires a temporal dimension. Returning to the tree can make its history more familiar without bringing all of that history into view. I can reasonably expect fruit to fall while remaining unable to say when this walnut will detach or what will become of it. Familiarity makes some expectations better grounded; it does not make the future available for inspection.

## Research Connection

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) gives the first discipline. In *Ideas I* §§77–82, retention of a just-past phase differs from recollecting an earlier event, and immediate protention differs from explicitly anticipating a future one. Husserl also describes every experience as a flow of becoming with horizons of before and after. Remembering fruit fall days ago and expecting later detachment therefore should not be collapsed into the immediate temporal structure of this moment. His account makes the present richer than a snapshot before any new ecological concept is introduced.

[Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) changes the level of the question. In *Finite and Eternal Being*, plant becoming has a temporal structure and unfolds within a natural situation. Later, Stein says that ripe fruit belongs to the vital unity of the generating individual even as a new life may begin in what is generated through a further transition. That makes “still attached” more than a temporal phrase: it also raises a question about present membership in one living whole. Applying Stein’s metaphysics to this walnut remains an interpretive step; the photograph does not establish it.

[Maurice Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) makes the temporal comparison harder before the late vocabulary of flesh and *écart* even arrives. In *Phenomenology of Perception*, he takes up Husserl’s passive synthesis of time as a **transition synthesis**: moments hold together by passing into one another rather than by being externally joined afterward. He describes the present as carrying its genesis and speaks of the cohesion of a life through temporal *ek-stase*. The later ontology therefore has a higher burden than I first gave it: what does *écart* add beyond Husserlian temporal synthesis, Merleau-Ponty’s own transition synthesis, and Stein’s account of living form?

[Robin Wall Kimmerer](../concepts/Robin%20Wall%20Kimmerer.md) keeps the inquiry answerable to the fruit's other possibilities. Her work on plant relations and gift directs attention toward food, seed, and the lives with which fruit may become involved. My application here is to resist making the walnut only a marker of time. Possible usefulness is not yet an invitation to take, and an anticipated future is not an observed outcome.

For [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md), the burden remains concrete: show what sustained encounter contributes after these distinctions have done their work. This sit strengthens one possibility now under pressure: ecological intentionality may name a discipline of transitions among perception, memory, anticipation, biological development, metaphysical living form, and stronger ontological claims. Coordinating those transitions responsibly may matter more than naming another structure of perception.

## Question Carried Forward

**What warrants seeing this moment as a phase of one life, and what would the later ontology of *écart* add after temporal synthesis and living form have already done their strongest work?**

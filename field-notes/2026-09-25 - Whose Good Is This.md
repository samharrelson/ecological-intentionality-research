*September 25, 2026 · Black walnut · 20 minutes*

## Prompt

> When I speak of the good of the walnut, whose good do I mean?

## Field Notes

The morning was clear and about sixty-five degrees after temperatures in the fifties. Sunlight moved through a canopy that remained mostly green, though more leaves had yellowed. Walnuts remained overhead and lay on the ground. The soil was wet and cool after recent rain, and branches brought down by a storm earlier in the week were still present.

The field contained different kinds of evidence. Green and yellowing leaves, fruit, broken wood, movement, sunlight, and cool soil were presently sensible. The preceding hot, dry months and the fuller green canopy belonged to memory. Biological knowledge made fruiting, seed, dormancy, germination, consumption, and decomposition intelligible. Calling any of these possibilities flourishing, impairment, fulfillment, or loss introduced a further judgment.

## Reflection

Autumn makes a simple scale of more and less inadequate. More growth is not automatically flourishing. Less visible activity is not automatically decline. Leaves yellow and detach, fruit falls, and dormancy approaches as part of the tree’s temporal life. Yet this does not make every loss good. Seasonal leaf fall and a storm-broken limb cannot be treated as equivalent simply because both involve detachment.

A walnut on the ground sharpens the problem. It could germinate, be eaten by a squirrel, decompose, or fail to develop. Which possibility is good depends partly on what bears the good. The seed, the parent tree, the black walnut form, the squirrel, and the surrounding place do not necessarily have the same stake in what happens next.

Three possible centers of normativity begin to appear: this individual tree with its exact history of growth, pruning, wounds, and storms; the specific form whose fulfillment includes fruiting and propagation; and the wider relations among soil, water, weather, animals, fungi, insects, and human activity. These goods can overlap without becoming identical. What is good *through* a being may not be good *for* that being. A dead limb can become habitat without death thereby becoming flourishing for the organism that dies.

This makes [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) answerable to a difficult conjunction: **integrity without isolation; relation without dissolution**. [Place and Agency](../concepts/Place%20and%20Agency.md) prevents the walnut from being treated as self-contained, while [Vegetal Encounter](../concepts/Vegetal%20Encounter.md) prevents the particular tree from disappearing into an ecological whole. [Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) now includes unresolved conflicts among real goods, not only limits of knowledge.

## Research Connection

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) supplies a discipline of levels. *Ideas II* already distinguishes mere nature from valuing and practical attitudes, and describes an original affective “value-reception.” So this clear autumn morning can genuinely appear good *to me* without that value being a natural-scientific property of the weather. But good *for me* is not yet good *for the walnut*. Phenomenology can clarify how value is given without deciding whose good should prevail.

[Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) gives the problem metaphysical weight, and more differentiation than I expected. In *Finite and Eternal Being*, she insists that each living being has its own essential form and life while also making propagation of the specific form central to vegetal existence. Later, in her account of goodness, she says that an existent’s own perfection is its immediate good, while other beings can be mediately good insofar as they contribute to that perfection. Finite goods therefore have different bearers and limited spheres.

But Stein also places those goods within a deeper divine order in which creatures’ perfections ultimately belong together. That creates a new pressure from the walnut: are conflicts among individual, species-form, and relational goods only apparent from my limited perspective, or can ecological life contain losses and incompatibilities that resist final harmonization?

[Robin Wall Kimmerer](../concepts/Robin%20Wall%20Kimmerer.md) keeps the metaphysical question answerable to practice. “Old-Growth Children” shows that affection and good intentions are insufficient without learning how particular trees, animals, disturbances, and weather live together. “The Sacred and the Superfund” makes the problem harder still: restoration can mean ecological function, return toward an earlier community, cultural renewal, reciprocal relationship, responsibility, or home. Her reminder that nature itself is a moving target prevents one static reference state from becoming the whole meaning of ecological good.

[Vanessa Watts](../concepts/Vanessa%20Watts.md) prevents place from becoming inert context around an otherwise self-contained organism. Her account of Place-Thought is a stronger and specifically Haudenosaunee and Anishnaabe cosmological and political claim. Within that framework, what might otherwise be called habitat or ecosystem is not merely a larger functional unit but a society structured through agency, history, communication, and obligation. The narrower pressure carried here is therefore not that “the whole” outranks the individual, but that the category of the whole is itself not neutral.

The argument therefore needs four distinct tasks: **apprehend the condition → articulate the organismic norm → distinguish and order the bearers of good → justify the ethical response**. Today adds two cautions to that bridge: **organismic good is not automatically ecological good**, and **what is good through a being may not be good for that being**. Even an immanent good does not automatically become my obligation.

## Question Carried Forward

**If a living being has an immanent measure of actualization, what bears that good: the historically particular individual, the specific form, the wider relation, or some ordering among all three, and are their conflicts finally reconcilable, or can creaturely goods remain genuinely at odds?**

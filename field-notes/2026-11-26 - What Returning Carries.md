*Thursday, November 26, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 25 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> How does a history of living relationships become part of what I can notice, undertake and share now?

## Field Notes

The walnut's crown looks bare against the gray sky. It is raining hard, and the bark is dark with water. Fallen leaves cover much of the ground. I look down at the central axes of compound leaves and remember when I would have taken them for twigs. Learning that distinction changed what is available for me to recognize this morning.

*Open branching above the leaf-covered ground on a rainy November morning.*

Yesterday I photographed the last yellow blade I could see attached. Later I returned to its absence. I did not witness its fall. Today the branching draws my attention, alongside the fallen structures I have learned to recognize.

## Reflection

An older entry has returned, too. On November 11, 2025, I titled a walnut note “Last Leaf on the Tree” and linked Willie Nelson. The song came to mind again yesterday. After the sit and publication, the older entry resurfaced. Today I can put the two occasions beside one another.[^archive]

The sequence changes what I can ask. I know I've made this association before, and I can examine how the two accounts differ. I still don't know how much the first encounter contributed to yesterday's attention. Finding the record now gives me something further to investigate. Its recovery has become another event in the inquiry.

I am developing ecological intentionality through connections like these. Repeated encounters and learning can form capacities. A particular encounter can give a question or undertaking a more definite shape. The being or situation concerned then contributes to what would count as fulfilling it. Recognizing a leaf axis, establishing an earlier reference and benefiting a tree require different kinds of success.

The walnut has continued through all these visits. Songs, books, photographs and records also enter my relation to it. Their contributions need to be traced with care. A history can help explain what I now notice without making everything in that history perform the same work.

## Research Connection

[Brentano's](../concepts/Franz%20Brentano.md) account keeps intentionality broad enough to include presentation, judgment, love and desire. My deliberate practical purposes belong within that wider directed life.[^brentano] [Husserl's](../concepts/Husserl.md) familiar-melody example sharpens another distinction: anticipating the next sounds differs from enjoying them or willing something to happen. Those directions can intertwine while retaining different conditions of fulfillment.[^husserl]

I need to explain how an acquired capacity enters what I'm doing now. Recognizing the fallen axes lets me follow this walnut's leaf structure; reopening an old entry lets me investigate a history of attention. Husserl already provides substantial resources for understanding these changes.[^husserl] Archives and artifacts can also form attention and redirect inquiry. To argue that a living organism helps constitute the ongoing act, I have to show what its continuing life contributes beyond those resources.

I've got mashed potatoes to finish and people to see later today. Thanksgiving brings receiving and contributing into the same morning. [Gregersen](../concepts/Niels%20Henrik%20Gregersen.md), interpreting Luther's theology of the cross, describes received grace and participation in Christ as enabling works of love and justice. The cross belongs to an account of divine love making responsive agency possible amid human limitation. It gives no license to treat suffering as something another person should endure for our purposes.[^gregersen]

For the Ecology of the Cross, I propose that participation in Christ can form communal capacities to notice needs, share responsibility and revise purposes. The ecological extension asks how those purposes affect other creatures and places. It needs theological grounds and evidence appropriate to the particular lives involved.

[Moltmann's](../concepts/J%C3%BCrgen%20Moltmann.md) Sabbath account adds the good of shared existence. Grateful recollection, rest and enjoyment belong together. Children, servants, strangers and animals belong within its peace; celebration cannot rest on another person's exclusion or imposed cost.[^moltmann] I want that claim beside the capacity to act. A person's place in shared life cannot depend on being able to contribute. Bringing this into Thanksgiving makes me ask who prepares the gathering and whether they also get to enjoy it. The comparison draws on Sabbath's Jewish context and Moltmann's Christian theology; today's meal still lies ahead.

## Question Carried Forward

What does a recovered record enable me to understand now, and what would establish how the earlier encounter helped form that capacity?

I intend to keep returning through winter. Today I also look forward to the shared life beyond the writing. Receiving it and contributing to it can belong together without making enjoyment another accomplishment to complete.

*October 2, 2026 · Black walnut · 20 minutes*

## Prompt

> Whose “I can” is this, what makes this embodied view possible, and what histories remain operative here that my presence and personal memory cannot supply?

## Field Notes

Looking upward from beneath the walnut, branches crossed the field and leaves gathered around openings of blue sky. Morning light entered through the canopy. The view existed from one bodily position: a step left, a crouch, or lying on the ground would have reorganized the branches and opened another field of appearance.

The photograph gives a real appearance. It also excludes nearly everything that might explain the history of this place: no property boundary, house, street, power line, record, former inhabitant, or account of labor appears within the frame. **A beautiful view can be true without being complete.**

## Reflection

Yesterday’s bodily question began with an “I can”: I can approach, circle, touch, sit beneath, and return to this tree. Today the pronoun became less innocent. These possibilities belong to a body, but they are also conditioned by access to this ground and by histories of property, law, labor, family, neighborhood formation, and land use. The sit does not establish which of those histories matter here. It establishes that bodily possibility does not explain itself.

This changes [Attention](../concepts/Attention.md). Looking can make branches, light, scars, boundaries, and relations more determinate. It can also reveal where looking must hand a question to another method. **Attention can disclose the need for history without disclosing the history itself.** Archives, property records, oral histories, law, ecological records, testimony, and community memory answer different questions than perception does.

It also differentiates [Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md). The hidden side of a branch can become visible by moving. A biological process may become scientifically knowable. A property history may be recoverable in records. Another life’s experience remains inaccessible in a different and stronger way. Personal recollection is not the measure of a place’s past, but unknown history should not be treated as evidence for a history I have not established.

The wider frame must still protect the walnut. Historical and structural analysis cannot turn this organism into scenery for a human political narrative. [Place and Agency](../concepts/Place%20and%20Agency.md) must hold together the conditions shaping access and the particular tree whose form, good, and history exceed those conditions.

## Research Connection

[Maurice Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) makes the embodied starting point unavoidable. The “I can” names an operative bodily relation to a world already organized by reach, direction, movement, and practical possibility. But Merleau-Ponty’s own account does not end with a generic motor body. Later in *Phenomenology of Perception*, the social world becomes a permanent dimension of existence, freedom is historically and socially situated, and the self is described as a psychological and historical structure. The live question is therefore not whether Harris adds history to an otherwise ahistorical Merleau-Ponty, but what kind of historical situatedness each account describes and what each asks ecological inquiry to do.

[Melanie Harris](../concepts/Melanie%20Harris.md) changes the stakes through ecowomanism. Her 2016 introduction foregrounds women of African descent and women of color, Black women’s lived experience, ecojustice and environmental justice, agency, earth community, intergenerational sharing, method, and praxis. Her work should not be reduced to an added reminder that context matters. It asks what ecological knowledge is for, whose experience and history it takes seriously, and whether refined perception becomes accountable within structures that distribute access, exposure, harm, and possibility unequally.

[Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) supplies a restraint from the other direction. Widening the historical frame should not dissolve this particular walnut into the structures surrounding it. The tree retains its individual form and proper good. The encounter therefore requires two disciplines at once: do not let the perceiver become historically anonymous, and do not let structural analysis make the walnut disappear.

The pressure on [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) is now practical as well as descriptive. Situated perception is not yet environmental justice; embodied belonging is not yet accountable praxis. A practice that produces a beautifully formed perceiver while leaving harmful arrangements unexamined would remain incomplete. At the same time, the transition cannot be reduced to a division in which phenomenology describes the present and history explains the past. Merleau-Ponty already makes historical and social situation part of embodied existence. Harris presses the project further toward environmental justice, accountable method, and praxis.

## Question Carried Forward

**What would change in the encounter if the histories shaping this place and my access to it became known: the walnut’s appearance, the meaning of my presence, my responsibility, or all three?**

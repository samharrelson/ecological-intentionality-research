*October 29, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 37 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What changes in my way of seeing when the life I return to keeps changing too?

## Field Notes

The walnut stopped me before I intended to sit this morning. Its canopy looked unexpectedly yellow. Later, returning under almost complete cloud cover, I found muted light, a slight breeze, and leaves scattered beneath the tree. The difference from yesterday involves memory as well as what is here now.

*October 29. Green and yellow leaves against a pale sky, with fallen leaves below. The changing light is part of this morning's appearance.*

Nearby leaflets carry green, yellow, and small spots. Gaps open onto the cloudy sky and branching structures. I follow individual leaves fluttering downward.

At one point I intend to study a particular cluster. Three leaves fall elsewhere, and my eyes follow them instead. My attention has moved before I have given myself an instruction to move it.

Later I return to the nearby leaves. They are within reach, and I want to touch them. I stop myself because I am unsure whether touching would disturb them. That hesitation tells me something about my concern, though it does not establish that touching would cause harm.

## Reflection

The canopy is changing unevenly. Some leaves remain green while others yellow and fall. Following a leaf's movement does not tell me the history of its tissues, and recognizing the walnut through those changes does not give me its whole life.

The interruption and the hesitation are different moments of [attention](../concepts/Attention.md). In one, movement draws my eyes away from the object I meant to examine. In the other, a concern about care leads me to withhold a possible action. Neither is adequately described just by saying that the tree and I are connected.

I also find myself wondering how these returns affect encounters elsewhere. I seem more ready to notice that my purposes are not the only ones present. While driving, I find myself considering the people in other cars, whose obligations and reasons for being on the road I cannot know. I've also noticed a practical change at home: I now leave taller grass and other growth around exposed tree roots rather than mowing right up to the trunks, something I began doing intentionally this summer. My reasons include concern for the trees, but I haven't verified the ecological effects of that choice. These are reports about my experience and practice, not yet an explanation of what changed me. Sustained practice, reading, memory, and prior commitments may all matter.

## Research Connection

I spent time last night with [Gregory Bateson](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md) and “Form, Substance, and Difference” in *Steps to an Ecology of Mind*. His person–axe–tree example places the relevant mental process in a circuit of differences passing through tree, perception, bodily movement, and the next stroke. He makes the system's boundary depend on what we are trying to explain, and includes relevant memory (Jason Aronson reprint, pp. 465–466).

That makes him a demanding interlocutor for [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md). An immediate shift of attention, a decision not to touch, and a habit that changes another encounter are different things to explain. A theory of ecological learning already has resources for change that reaches beyond the present scene.

The positive proposal I want to test is that repeated relations with independently unfolding lives help form the possibilities and direction of our intentions. Its stronger version needs to show what the living participant contributes to realizing an act beyond information, learned concern, or the conditions under which care succeeds. Moving images might draw my eyes too; art and literature can change how I encounter the world. Those possibilities deserve a serious comparison.

The leaves have given me a concrete interruption. They have not settled its explanation.

## Question Carried Forward

**What does the walnut's independently changing life contribute to these intentions that movement, memory, learned attention, and a convincing representation would not explain?**

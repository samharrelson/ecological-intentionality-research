*October 17, 2026 · Black walnut practice, coastal comparison · approximately 25 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What changes in an encounter when the weather changes, and what remains to be learned by returning?

## Field Notes

The Atlantic lies beneath a broad clouded sky this morning. A narrow band of warm light remains near the horizon, and waves leave white edges along the shore. Yesterday's bright blue sky is something I remember while looking at this different scene.

The air feels colder. Wind makes itself felt against my skin, and I have added clothing and sought shelter. The change reaches me through more than the view. A few birds visit the balcony and look toward the interior, briefly interrupting the reflection. I cannot tell from that visit exactly what brought them here.

The walnut is elsewhere this morning. I am thinking ahead to the next encounter: whether its canopy will have changed, whether more leaves will be on the ground. Those possibilities are already directing my attention, although I have not yet seen what awaits.

## Reflection

A conversation yesterday about intentionality has stayed with me. It made me notice how much I can anticipate an encounter before it happens, including what I expect it might mean. But those expectations are mine. The encounter may confirm them, complicate them, or turn my attention somewhere I hadn't expected.

I came outside expecting cooler weather. Now the expectation meets the particular feel of the morning. The forecast and the encounter have different roles: one prepared me for conditions; the other gives me wind, cold, light, and water from this bodily situation. Feeling the wind does not let me see the whole atmospheric system described by a weather map.

The anticipated return to the walnut brings another distinction into focus. A new view may confirm or complicate what I expect. Even if more leaves are down, I will not thereby have witnessed their falling or learned the precise causes of the change.

[Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) has a temporal shape here. Yesterday's brightness is remembered, today's cold is felt, and the walnut's next appearance is anticipated. Returning can change what is available without making the interval between visits transparent.

## Research Connection

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) makes the philosophical question harder in a useful way. In *Ideas II* §18(b), changing illumination and other perceptual conditions already belong to the account of how the same thing appears differently. His resources extend beyond a perceiver walking around an object. Weather changing at a familiar standpoint gives no immediate reason to claim that a new kind of intentionality has been discovered.

[Maurice Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) presses a further question about the perceiver's belonging to the sensible world. His later account of flesh includes divergence and incomplete reversibility: relation does not require complete coincidence. But my exposure to the wind cannot establish that ontology, and a shared sensible world does not mean that the ocean experiences the weather as I do.

For [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md), two tasks remain. First, what does late flesh explain beyond the already embodied and temporal accounts available in Husserl and early Merleau-Ponty? Then, what additional work does an ecological account perform? Understanding how conditions appear to me and investigating their consequences for particular living beings require connected but distinguishable inquiries. The need for both does not yet settle whether a new philosophical category is necessary.

## Question Carried Forward

**When I return to the walnut, what will actually revise my expectations, and what will remain unknown even after I can see the tree again?**

*October 21, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 17 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What does sharing this morning let me encounter, and what will require a different kind of inquiry?

## Field Notes

I stand beneath a different part of the walnut, with the sun behind it. High on the trunk, near its division into large branches, a projecting feature catches my eye. It is above my reach. I have noticed it before, but not in this light.

Part of the trunk is lit and part lies in shade. I walk between sunlight and shade, feeling the cold in my own body. Even a small shift of weight changes what I notice on the bark. As the visit continues, I see the light moving across the branches and leaves.

Yesterday I attended to a lower, familiar knot while touching the bark. Today's higher feature remains at a distance. I photograph it without learning how it formed.

*October 21, 2026. A raised, rounded bark feature near the branching trunk. Its visible form does not establish its cause or history.*

Near the end of the visit, a squirrel runs along a branch and briefly pauses. I have the impression that we are looking at one another. I try to take a photograph, but it moves away before I manage it. For a moment, my attention follows a different living being through the same tree.

## Reflection

There are two questions in my interest in the bark feature. Why did it catch my eye this morning? And how did it come to have this form?

Light, position, and familiarity belong to the first question, though I have not established exactly how they combined to draw my attention. The second asks for evidence about the tree's history. A clearer photograph might help describe the feature. An earlier photograph might show that it was already present. Neither would necessarily explain how it arose.

Paying [attention](../concepts/Attention.md) makes the question more definite, but it doesn't supply an answer. The [remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) is also more specific now: I can see the feature, I don't know its history, and I have to decide what kind of inquiry could tell me more.

The squirrel brings another distinction. I can report its movement and my impression of being looked at. The humorous commentary I imagine it making belongs to me. Its particular thoughts remain unknown. Sharing this encounter does not make our experiences identical, and the animal's presence tells me nothing directly about the walnut's experience.

## Research Connection

Returning to [Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) after the sit, I find that *Ideas II* already has substantial resources for what happened. An experienced feature can catch my interest, motivate movement or comparison, and send me toward further investigation. Husserl also distinguishes understanding why I became interested from explaining what caused the feature to form. I can't claim a new kind of intentionality merely because looking has led me toward a biological or historical question.

[Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) makes the bodily part of the encounter harder to overlook. His earlier work already gives me movement and a situated capacity to perceive. His late account of flesh asks a further ontological question about the sensible world to which my own visible body belongs. I'm still working out what this adds. Neither shifting light nor a squirrel's glance establishes that ontology, much less that the walnut perceives me as I perceive it.

For [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md), I still have to ask what this practice contributes beyond those accounts and existing ecological inquiry. If I learn how the protrusion formed, I may see it differently or reconsider a judgment. But I would need to show how that change happens and why my proposed term explains anything that existing approaches don't.

## Question Carried Forward

**How might learning this feature's history change the way I see it, and what evidence would warrant that change?**

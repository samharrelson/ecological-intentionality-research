*September 11, 2026 · Black walnut · 20 minutes*

## Prompt

> Does the walnut become more present as I attend to it, or does its presence become more obviously incomplete?

## Field Notes

From where I sit, one side of the trunk is visibly present while the other disappears from view. Some compound leaves are fully visible, some appear edgewise, and others are hidden behind branches. Wind and changing sunlight continually alter what becomes salient.

*Photograph available on the [illustrated field-notes site](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/).*

*September 11, 2026. Looking upward into the canopy: overlapping leaves and branches reveal some profiles and conceal others.*

My own body belongs to this field of appearance. To see another side of the trunk or hidden branches, I have to move. To see the crown, I have to crane my neck upward. The walnut’s height, opacity, branching form, and position constrain what can appear from where I am.

The roots make the distinction especially clear. A few are exposed above the soil, but most of the root system is not given to perception. I know more is there from previous encounters and biological knowledge, but that knowledge is not the same thing as what is presently visible.

## Reflection

Sustained attention is changing what I mean when I say the walnut becomes “more present.”

More presence does not mean greater possession or complete availability. Instead, attention makes both presence and absence more precise. I notice more clearly what is given, but also where perception ends and memory, inference, expectation, or conceptual knowledge begins.

The walnut therefore became both more present and more incomplete today. Its visible form came into sharper focus at the same time that its excess over any single appearance became harder to ignore.

> **The more determinately the walnut appears, the more determinate its excess over its appearance becomes.**

That excess is not simply a gap waiting to be eliminated by better observation. Some hidden profiles can become visible if I move. Other dimensions of the walnut, its history, future development, and life processes, are available through different modes of knowing.

This creates an important distinction. The unseen reverse side of a trunk is not the same kind of absence as the inner life of another living being. Perceptual excess alone does not establish vegetal interiority.

## Research Connection

Much of this encounter can be described through ordinary structures of embodied perception: changing profiles, bodily movement, memory of previous appearances, and anticipation of unseen ones. That matters for [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md). If ecological intentionality is doing real philosophical work, it cannot simply rename structures already available in phenomenology.

A possibility emerging from the practice is that ecological intentionality may be less a wholly new kind of intentional act than a **discipline or education of intentional life**: sustained attention that makes increasingly explicit the transitions among perception, memory, inference, attribution, anticipation, and interpretation.

That discipline also bears on [Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md). Intimacy may deepen not because remainder disappears, but because attention becomes more precise about what is given and what remains inaccessible.

## Question Carried Forward

**What distinguishes the horizonal excess of a perceptual object from the non-primordially given interiority of another living center?**

*Friday, November 20, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 32 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What has this practice made me capable of noticing, understanding or doing, and what belongs to the lives through which those capacities develop?

## Field Notes

The walnut's branches are mostly bare. A few patches of yellow leaves remain, looking darker now. Sunlight catches the branching against a mostly blue sky, with clouds low on the horizon. I keep noticing shapes that the fuller canopy once made harder to follow.

I look from the leaves on the ground to the bark, then move to a side of the trunk where I don't usually stand. I shift my weight from one leg to the other to follow variations of light and surface. The position is less familiar; the tree has not become a new tree because I have changed where I stand.

A lighter area of bark holds my attention. I have seen it before, but I don't know how it formed. It could concern an old branch, an injury or another part of the tree's development. Looking more closely gives me a better question without giving me the history.

## Reflection

The exposed root brings back the decision to move the climbing dome, an action I had already reported on [November 11](2026-11-11%20-%20Done%20Isn%27t%20Finished.md). Today I'm asking what that history says about the person making the decision.

At first, the walnut's shade helped make the spot seem suitable. Repeated sitting brought the root into a different practical consideration: what might this use of the space mean for the living tree? What I had learned, what I remembered and the possibilities I could imagine all contributed. The concern became part of a purpose that I acted on.

I can now name the capacity. I have become readier to reconsider an arrangement through a life affected by it. That is a change in how I approach a situation. Its history includes the tree without belonging to the tree alone. Nor does moving the structure establish that the root benefited. A purpose can become more attentive while its effects remain unmeasured.

Some of this capacity may travel. I can ask about affected lives in another place. An informed judgment about this particular root still requires knowledge of its conditions. A continuing relationship can provide new evidence, while a habit can persist between visits. These are different ways that a life enters my development. The same process can also form selective or damaging habits. Explaining how I become directed and judging whether I care well are connected tasks, but they need different evidence.

There is a danger here too. If I value the walnut only for educating me, my own development still determines the terms of its importance. The tree continues through processes I did not begin and do not fully know. Intimacy can deepen while that [remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) persists.

## Research Connection

I'm developing [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) as an account of how living relationships help form capacities for directedness. In this case, a living situation becomes available through perception and inquiry, enters practical judgment and helps specify a purpose. The beneficiary's independent conditions also matter to whether that purpose succeeds. My proposal connects the person who becomes able to act with the life that gives this act its particular concern.

Moving beside the trunk brings [Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) into the question. I belong bodily to the world I encounter, and that belonging includes differences of position and access. His late account of flesh helps me describe this sensible belonging without claiming that my experience becomes the tree's. The movement gives the argument something to explain; it doesn't establish the ontology by itself.[^flesh20]

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) already offers resources for embodied familiarity, habituality and practical motivation. [Bateson](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md) gives learning a history within wider relationships.[^formation20] I want to develop the account with those resources. A habit I acquired and a relationship involved in exercising it now need different explanations. Showing how the practice changed me doesn't by itself establish how this relationship helps realize a particular act now.

[Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) helps me describe a living being's own organization and distinguish knowing a possible good from realizing it. [Ruyer](../concepts/Ruyer.md) makes the stronger metaphysical proposal that living unity involves primary consciousness.[^individuality20] I can take that proposal seriously while asking what my account already requires: the walnut's good can help specify my purpose without the walnut becoming an organ of my project. My comparisons in [What Flesh Explains](../essays/what-flesh-explains.md) and [A Life of Its Own](../essays/a-life-of-its-own.md) keep those different claims in view.

[Vanessa Watts](../concepts/Vanessa%20Watts.md) presses on the starting point itself. In her Haudenosaunee and Anishnaabe account of Place-Thought, land and nonhuman agency don't acquire significance only when they enter a human project.[^watts20] I need to let that challenge question who gets to define participation. Her argument has its own intellectual and political commitments; I can't turn it into another name for feedback or a confirmation of my vocabulary.

The theological proposal begins with participation in Christ. I understand the Ecology of the Cross as an account of receiving and sharing life among creatures whose goods cannot be exchanged without remainder. [Gregersen](../concepts/Niels%20Henrik%20Gregersen.md) supplies a positive resource for this account of formation. In his reading of Luther, receiving grace and faith makes possible active responsiveness to others' needs. Later in the 2001 essay, he argues that redemption involves divine transformation beyond subjective consolation and describes creaturely preservation within unity with God.[^gregersen20]

The 2015 introduction extends incarnation's scope to trees, grass, soil and moisture, while distinguishing that reach from the question of final salvation. Deep incarnation does not logically settle universal redemption. Gregersen also describes participation in divine life as differing with the recipient. These passages help me argue for creaturely participation while leaving my hope for transformed communion in need of its own defense. [Colossians 1:15–20](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/colossians/1) brings creation, church, resurrection and reconciliation into that theological argument.

I propose that participation can form a community's capacity to learn, deliberate, share responsibilities and change its use of a place. That requires attention to who carries the costs. A community cannot call another creature's injury its own self-giving. Gregersen's account of suffering's place in evolution remains a difficulty to engage; an explanation of that history cannot by itself authorize avoidable harm. The practical question is whether living requirements can change our purposes before we've decided what role each creature will play.

## Question Carried Forward

What can I carry from this practice into another place, and what will I have to learn again from the lives already there?

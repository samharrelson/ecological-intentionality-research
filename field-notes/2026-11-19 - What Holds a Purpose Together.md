*Thursday, November 19, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 37 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> When the conditions change, what are we still trying to do together, and what would it take to do it well?

## Field Notes

I began the recording by saying that water had returned at home. That was a change from the situation when I prepared the morning's questions, and a different matter from whether the wider system was restored or the advisory lifted. Yesterday's disruption was still shaping the day.[^news19]

It feels warmer this morning. Sunlight comes through the walnut's mostly bare branches. A few dark yellow compound leaves remain below the open upper canopy. I still carry yesterday's surprise at their color.

What catches me today is the litter. It looks thinner and less continuous than the blanket I remember from a week or two ago. More grass shows through. The maple's leaf layer nearby looks fuller, and the difference is striking when I look from one to the other.

Small insects fly above the leaves. I hear birds. Later, a gust interrupts the reflection, and an acorn falls on me. The morning keeps adding things I did not bring in the script.

I can describe the litter's appearance and compare it with what I remember. Explaining how it became thinner requires more. I have not separated the effects of breakdown, movement and disturbance, or learned what is happening beneath the visible layer. Near the end, looking between the walnut and maple litter, I describe the contrast as almost like two bodies of water meeting. It gives me a particular difference to investigate.

## Reflection

### What continued, and what did not

A water disruption changed yesterday's ordinary arrangements. The congregational dinner became food for pickup. Food could still be provided, but we could not sit around tables for the usual meal together.

For me, those conversations are a substantial part of the evening. I value talking with people I do not ordinarily spend much time with. Bringing dinner home met one purpose and left another unfulfilled. I can name what I missed without assuming that everyone sought the same things from the meal.

This makes the question of a continuing purpose more precise. Sometimes the end remains and the means change. Sometimes a commitment becomes a particular task. Sometimes one part of an undertaking succeeds while another cannot be carried through. These descriptions can overlap. I need to say what continued before calling the whole arrangement a success.

Taking water to others gave an existing commitment a particular task. I already had that possibility in mind before going to the store. It would be misleading to describe the delivery as an entirely new end discovered there. What changed was what the commitment called for in those circumstances.

The leaf inquiry has taken another step as well. Yesterday, shared preparation became a brief first observation and a notebook entry. We did not discover new creatures. Beginning the work gives us something to continue, while leaving the inhabitants, the method and its effects open to inquiry. It also leaves a question about shared direction: what can each participant propose or change?

### What the living place contributes

The walnut's continuing life gives this inquiry a particular subject. Its seasonal changes affect what becomes available to notice. Perception, memory, conversation and research can help make that activity consequential for what I intend to investigate or do.

Its life also continues beyond my account. If I intend care, what counts as fulfillment depends partly on the tree's actual conditions. While speaking, I went too far and said I could only assume what would help it. Returning to Stein gives me a reason to revise that. She allows knowledge of a possible good for another being while distinguishing that knowledge from actually bringing the good about.[^stein-good19]

I can investigate what supports this walnut's life without possessing its experience. Knowing what may help, deciding what to do and establishing the consequences are different achievements. That also helps me distinguish kinds of [remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md): an unanswered question about the litter's changes calls for investigation, while another's experience is not mine to possess.

This is a task for [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md): explaining how particular relations help specify a purpose and what would fulfill it, including when the end remains. A maintained pipe, another person's communicated reason and an independently living organism can all affect what I do. Their contributions need different descriptions. Their connection does not make them one intention.

### The grounds for an explanation

The disruption also made me notice how quickly an explanation can acquire force when it fits a concern people already have. I found myself resisting a causal connection I didn't think the available evidence supported. A concern I share about a local proposal does not establish that it caused this event. The utility's November 18 account attributed damage to its distribution lines to a surge following an electrical interruption. I can identify whose account that is without treating it as a complete investigation.[^news19]

That is part of the method of this notebook too. My recollection, a utility notice, the photograph and a philosophical interpretation each give a reader something different to assess. Schutz's distinction between socially derived and socially approved knowledge helps me ask what grounds a report and how its acceptance affects its weight. Repetition and familiarity cannot do all the work of evidence.[^schutz-knowledge19]

## Research Connection

[Alfred Schutz](../concepts/Alfred%20Schutz.md) already gives a strong account of the practical change. In “The Well-Informed Citizen,” he describes conditions on which planning depends, changing systems of interests and events that become relevant without our choosing them. The interests of a parent, a citizen and a church member can press on one another. The relevant regions of a life interpenetrate rather than fit into separate boxes.[^schutz19]

My account can build with this explanation. Its additional task is to show what the particular relations contribute to the content and fulfillment of an undertaking. An interruption revealing dependence does not by itself establish a new form of consciousness.

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) helps me follow how communicated changes become reasons for a response. An announcement can be understood, answered, accepted or challenged. In *Ideas II*, §§51 and 56(a), these relations belong to an account of communal life and meaningful motivation. A commitment can become a more definite act through them without my having adopted an entirely new end.[^husserl19]

[Bateson](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md) asks me to trace the pathways relevant to the activity I am explaining. I can describe how information about available water and another person's need entered my delivery. Explaining the repair operation would require a different account. The circuit is not whatever happens to be causally necessary for the day, and its participants need not share one experience. This helps me ask how a living beneficiary matters within an activity while continuing beyond it.[^bateson19]

[Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) helps with the communal question. Her account allows common work to include different motives and distinguishes realizing communal experience from doing justice to its object.[^stein19] I extend that distinction when I ask whether an inquiry can be genuinely shared yet fail to provide adequate care for the lives it concerns. Participation, authority over the next decision and ecological benefit remain different questions. I also need to keep my use of practical fulfillment clear: carrying through an undertaking is not automatically the same as an intention's intuitive fulfillment or an experience's adequacy to its object.

The theological proposal begins elsewhere. In [1 Corinthians 11:17–34](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Corinthians%2011%3A17-34&version=NRSVUE), the received supper gives Paul grounds to question a gathering that leaves people hungry and humiliated. I take from that relation a question about how a practice's purpose can judge its arrangements. An ordinary congregational dinner is a comparison, not an identification with the Lord's Supper.

For the Ecology of the Cross, I propose that participation in Christ can form the purposes a community carries through changing circumstances. Food, welcome and practical attention to others can guide how an arrangement is revised. [Gregersen](../concepts/Niels%20Henrik%20Gregersen.md) reports a distinction that helps here: Christ's presence in, with and for creatures need not mean identifying God as every event that occurs.[^gregersen19] I can ask what care the situation requires without making the disruption a spiritual lesson or claiming that anyone was excluded. Extending the account toward other creatures requires further understanding of their lives and goods.

## Question Carried Forward

When I say that we have kept a purpose, what have we carried through, and whose account could change my answer?

## Reading notes

The prepared questions already distinguished changing an end from carrying it through changed circumstances. What I added this morning was the account of the conversations I missed, the first shared leaf session and the unexpected contrast in the litter. The later reading helped me correct what I said about knowing the tree's good. I want those different contributions to remain visible in the account.

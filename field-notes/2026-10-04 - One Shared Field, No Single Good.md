*October 4, 2026 · Black walnut · 20 minutes*

## Prompt

> Can beings belong to one shared field without sharing one good, and what kind of belonging is actually given before I infer harmony, conflict, or ecological function?

## Field Notes

Rain had moved in overnight. The morning was overcast and much cooler, near 60°F. Flat light, wet ground, cool air, overlapping branches, the nearby oak, other vegetation, power lines, and the faint line of a fence made the walnut appear within a dense field rather than against an empty background.

A closer photograph showed pale fungal bodies along dark woody tissue, with green and yellowing leaves nearby. That much was visible. The image did not establish whether the branch was dead or dying, whether the fungi were decomposing tissue, whether disease or earlier damage was involved, or whether several processes were occurring together. **Visible fungi are not yet a biological diagnosis.**

## Reflection

The rainy field clarifies three different claims. First, branches, leaves, fungi, rain, soil, infrastructure, and perceiver appear together. Second, some of them stand in real ecological relations. Third, relation may help constitute how living beings become what they are. Each claim is stronger than the one before it. Appearing together does not prove causal interaction, and causal interaction does not by itself establish a relational ontology.

This matters for [Place and Agency](../concepts/Place%20and%20Agency.md). One field is not one organism. The same rain encounters leaf, bark, fungal tissue, soil, clothing, skin, pavement, and roof differently. Shared condition does not mean identical significance; a common world is not a common experience of the world.

The fungi also complicate [Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md). The photograph places lives close together without revealing the full biological relation or making their goods coincide. The fungi may be flourishing while the branch or tree is impaired; insects may gain habitat; soil may later receive material. Those possibilities do not make the branch’s condition good for the branch or the walnut. **Living together does not guarantee flourishing together.**

For [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md), deeper perception of connection can no longer count as the achievement by itself. Attention must differentiate kinds of relation, participants, temporal scales, vulnerabilities, and goods. The better question is no longer whether everything is connected, but what kind of connection this is, whose good it serves, and what difference must remain even when belonging is real.

## Research Connection

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) distinguishes an intentional relation within experience from a real causal relation. That keeps perceptual co-presence from becoming an ecological conclusion. [Maurice Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) then strengthens the account of embodied inherence, but *The Visible and the Invisible* also answers one of the worries this sit carried into the reading. Flesh is not supposed to end in fusion. Merleau-Ponty makes noncoincidence internal to belonging: distance belongs to access, reversibility remains incomplete, and he explicitly rejects the image of one huge animal whose organs the different bodies would be. The remaining question is therefore no longer whether flesh can preserve difference at all, but what kind of difference it preserves.

[Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) protects another side of the problem. Non-primordiality allows the walnut and perceiver to share a sensible world without sharing one experience. In *Finite and Eternal Being*, she also says that not every existent is a good for every other existent: each strives toward its own goodness, while other beings are mediately good insofar as they contribute to that perfection. That gives the walnut work a stronger language for differentiated goods. Yet Stein also places those goods within an ultimately ordered creation, so the possibility of tragic conflict remains a pressure on her account rather than a conclusion she supplies.

[Robin Wall Kimmerer](../concepts/Robin%20Wall%20Kimmerer.md) supplies a concrete ecological counterexample. In her black-ash account, canopy gaps opened by disease or windstorm can create the light young black ash need to flourish. One loss can become another life’s possibility. But Kimmerer also describes black ash and basket makers through symbiosis, reciprocity, balance, and mutual flourishing. Her text therefore prevents an easy move in either direction: relation is not automatically harmony, but loss is not her final word either.

[Vanessa Watts](../concepts/Vanessa%20Watts.md) challenges the starting architecture in which independent beings are established first and relation is added afterward. Her Place-Thought arises within specific Haudenosaunee and Anishnaabe cosmological and political framings, where place and thought are not separated in the first place. Watts also resists accounts that admit nonhumans into an interconnected causal web while retaining human control over what agency can mean. Place-Thought therefore should not be reduced to a portable slogan about relationality or absorbed into Merleau-Pontian flesh.

The fungi are not an illustration of environmental justice, and [Melanie Harris](../concepts/Melanie%20Harris.md) does not supply a biological account of whose good is served here. Her ecowomanist work foregrounds lived experience, earth community, environmental justice, agency, method, and praxis. For this project, that means a shared world cannot become ethically self-sufficient: phenomenological belonging does not by itself establish which historical harms matter, who bears responsibility, or what justice and action require. **A shared world is not yet a just world.**

The source pass revises the constructive proposal: **belonging without coincidence is not yet an account of shared or differentiated good**. Merleau-Ponty now appears stronger than the sit initially allowed. He can make difference internal to belonging without turning the field into a cosmic superorganism. Stein still gives another kind of discipline through non-primordiality and differentiated creaturely good. The older formula “Stein protects otherness; Merleau-Ponty explains belonging” therefore becomes less tidy, because Merleau-Ponty protects a real noncoincidence of his own.

## Question Carried Forward

**If flesh can name a shared field without fusion or a cosmic superorganism, what additional account is needed to distinguish the goods of the beings who belong within it?**

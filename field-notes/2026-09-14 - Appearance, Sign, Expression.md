*September 14, 2026 · Black walnut · 15 minutes*

## Prompt

> What, if anything, does the black walnut express?

## Field Notes

On a clear, warm morning, yellow leaves had begun to stand out within the walnut’s mostly green canopy. More walnuts lay on the ground than remained visibly held in the tree. Brown leaves crunched underfoot; a light breeze occasionally rustled the leaves and shifted a branch.

I noticed how quickly description acquired stronger verbs. Branches *reached* toward light. The tree *responded* to the season. Wounded bark *healed*. Some of these words may be warranted, but each says more than the immediate appearance.

## Reflection

The sit produced a three-part field distinction:

- **Appearance:** what presents itself here: yellow leaf, weighted branch, scarred bark, movement in wind.
- **Sign:** what functions as evidence of something not presently given: seasonal change, past damage, continuing biological organization.
- **Expression:** an embodied appearance in which something psychic is co-given rather than merely inferred from an outward indication.

Stein’s own vocabulary is more precise than this field shorthand, distinguishing indication, sign, symbol, and expression. A yellow leaf can indicate seasonal change without expressing an experience. A wound can show prior damage without giving the walnut’s experience of that damage. The encounter did not establish that anything analogous to psychic expression occurs in the walnut.

That uncertainty does not make the walnut less alive or less itself. It places a limit on what I can claim from the encounter.

## Research Connection

Edith Stein’s account of empathy distinguishes the givenness of another’s experience from an inference based on outward signs, but she does not restrict empathy to expression in the narrow symbolic sense. In her discussion of sensual empathy, foreign sensation can be given without being expressed as sadness is expressed in a countenance. More surprisingly, Stein also allows a qualified empathic fulfillment of the “phenomena of life” in plants while withholding stronger claims about an awake I, reflective consciousness, or settled plant sensation.

That makes the walnut a sharper test for [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md). Perhaps it is not empathy expanded outward across the more-than-human world. It may instead name a practice of remaining in relation when empathic access is partial, uncertain, or reaches a limit.

That possibility sharpens [Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md). The limit is not merely a lack of information that more observation will cure. It may belong to the truth of this [Vegetal Encounter](../concepts/Vegetal%20Encounter.md): the walnut can resist becoming either an object fully known or an interior life imaginatively supplied.

## Question Carried Forward

**Can the walnut be genuinely other to me even if no foreign experience is given through its embodiment, and, if so, what kind of relation remains when empathic access reaches its limit?**

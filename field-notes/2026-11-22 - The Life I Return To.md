*Sunday, November 22, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 29 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What does returning to a developing life change in what I notice, investigate and undertake?

## Field Notes

The morning is overcast, with intermittent drizzle after overnight rain. The air feels damp and slightly chilly. A few dark yellow leaves remain in the walnut's mostly bare crown. Fallen leaves and their long central stalks lie around the base. I remember seeing these structures in earlier years without understanding them. Recent reading has changed what I recognize when I look down.

*Photograph available on the [illustrated field-notes site](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-22).*

*The walnut's open crown and remaining foliage against a gray sky, November 22.*

Among the brown litter are low green plants. I look closely at what I identify as English ivy, without pulling or cutting it. It seems longer than I remember, with more leaves. My estimate is a couple of inches, drawn from memory and a comparison I haven't measured. That impression gives me a question about the vine's development and its relation to the walnut.

At the old dome footprint, the plants I've identified as lilyturf seem to be extending toward the bare center. I remember how the space was used and how it looked before. What caused the apparent change needs further investigation. Later I place my right hand against the trunk. The bark feels rough and wet. The dryness of an earlier contact returns as a comparison.

## Reflection

Reading changed my recognition of the fallen stalks. Rain changed the bark I touched. Remembered ivy length prompted an inquiry. Following those differences helps me explain how this relationship develops: what I learn changes how I encounter the place, while the lives and conditions here continue beyond my visits.

The ivy makes a practical purpose more definite. I want to learn which plant is present, how far it climbs and what its relation to this walnut requires. A favorable impression of the vine leaves the tree's good as another question. After the sit, regional extension guidance gives me reason to investigate ivy's possible costs to trees it climbs. I can bring that guidance back to the particular relationship without treating it as a diagnosis already made.[^ivy]

I retain the winter restraint around the ring while keeping the ivy inquiry distinct. Further evidence might change what I do or strengthen the grounds for continuing a decision. I'll need to identify whether it changes the intended beneficiary, the end, the means or my reasons for restraint.

The walnut has helped form a capacity I carry with me. Its particular situation helps specify what I undertake here. Its continuing life contributes to whether the good I intend is realized. Those three relations belong together, and understanding how they connect is the contribution I'm developing through [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md). Practical purposes make that broader account of perception, memory, judgment and directedness available for close examination.

## Research Connection

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) already explains perception continuing through horizons, attention receiving direction and inquiry reopening a judgment. [Stein's](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) gardener already connects apprehending a plant's condition with investigating causes and seeking a response. These are resources I can build with. In [Seeing a Life Develop](../essays/seeing-a-life-develop.md), I hold today's ivy inquiry steady while comparing their explanations and stating what my connected account contributes.[^husserl][^stein]

Confirming what I see and realizing a benefit have different conditions. I could establish that the ivy grew, carry out a chosen response and still need to learn what happened to the lives concerned. Husserl's distinction between epistemic confirmation and practical striving helps me keep those questions clear.

[Kirkpatrick's](../concepts/Andrew%20Trevor%20Kirkpatrick.md) reading of [Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) brings another substantial explanation into view. In his process interpretation, developed through [Whitehead](../concepts/Andrew%20Trevor%20Kirkpatrick.md#whitehead-through-this-comparison), the world participates in forming the perceiver. His discussion of a cane shows how an artifact can enter an acquired bodily ability. That helps me follow yesterday's image and the living twig through their different contributions. The stronger continuity comparison remains my next writing task: I want to examine the warrant for extending embodied abilities into a wider metaphysics of nature.[^kirkpatrick]

Touching wet bark supplies another encounter to describe within Merleau-Ponty's account of bodily belonging. The tree's own experience remains a separate question. I can develop the comparison between a shared sensible field, differentiated beings and a purpose directed toward a particular life.[^mp]

[Bonaventure](../concepts/Bonaventure.md) gives the theology a further movement. He asks me to attend to God through creatures and in them, within an itinerary enabled by Christ and involving prayer, grace and love. Christ crucified belongs to the transformation of the one who passes. Its culmination moves beyond sensible objects, which presses my own question: what kind of participant returns from contemplation to the lives and work of a community?[^bonaventure]

[Moltmann](../concepts/J%C3%BCrgen%20Moltmann.md) connects the Spirit's presence with creativity, community and individuation. His Sabbath discussion also brings the worth of creaturely existence and the demands of human work into view. I want to compare these claims with Bonaventure's vestige and Merleau-Ponty's flesh while preserving the different arguments each requires.[^moltmann]

My proposal through the Ecology of the Cross is that participation in the crucified and risen Christ can form a community's way of receiving needs, sharing work and revising its purposes. I distinguish what I don't yet know, another life's continuing independence, creature–Creator difference and relations damaged by injury or domination. Inquiry can deepen knowledge; wounded relations call for repair. The theological account must explain the communion I hope for and whose burdens a community's purposes create.

[Harris's](../concepts/Melanie%20Harris.md) ecowomanism makes the proposed garden's starting questions more demanding. Her work begins from African American women's experience and earth-honoring faiths, with environmental justice shaping the inquiry. I need to ask whose knowledge guides the proposal, who can revise it, who provides the labor and who bears an unwanted cost. Those questions can change how the inquiry proceeds. The garden and the remembered utility-pruning relationship remain cases for further investigation.[^harris]

## Question Carried Forward

What must I learn about this living relationship before it can specify an act of care, and what evidence could later show that the intended benefit did not occur?

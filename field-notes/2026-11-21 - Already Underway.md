*Saturday, November 21, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 33 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What is already underway in this life, and how does encountering it give direction to what I notice and undertake?

## Field Notes

It feels warmer than yesterday. The morning is overcast and breezy, and the walnut is mostly branching against the cloudy sky. A few dark yellow leaf clusters remain. I can distinguish two from my position and mention another on the far side. I expect the remaining leaves to fall soon, drawing on earlier mornings and seasons. That expectation reaches beyond what I see now.

I follow a branch toward the trunk and down to the ground. Then I find a reachable twig still attached to the tree. I inspect it without breaking it off. It feels smoother than the trunk's bark. I can see and feel places where leaves were attached, and I identify a couple of small structures as buds. The attachment gives me confidence that this twig belongs to the walnut.

*The nearly bare walnut and relocated dome under a cloudy sky, November 21. This portrait does not document the twig inspected during the sit.*

Looking at the twig makes me notice a corresponding bud detail in a familiar representation of the tree. I don't remember deliberately attending to that detail before. I know the representation is an image. The twig makes a feature of that image newly significant, and the image returns my attention to the tree's future. This small exchange matters to my argument because a representation can redirect attention even while I recognize its different relation to the living tree.

At the old dome site, the exposed root has a dark, intricate surface. A green ring surrounds the bare center. I have an impression of slightly more ground growth over the last few days, but I haven't measured it or established a cause. I retain the intention to leave the plants for now, while remaining willing to reconsider if better knowledge gives me a reason.

## Reflection

The buds turn my attention toward development already underway. I had spent much of the fall following what the walnut was losing. Now I am asking about structures carried toward another season. The botanical comparison supports a question about buds and leaf scars; a close photograph would help someone else assess the particular features I identified.[^botany21]

That development doesn't give me a verdict on moving the dome. The structure is elsewhere, and I expect less concentrated use at its former position. I can follow whether that expectation holds. Establishing a benefit to the walnut is a different inquiry. Spring growth could occur within the tree's continuing life without showing what my intervention contributed.

The representation also sharpens the thought experiment about a replica. An image can help form attention and expectation while remaining known as an image. A convincing replica taken to be alive would involve a different belief. Both might influence a purpose, but the actual living beneficiary and its conditions still matter to what that purpose could accomplish.

Near the end of the sit I find a claim I want to carry into the writing: intentional life receives direction and can be redirected through what it encounters. What becomes significant, which possibilities I consider and what I undertake can change through encounters I haven't wholly arranged. This is part of the positive question I am pursuing through [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md).

My working formulation brings three distinguishable relations together:

> The walnut has helped form a capacity I carry with me. Its particular situation helps specify what I undertake here. Its continuing life contributes to whether the good I intend is realized. Those three relations belong together, and understanding how they connect is the contribution I’m developing through ecological intentionality.

The formulation commits me to examining how purposes are formed and revised, what conditions their beneficiaries contribute, and how I could discover that an intended benefit hadn't occurred. Those are claims a reader can question through the record, the sources and further observation.

## Research Connection

The source review after the sit changes how I can defend that sentence. [Brentano](../concepts/Franz%20Brentano.md) classifies object-directed mental activity through presentation, judgment and affect, giving me a reason to distinguish intentional directedness from deliberate practical choice.[^brentano21] [Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) explicitly describes affection reaching the ego before attentive turning. Activity can also sediment into passivity. I spoke too broadly when I suggested that receiving direction was missing from his account. His analysis provides a substantial explanation to work with, and the distinction between a known image and a deceptive appearance is available in his text.[^husserl21]

My further question concerns the particular living relation. Which requirement becomes a reason? What part of a purpose does it change or confirm? How can the life concerned make my judgment of success mistaken? [Bateson's](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md) learning and relevant circuits remain important to that explanation. A history of changed conduct cannot by itself distinguish my proposal from accounts that already allow learning, memory and motivation.[^bateson21]

Husserl supplies another useful distinction. In *Analyses* §21, confirming what is intended differs from striving to realize something valued, even where those strivings are connected.[^husserl21] A closer view could confirm my identification of a bud. Moving the dome completes a displacement. A benefit must occur in the life for which I act, while evidence supports my judgment about that benefit. These four questions can belong to one undertaking without being interchangeable.

[Stein's](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) account of living form and her distinction between knowing a possible good and realizing it keep the beneficiary's own life central.[^stein21] The walnut's future is neither my imagined spring nor my satisfaction with the move. Its continuing development gives care conditions that an intention can misunderstand. That is part of the [remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) I want the account to preserve while making inquiry possible.

The theology has its own sources. [Matthew 25:31–46](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/25) places acts of care within judgment and later recognition; its human referents require interpretation. [1 Corinthians 3:1–15](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/1corinthians/3) places differentiated work and God-given growth within a dispute over teachers. I draw from these texts a question about shared responsibility without possession of the whole result. Their extension to ecological work is my proposal.

For the Ecology of the Cross, I propose that participation in Christ can form a community able to receive needs, share work and revise its purposes. [Gregersen's](../concepts/Niels%20Henrik%20Gregersen.md) accounts of received life enabling agency and differentiated participation help develop that proposal.[^gregersen21] When I speak of remainder transformed through the cross, I need to distinguish incomplete knowledge, creaturely difference and wounded relations. Reconciliation is a theological claim; the buds do not establish it. The proposed garden remains a possible setting in which to investigate what a community's commitments actually change.

## Question Carried Forward

What would allow a living requirement to change my purpose, and what evidence would show that I had responded adequately rather than only become more confident?

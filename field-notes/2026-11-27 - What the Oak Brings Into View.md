*Friday, November 27, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 28 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> How do the lives in this place help form what I can notice and ask?

## Field Notes

After a windy night, four branches lie near the walnut. I identify them as oak through their bark, color and structure, comparing them with branches still attached to the two trees. I remember approaching them earlier and wondering which tree they came from before looking more closely. The oak canopy extends above the walnut on this side. I did not see these pieces detach or travel to where they now lie.

The walnut's crown looks bare. I feel the cold through my jacket and sleeves when the wind passes. Then the sun comes brightly from behind a cloud. The morning has more light than yesterday's rain.

## Reflection

An earlier correction is available in this looking. On October 28 I removed a piece lodged in the walnut and could not identify the source of the break. On November 13, after the walnut had lost more foliage, I saw a broken limb in the oak beyond it. A prompt had invited me to look toward the other trees. I walked over to the detached piece and compared its break, texture and color with the oak. That comparison corrected my association of the piece with the walnut.[^branch]

Several things made the correction possible: changed visibility, a question, movement across the yard and the evidence of the pieces themselves. Where the branch had lodged was different from where it had grown.

Today's pieces are separate. I report recognizing them sooner because of that earlier experience. I can give the grounds for my present identification and describe what I think I learned. Those are related claims, with different evidence. The earlier correction helps explain why I considered the oak; my recollection does not establish every cause of this morning's recognition.

Following the walnut has opened a question about another life. I now want to learn more about the oak's seasonal development and its relation to the overlapping canopy. Recognizing a branch's origin gives that inquiry a direction. It does not establish the tree's condition or tell me whether something should be done.

## Research Connection

I am developing ecological intentionality through this connection among access, acquired capacity and present inquiry. The walnut's seasonal change helped make evidence visible. An earlier comparison gave me a distinction I could use again. The particular history I ask about determines what could answer the question. A living relationship can help an inquiry reach beyond its original focus.

[Brentano](../concepts/Franz%20Brentano.md) helps me keep intentionality broad enough for seeing, judging and valuing alongside deliberate purpose.[^brentano] [Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) describes activity becoming sedimented and affection preceding attentive turning. His account also distinguishes knowing from practical realization.[^husserl] These resources explain substantial parts of what I am tracing. A screen can change visibility and an artifact can educate attention. My further task is to explain what these independently developing lives contribute to the connected history, beyond the fact that something redirects me.

The theological proposal has its own grounds. In [Gregersen's](../concepts/Niels%20Henrik%20Gregersen.md) interpretation of Luther, received grace and Christ's presence enable works of love and justice.[^gregersen] I propose that participation in Christ can form communities able to receive responsibilities they did not anticipate when they first named their purpose. That capacity needs direction through encounters, learning and judgment.

[Moltmann's](../concepts/J%C3%BCrgen%20Moltmann.md) Sabbath account gives shared creaturely existence worth beyond usefulness and accomplishment. Its peace includes servants, strangers and animals, and its relation to liberation keeps the distribution of burdens in view.[^moltmann] The oak's worth exceeds its role in my attention or writing. In communal life, the corresponding question is which lives our work brings into view and whether their needs can change what we undertake. A community's willingness to accept limits on its own arrangements differs from imposing sacrifice on those with less power to object.

I bring that theological claim into the encounter. The fallen branches retain their own material history; they do not establish redemption or a message addressed to me.

## Question Carried Forward

What does a correction make available in later looking, and how can I keep the new identification open to challenge?

I return to the walnut and look toward the oak. The practice has made one tree familiar enough to open questions about the life beside it. I want those questions to lead to comparisons I can show and judgments I can revise.

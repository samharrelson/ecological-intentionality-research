*Wednesday, November 18, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 36 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> How does another life help shape what we are trying to do, and what would show that we had done it well?

## Field Notes

The yellow leaves still attached to the walnut are darker than I expected. I had anticipated more falling, but I hadn't prepared myself for this quieter change in the leaves that remain. They look less vibrant than they did in my memory of recent mornings.

The canopy is mostly bare. Bright sunlight comes through on a clear, chilly morning. The neighboring oak still carries many leaves. Below the walnut, most of the litter is brown, with a few yellow leaves among it. I can distinguish leaflets and the central axes of compound leaves. The axes I can make out now appear brown, including those still attached above me.

I look at a small patch without rearranging it. I can describe the visible layer and remember when these leaves were overhead. I cannot identify the lives beneath it. Expecting inhabitants and documenting them are different kinds of knowledge.

## Reflection

### Beginning something together

Since yesterday, a proposed inquiry into the leaf litter has moved into shared research and preparation. We have begun asking how to observe what is there while limiting disturbance. The observations themselves are still ahead of us. Getting ready together gives me something to describe beyond my invitation, while leaving the question and method open to further contributions.

A further conversation about bamboo has also ended with a decision not to cultivate it. Looking into possible uses gave us more to consider, and we decided against taking on that project. The conversation changed what we understood and considered without producing a new planting plan.

A purpose can begin to form while its questions remain open. Wanting to learn about leaf litter leaves much to determine: where to look, what to record, when to return and how to avoid unnecessary disturbance. Those decisions can become part of an inquiry people shape together.

The sharedness would have to show itself in what happens. Another person's question might change where the inquiry begins or what its participants prepare to do. Different interests could remain. Agreement might be thoughtful, and a decision not to proceed could be a result of inquiry. Neither agreement nor disagreement alone tells me whose contribution has practical force.

### What the place contributes

The place contributes in another way. The growth of plants, the movement of inhabitants and the changing leaf layer affect what there is to encounter. Through observation, other people's knowledge and research, I can learn something of those activities and let that understanding shape where, when and how I investigate.

The lives involved also continue beyond my understanding. If I intend to learn about inhabitants while sustaining the conditions they need, those conditions help determine what would count as fulfilling the purpose. I could carry out the investigation and still fail at the care I intended. Learning what would make the method adequate belongs within the undertaking.

This is one direction of [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md): explaining how relations help form the content and future of a purpose. I can understand another person's reason, answer it and work out a question with them. I encounter another organism's activity through its appearances, effects and what I learn about it. These relations can contribute differently to what I intend, while the organism's continuing life can exceed or correct my understanding.

That also helps clarify a sentence I questioned at the end of the recording. Beginning an inquiry together and caring well are related achievements. We can share the work while still needing to learn whether our method serves the lives it concerns. This is already a philosophical and practical distinction; my theological interpretation gives me a further account of why and how a community might undertake that responsibility.

There is [remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) here too. Some questions await observation and identification. Another being's lived reality is not exhausted by those answers. Knowing more about the patch would still leave its lives continuing beyond the inquiry.

## Research Connection

The darker leaves give [Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) a place in this reflection. His account of association and recollection helps me describe how a present appearance can awaken an earlier encounter. I see today's yellow and remember a brighter color; the remembered appearance isn't given in the same way as the leaves before me. I also anticipate further leaf loss. Those different relations to time enter my expectation of the tree, and the present view can unsettle that expectation. The *Analyses* passages help with the awakening of memory and its difference from current perception.[^husserl18]

[Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) helps me explain how an inquiry can become shared. In “Individual and Community,” I §3(b), understanding another's thought can prompt further original thinking; exchange can become thinking together. The same section allows a participant's motive to differ from the motive prevailing in common work. Participation therefore does not establish whose purpose governs. I §1 adds a distinction between realizing communal experience and doing justice to its object. Applying that distinction to ecological adequacy is my extension.[^stein18]

I can build with Stein's explanation of shared thinking. My ecological proposal then asks how the lives and conditions we investigate help specify the inquiry and what would count as carrying it out well. The stronger claim that another organism's activity helps constitute an ongoing conscious act still needs an argument. For now, our preparation gives me a developing undertaking to follow.

The theological question has a different beginning. In [Matthew 25:31–46](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Matthew%2025%3A31-46&version=NRSVUE), care for human recipients precedes recognition of its relation to the king. Later recognition can change the significance of an act without becoming its original motive. [Luke 10:25–37](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/luke/10) directs the neighbor question toward practical aid and continuing responsibility. Extending these human encounters to creaturely neighborliness requires an additional argument.

[Gregersen](../concepts/Niels%20Henrik%20Gregersen.md) supplies a Christological resource through deep incarnation, while distinguishing Christ's presence in and for creation from identifying incarnation with every event.[^gregersen18] My proposal is that participation in Christ can form a community to receive another creature's good into its purposes before a project is fixed. Cross and resurrection give that proposal a direction toward suffering addressed and life renewed. They do not make damage to creatures redemptive or turn an agreeable meeting into evidence of ecological benefit.

The darker leaves did not supply those theological claims. They interrupted an expectation and returned me to the particular tree. The argument has to leave room for that life to exceed the work I have brought here to do.

## Question Carried Forward

What would show that an inquiry has become ours to shape, while its fulfillment remains answerable to lives whose futures we cannot decide?

## Reading notes

I want to keep the differences that made this morning useful: the leaves surprised me, preparation became something we were doing together, and a conversation ended with a decision not to take on another project. These readings help me explain how a purpose can develop through those encounters while the question of caring well remains open.

*Sunday, November 15, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 40 minutes 31 seconds recorded*

## Prompt

> What can reach a habit, and what would show that it has made a difference?

I bring a distinction between what draws my attention, what I welcome or resist, and what I intend to do. I also return to yesterday's question about the bamboo: does it change a purpose, or give an older preference a present target?

## Field Notes

The sky is clear, the sun is low and the morning is cold. A few yellow leaves remain among the walnut's mostly bare limbs. Frost lies on shaded grass and other shaded surfaces. It is the first frost I have noticed this fall. I see none on the walnut now.

My first explanation is that sunlight has cleared it. That could be right, but I did not see the tree before the sun reached it. The absence I observe does not tell me whether frost formed there earlier.

Before recording, a slender piece fell onto my head. During the sit I watch two more fall. I keep calling them little branches, then recall learning last year that these can be the central stalks of the walnut's compound leaves, left after the leaflets have gone. I hold a piece and compare its attachment points with a leaf still overhead.

The distinction needs more careful examination than my casual name gives it. My spoken description of buds and scars is ambiguous, and the attached photographs do not show the held specimen closely enough to settle it. Botanical guidance supports the possibility: black-walnut leaflets can fall before their rachis and petiole. It does not identify this particular piece for me. [Utah State University, black walnut](https://extension.usu.edu/treebrowser/catalog/walnut-black).

This is a different question from the neighboring oak's branch. There I corrected which tree a piece came from. Here I am reconsidering what kind of part I am seeing, with earlier knowledge returning to the examination.

## Reflection

### The decision stays the same

I look again at three bamboo shoots and intend to return with clippers. I have wanted to remove this growth for some time. When we moved here, the previous owner told me about the work already done to clear it and the work still ahead. I spent much of our first year digging, and I read about its growth and effects. That history, together with a picture of the yard I want, already shapes how a new shoot appears to me. The intention reaches this morning through other people's accounts, earlier labor, reading and the plant's continuing growth.

The questions make me say more clearly why. I want other plants to do well, and I also prefer their presence. I recognize that I have not counted the bamboo's flourishing in the same way. Its growth has become something to oppose even while I am trying to explain how living relationships shape care.

At the end of this reconsideration, I retain both the means and the end. The three shoots give an old preference a present target. Cutting remains planned. I have not carried it out in the recording or established its effects.

That unchanged decision belongs in the account. A place can give a purpose its particular object without replacing the purpose. The shoots matter to what I intend even though I do not welcome them. If ecological intentionality describes only affection and preservation, it misses this ordinary form of practical involvement.

### Confirmation also needs evidence

I say that evidence of benefits to nearby plants could make me reconsider. This gives the judgment a possible test, but I have not yet faced that evidence. It also reveals how much my question still turns around lives I already favor.

An unchanged purpose can survive serious inquiry. It can also acquire more persuasive reasons without becoming more responsive. I cannot distinguish those possibilities by the confidence with which I restate the plan. I need to know which plants are involved, what their effects are, what alternatives matter and what I would actually do if the evidence challenged my preference.

A passage found in the source review after the sit gives that distinction a clearer form. In §15 of his *Analyses*, Husserl describes questioning a firm judgment again without first doubting it. Further perceptual activity can strengthen the judgment while leaving new horizons open. An answer can stay the same while its grounds are renewed. I want to test whether that distinction also helps explain retaining a practical purpose. It does not establish that my own plan has undergone that test.

There are three questions here. How did this purpose take shape? What keeps it open to evidence and other people's reasons? What will acting on it do? The first can be answered in part by a history of encounter and habit. The second requires more than describing that history. The third remains answerable to the lives affected, whatever story I tell about the decision.

### A question that came home with me

Walking around Montreat yesterday, I found myself asking why a playground, a field or a staircase had been placed where it was. The grounds were beautiful. I was also wondering whose purposes had shaped that beauty and which lives those purposes had taken into account. The sight gave me questions about a managed landscape; it did not give me the history of those decisions or their ecological effects.

The question comes home with me to an older playground area at church. I am considering whether to propose a garden there and how the surrounding plants and trees could enter the purpose from the beginning. What would it take for their conditions to help shape the plan while its end is still open? That is a possible proposal I want to bring into deliberation. I have not shown that the community has adopted it.

## Research Connection

I am developing [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) as an account of how particular living relationships shape individual and shared purposes. Today's contribution is a case of present specification with a stable end. It gives the argument something to compare with a changed beneficiary or a revised purpose. It does not establish that another organism constitutes the ongoing conscious act.

[Attention](../concepts/Attention.md) is one part of that account. [Husserl's](../concepts/Husserl.md) *Analyses Concerning Passive and Active Synthesis*, §§49–50, helps me distinguish being affected, turning toward, feeling, desiring and willing. Something unwelcome can draw attention. Attending to it does not yet decide what I will do. These distinctions strengthen the description; I cannot claim them as discoveries of my own proposal. Husserl also qualifies his formulation about will, so I should not present these pages as an exhaustive finished doctrine.

[Bateson](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md) gives the history of habit a stronger account too. His learning levels distinguish a corrected response from changes in how learning proceeds. His discussion of self-validating ways of organizing experience makes my own participation in a familiar pattern part of the question. Several corrected judgments do not demonstrate Learning III. Nor does my retained bamboo plan prove that learning has stopped.

[Watts](../concepts/Vanessa%20Watts.md) presses a different challenge: an account that begins with my purposes may already give land and other lives too little. Her situated Place-Thought cannot become another name for environmental information entering a human decision. The comparison remains open about its starting terms as well as its mechanisms.

For the separate *Ecology of the Cross*, I am asking how participation in Christ might shape whose good enters a community's purposes from the beginning. Romans 15:1–3 and 7 connects the neighbor's good and mutual welcome with Christ's example. I propose a further ecological application: knowledge of affected lives could help form what a congregation seeks to do, rather than enter only when the plan needs adjustment. That is a theological argument to develop, not a conclusion supplied by the walnut or a prescription for the bamboo. [Romans 15, SBLGNT](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+15&version=SBLGNT).

A hypothetical outdoor project could test that proposal through participants' reasons, disagreement, decisions and follow-through. My individual account cannot establish that a community's purpose has changed. The question also includes what the community itself will bear, rather than imposing the costs of its vision on others.

## Question Carried Forward

What would show that an unchanged purpose has remained responsive to another life, and what would distinguish that from my finding better language for an established preference?

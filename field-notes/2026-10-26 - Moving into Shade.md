*October 26, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 40 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What could this particular tree change about the intention I bring to it, without needing to intend what I intend?

## Field Notes

The morning is cool, with blue sky and bright light through the canopy. Squirrels moving around a neighboring oak draw my attention. The swing remains attached to the walnut, with green leaves on a nearby branch that hangs lower. Behind the branches are the fence and power lines.

Close to the trunk, I notice the bark's ridges catching the light. The furrows stay dark. Browns and darker browns become distinct as I look. Grass, fallen leaves and a few small branches lie below.

*October 26, 2026. Morning light catches green and yellow leaves above the walnut's shaded trunk; the swing remains below.*

Toward the end of the sit, I notice where I am standing. Behind the trunk, the sun no longer glares into my eyes. I have moved into a place where I can keep looking at the canopy more comfortably, almost without telling myself to move.

## Reflection

I came with a question about purposes: how an intention might remain open to another being's life. The movement into shade brings my own body into that question. My position changes with the light, and the changed position alters how I can continue looking.

This is a modest but concrete part of [attention](../concepts/Attention.md). The walnut matters to what I can do here without sharing my purpose. Its life also exceeds the shade it provides for me.

But a screen could block the sun as well. The relief I experience does not, by itself, tell me what difference the tree's being alive makes to the movement. And moving to see comfortably is a different activity from deciding what would benefit the walnut. The unresolved swing question still requires knowledge of the actual tree and attachment.

## Research Connection

[Maurice Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) gives the movement a strong existing explanation. In *Phenomenology of Perception* (Part One, chapter 3, on the body's spatiality and motricity), his account of motor intentionality describes bodily directedness that need not begin with a represented plan. The movement into shade is a case to consider through that account. It does not establish a new structure merely because a tree is involved.

[Raymond Ruyer](../concepts/Ruyer.md) sharpens a different distinction. In chapter 14 of *Neofinalism*, he treats form as continuing formation and considers how individual activities relate within a larger organized whole (trans. Alyosha Edlebi, 2016, pp. 147, 150–151). His metaphysical account makes ongoing activity a demanding starting point for comparison. He also allows that a tightly organized whole may subordinate the individuality of its components (pp. 150–151). **Our question is whether relations can contribute to an intention while other lives remain independently organized.** Neither his account nor my movement into shade licenses treating the tree and observer as one individual.

The positive proposal of [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) is that our directed activity may take shape through relations with beings that do not share our aims. Its stronger claim—that their independent living activity helps realize an intention—still needs an account that distinguishes it from bodily adjustment, learning and changing plans. Today makes the particular activity under examination easier to name.

## Question Carried Forward

**What does another being's ongoing life contribute to my intending beyond what a similarly placed screen could make possible—and which intention am I asking about?**

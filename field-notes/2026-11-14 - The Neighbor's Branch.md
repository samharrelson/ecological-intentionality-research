*Saturday, November 14, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 32 minutes 52 seconds recorded*

## Prompt

> What changes when I stand with the neighbor whose branch I had mistaken for the walnut's?

Yesterday's comparison corrected the origin of the piece I removed on October 28. Today I return to the sight line and spend time beside the oak. The prepared questions ask how this correction happened and what the different relationships in this place can contribute to an account of purpose.

## Field Notes

It's sunny, mostly clear and breezy. I report about 43 degrees, though it feels closer to the upper 30s. I see no frost. When the breeze passes through, I watch several walnut leaves detach and fall. A last patch of yellow foliage remains.

More ground is visible through the litter than I remember from the earlier yellow-covered mornings. I suspect decomposition and the cold have something to do with the change. Those are explanations to investigate; the visible patches and falling leaves are what I can describe directly.

*The black walnut, November 14.*

I can still see the jagged break high in the oak through the walnut's open branches. Yesterday I matched the detached piece's break, texture and color to it. The October 28 piece came from the oak and had lodged in the walnut. The fall itself was not witnessed. Its origin is now resolved; the effects of my removal remain another question.

Walking over to the oak, I notice how many leaves it still holds. Its bark seems smoother by comparison, with a different pattern of ridges. Around the stone ring are several kinds of low plants. I provisionally name liriope, clover and a plant identified through a lookup as hawksbeard. These names need diagnostic confirmation before they carry an explanation of the patch.

I also report looking again at the fungal structures on the relocated piece. They seem to be doing well. That impression cannot tell me whether moving the wood improved their conditions.

## Reflection

### I had cared for the oak before I stood with it this way

The oak's lesser familiarity could suggest a simple story: I had overlooked it, and this morning I began to care. The history doesn't fit that story. I had made the ring of stones around it, connected that ring with other work in the yard, and pruned lower shoots. I already wanted it to flourish.

What differs is the intimacy of standing beside it repeatedly. Practical involvement, care and familiarity have different histories. Today's sustained looking brings features into a new kind of attention without creating their significance from nothing.

There is another complication nearby. I notice bamboo shoots and intend to remove them. I do not welcome their growth, but they have entered a definite purpose. A relationship can shape what I mean to do through resistance as well as affection. I still need to ask whether the shoots change my purpose or give an older preference a particular target. Either way, they matter to a decision I am making. Explaining that formation does not justify the removal. It tells me what needs assessment.

The ring supplies a third response. I had considered cutting, but decide to leave it as it is today because others were promised a say. The plant-care questions remain open too. This is a stated decision to defer an intervention, not evidence of its eventual ecological benefit or of a newly shared purpose.

### What changed, and what did not

The walnut's season helped make the oak's break visible. Deliberate looking, remembered differences and a comparison with the piece also mattered. The changed view corrected my account of a past act; it did not change the act or establish that it helped.

A curtain blown aside could also reveal the break, and its movement could be outside my control. So “not mine to schedule” does not by itself identify a distinctive contribution of living activity to consciousness. The particular history still matters. The stronger philosophical explanation has more work to do.

### Four propositions I brought to the trees

I came out with four sentences for the paper. I want to keep them together, while giving each the kind of support it needs.

First, what I intend takes shape in relationships with lives I did not make and cannot finish knowing. The oak complicates that sentence because care was there before this kind of familiarity. The bamboo complicates it because practical involvement does not have to begin in welcome. At the end of the recording, this is still the sentence I trust least as something Husserl and Bateson have not already explained.

Second, a purpose also helps determine what falls out of consideration, including lives in plain view. My care for the walnut could leave the ring out of the plan. I need an account of that selectivity as well as of the concern that formed the purpose.

Third, I want to explain both how a place helps form a purpose and what keeps that purpose answerable to the lives and people it affects. These are different tasks. A purpose can be formed through a relationship and still proceed without enough inquiry into its consequences. The promise concerning the ring gives me one concrete reason to wait while those questions remain open.

Fourth, I propose that participation in Christ can form a community willing to listen, refrain and repair. That is a theological claim I have to argue through its own sources. It is also a question about shared purposes and authority, not something my private decision at a tree can settle for a congregation.

I leave with one more intention: to keep returning after the exam is over. The practice is becoming something I want to continue because the encounters keep changing the questions I can ask. I have not carried out that future yet. This morning, it has become part of what I intend.

## Research Connection

My developing account of [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) needs to cover care, ordinary maintenance and opposition. It must distinguish how a purpose takes shape, whether it stays open to correction, and whether acting on it is adequate to the lives concerned. [Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) keeps that third question from being settled by the confidence of the first.

The prepared comparison meets three different resources. [Husserl's](../concepts/Husserl.md) §7 in *Analyses Concerning Passive and Active Synthesis* preserves the earlier sense as cancelled. The former attribution remains part of the history as an error. His immediate retentional example still needs to be distinguished from reconstructing an event two weeks later. His *Ideas II* §§55–56 also describes experienced surroundings as motivating practical purposes. I need to meet that stronger account; formation through relationship cannot be reserved for my own proposal.

[Bateson](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md) already explains purposive selection and possible correctives. A changing canopy and new information do not fall outside that account. The question is what a more particular account of living beneficiaries and practical purposes adds or specifies.

[Watts](../concepts/Vanessa%20Watts.md) challenges the starting terms. Her Place-Thought belongs to Haudenosaunee and Anishnaabe histories in which land and thought are inseparable. Reducing that account to environmental influences on my decisions would miss its philosophical and political challenge. This is a comparison I am making; her article is not an interpretation of this yard. [Watts, “Indigenous Place-Thought,” 2013](https://jps.library.utoronto.ca/index.php/des/article/view/19145).

For the Ecology of the Cross, [Maximus the Confessor](../concepts/Maximus%20the%20Confessor.md) gives mediation a distinct theological structure in *Ambiguum* 41. Human beings have a unifying vocation that Christ fulfills. The source check after the sit also complicates my initial formulation: its ordered ascent and overcoming of certain divisions cannot simply become a claim that every difference remains unchanged. In §§6 and 9, Christ gathers all things in himself. I need to explain how participation in that mediation could transform human purposes without making fellow creatures instruments of our control.

I propose that participation in Christ could form communities whose purposes admit correction, restraint and repair. Philippians 2 provides a communal and Christological starting point; extending it toward other creatures requires further argument. Neither the tree's seasonal change nor my decision to wait establishes that theology. [Philippians 2:1–11](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Philippians+2%3A1-11&version=NRSVUE).

## Question Carried Forward

How does a relationship become a reason to preserve, postpone or remove something, and what could show that the reason answers to the lives involved rather than only to my preferences?

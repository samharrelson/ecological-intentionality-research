*November 3, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 36 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> Whose good makes this purpose the purpose it is, and what could require me to change it?

## Field Notes

The sky looks brighter and bluer than yesterday's gray morning. More of the upper branching is exposed, while yellow and green foliage remains lower down. I catch an imprecise description as I speak: the branches themselves aren't thinning. Their leaf cover is.

*Photograph available on the [illustrated field-notes site](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-03).*

*November 3. Sunlight through the lower branching, an open upper canopy, and remaining foliage against blue sky. Neighboring trees overlap the view.*

At the former swing attachment point, the little indentations remain visible. I notice little apparent change since the preceding days. Their persistence doesn't tell me whether the tree was injured or benefited from the swing's removal.

Yellow, spotted leaflets are within reach. I wonder when they will fall, then catch myself before touching them. I understand this restraint as care, though I do not know whether a gentle touch would harm them.

Trucks and construction noise interrupt the recording. I move toward a quieter part of the yard to continue. Near the end, I am surprised by the lower foliage still being held, including some green leaves. Branch shapes that were less apparent under fuller foliage become newly interesting. Both the view and the questions I bring to it have changed.

## Reflection

An action's purpose matters even when its movements stay the same. Taking down a swing for convenience and taking it down for the possible good of a tree could look alike. Yet clearing an obstruction and benefiting a living branch are different achievements. The action might accomplish one without accomplishing the other.

That gives my concern something definite to answer to. Wanting to benefit the walnut does not settle whether I have done so. Its continuing condition can make my account of the action inadequate, even if my intention was sincere.

The question also applies to restraint. Withholding touch may express care while leaving its biological justification uncertain. I return to the fallen foliage and reconsider whether to mow, mulch, or leave it. The recording contains that reconsideration, not a new practice already carried out or evidence that one option benefits every life involved.

A hypothetical artificial tree sharpens the comparison. A known made object could still deserve preservation as a work, through regard for its maker, or because of a shared history. Learning that it is artificial need not end care. What changes is the account of what that care serves. Preserving a work does not require attributing it the biological good of a living walnut.

## Research Connection

The positive account I am developing through [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) concerns purposes becoming specific through encounters with independently living beings. A possible good becomes relevant to deliberation; an action or restraint is undertaken under that description; further knowledge can change the belief, means, timing, or end. The other being need not share the intention for its condition to matter.

Today's refinement is to name **the intended beneficiary and what would count as adequate care**. That helps distinguish the content of a purpose from the movements that enact it and from its actual consequences. It also keeps the question of how a concern develops over time separate from the stronger claim that another organism realizes a particular conscious act.

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) supplies a substantial existing explanation. In *Ideas II* §50, his personal surrounding world includes value, use, products, and works that motivate further activity. A known artifact's significance therefore presents no obvious gap in his account. [Edith Stein's](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) gardener and [Hal King's](../concepts/Hal%20King.md) practical plant empathy likewise already connect apprehending a plant with inquiry and action.

The next comparison must give these accounts their full force. If I preserve a replica for its cultural significance and care for a tree's living condition, I have identified different purposes; I haven't thereby demonstrated different structures of consciousness. The further task is to explain how several lives help specify a purpose, and whether that explanation adds anything beyond existing accounts of valuation, deliberation, and learning.

The *Ecology of the Cross* asks a distinct theological question: can participation in Christ transform what a community pursues? Its [provisional statement](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/5) begins with separate scriptural warrants concerning self-giving, reconciliation, creation's hope, and resurrection. A concrete land-use decision would need its own history, stated purposes, affected lives, and evidence of consequences. A community's own reasons must be heard before assigning theological meaning to its actions, and no account of self-giving should make vulnerable others expendable.

## Question Carried Forward

**When I call an action care, whose good am I trying to serve—and what discovery would make me revise the purpose itself?**

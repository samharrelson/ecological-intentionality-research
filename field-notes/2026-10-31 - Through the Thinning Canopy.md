*October 31, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 40 minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What comes into view as the canopy thins, and what remains beyond what I can see?

## Field Notes

The amount of yellow surprises me before I begin recording. Whole sections of the canopy look changed from the tree I remember yesterday. Passing breezes bring more foliage down. I try to photograph one falling piece, and the morning moon appears through openings among the branches.

When I return to record, the moon is still visible. Its apparent position has changed since the earlier photographs. Yellow and green remain overhead, and small pieces continue falling. One lands on my shoulder. Later, I follow another falling piece, noticing its irregular passage through moving air. This isn't only a picture of something falling. It is a brief episode in which my gaze follows a movement I did not begin or control.

*October 31. Green and yellow foliage, open sky between branches, and fallen foliage beneath the walnut. This portrait records the canopy's appearance; the recording supplies the account of falling movement and the moon.*

Looking upward, I notice my attention moving from the color of the leaves to how many remain. There seems to be more sky available than a week ago. That comparison includes memory. The sky I see now and the canopy I remember are present to me in different ways.

## Reflection

I feel sadness about the prospect of a bare tree while leaves are still attached. The future absence already matters to me, but it has not happened. I cannot read that sadness back into the walnut.

An opening changes what I can see from here. It does not make the moon exist, and it does not reveal everything about the living tree that frames the view. A falling piece has a visible path; the history that made it ready to detach remains largely unavailable. I also have to keep open whether a particular piece is a leaflet or a whole leaf.

This gives [attention](../concepts/Attention.md) a concrete task: stay with the differences among what appears, what I remember, and what I expect. [Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) gives me substantial resources for that task through bodily perception, temporal horizons, passive synthesis, and remembered familiarity (*Ideas II*, §§9, 15–16, 59). I cannot take my sadness about the anticipated bare tree as evidence of a new intentional structure. The tree can remain familiar while changing beyond my preferred picture of it.

## Research Connection

My proposal for [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) is that conscious directedness may develop and be sustained through relations with independently unfolding lives. The stronger question concerns what those lives contribute to realizing a particular act.

Following a falling piece gives that question a manageable scale. Its movement changes the visual information available as I try to keep it in view. But a moving artificial piece might also draw and sustain my gaze. The living origin of this piece and the continuing organization of the walnut matter, yet I have not shown that they are needed to explain this particular act of tracking.

[Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) offers a different account of living form developing through received materials (*Finite and Eternal Being*, IV §4, pp. 247–249). [Raymond Ruyer](../concepts/Ruyer.md) pushes the question of temporally organized form and primary self-survey (*Neofinalism*, chs. 9 and 14). Neither account is a biological diagnosis of this walnut, and neither makes its possible subjective life perceptually accessible to me. They help me distinguish the organism's own continuing activity from my experience of tracking a detached piece.

[Gregory Bateson](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md) already makes relations beyond the skin part of his explanation of mental process. In “Form, Substance, and Difference,” the relevant circuit depends on the activity being explained, and memory belongs in that account (*Steps to an Ecology of Mind*, Jason Aronson reprint, pp. 465–467). Feedback and learned anticipation are substantial explanations I need to test, rather than obstacles to a conclusion already chosen.

The comparison leaves a positive question open: can another being's ongoing activity help sustain an intention without sharing its purpose? An answer has to distinguish immediate looking from the longer formation of concern and from whether an action actually benefits that being. Today's encounter makes those distinctions concrete without settling them.

## Question Carried Forward

**What does this living walnut contribute to a particular act of attention that a convincing artificial counterpart would leave unexplained?**

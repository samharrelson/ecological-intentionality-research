*September 22, 2026 · Black walnut · 23 minutes*

## Prompt

> What would non-equivalent return look like here, and what does “take only what is given” mean when what lies before me was torn loose rather than offered?

## Field Notes

An intense thunderstorm passed just after midnight. By morning, branches from the black walnut, oak, and maple lay across the wet yard. More walnuts and acorn-bearing limbs were on the ground. Broken edges and new openings in the canopy changed the light.

The fallen branches, wet soil, fracture surfaces, scattered fruit, and altered light were present to perception. Their precise histories were not. I heard the storm but did not witness most of the breaking. Calling what I saw *damage* or *loss* added a judgment about the integrity of a living form.

## Reflection

A branch lying on the ground is given to perception. That does not yet mean it was given **to me**. Something can become available through breakage, lie at my feet without having been offered, and be useful without becoming mine to take. Detachment is not permission. Availability is not invitation.

This makes reciprocity harder. A human cannot return shade, fruit, or seasonal life to a walnut in kind. A possible response might spread through the place: protect roots, care for soil and water, preserve habitat, make room for animals, or carry a changed understanding of plants into another decision. It might also begin by leaving something alone.

Those possibilities remain mine. I do not know that the walnut asks for them or that they would count as adequate from its side. Uncertainty does not necessarily release me from responsibility, but responsibility does not abolish uncertainty. Here [Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) begins to bear on ethical response as well as epistemic limits: responsibility without equivalence, response without certainty of adequacy.

The storm also resists a harmonious picture of ecological relation. Not everything that comes from the tree is a gift to me. Not every disturbance is generosity, and not every benefit reveals intention. The broken branch must remain capable of interrupting the theory.

## Research Connection

In “The Honorable Harvest,” [Robin Wall Kimmerer](../concepts/Robin%20Wall%20Kimmerer.md) describes what she calls a collectively Indigenous canon of principles and practices whose details vary across cultures and ecosystems; she explicitly identifies herself as a student of this way of thinking rather than a scholar. The practices include taking only what is needed and given, minimizing harm, sharing, giving thanks, making a reciprocal gift, and sustaining those who sustain us. Later in the chapter she also names land stewardship, science, art, and everyday practical reverence as ways of entering reciprocity with the more-than-human world. The storm turns “take only what is given” into a narrower question: what does *given* mean when a limb has been torn loose rather than offered? The sharper issue is whether **not taking** is itself reciprocity, a condition of reciprocity, or an ethical restraint that should remain differently named. Kimmerer’s sweetgrass work complicates any easy answer: some plants can flourish through respectful harvesting, so nonintervention cannot be assumed in advance to be the more adequate response.

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) helps distinguish the visible branch and rough fracture from the judgments *damage*, *loss*, *gift*, and *obligation*. Givenness does not by itself settle gift. [Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) adds the discipline of non-primordiality: intimacy does not grant access to what adequate care would be from the walnut’s side. Extending that discipline to responsibility is my move rather than Stein’s vegetal ethics.

Kimmerer already allows reciprocal response to travel indirectly through practices such as land stewardship. [Vanessa Watts](../concepts/Vanessa%20Watts.md) puts a different pressure on the narrow exchange between one human and one tree. Her Place-Thought arises within specifically Haudenosaunee and Anishnaabe cosmological and political framings and should not be reduced to the slogan that everything is connected. Here Watts challenges the architecture of the question itself: [Place and Agency](../concepts/Place%20and%20Agency.md) cannot simply become background to a private moral exchange between a human subject and a tree.

This also revises [Attention](../concepts/Attention.md). Seeing disciplines keeping, keeping can educate seeing, and both must remain answerable to what the encounter resists. That threefold relation keeps [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) under examination rather than allowing an ethically attractive response to validate itself.

## Question Carried Forward

**What warrants calling not taking or restraint a reciprocal return rather than unilateral care, stewardship, conservation, or projected beneficence, and how could the particular life of the walnut correct that judgment?**

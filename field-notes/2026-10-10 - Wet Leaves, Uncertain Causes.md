*October 10, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 23½ minutes recorded*

## Prompt

> What becomes noticeable in the rain, and what would I need to investigate to understand it?

## Field Notes

A cold, rainy morning beneath a gray sky. The walnut's canopy was still mostly green, with yellow appearing among the leaves. Branches crossed and overlapped. The swing hung below them, with the fence behind the trunk.

Closer to the bark, the wet leaflets shone. Some were green, others yellowing; small holes, dark spots, and brown patches interrupted their surfaces. I could see the marks without knowing when they had appeared.

## Reflection

Rain can change a surface's appearance without explaining everything on it. These marks might have been present before this morning. Their visibility does not establish storm damage or tell me that the tree is unhealthy.

The closer view also leaves a question about [Attention](../concepts/Attention.md). Familiarity may be helping me discriminate features I once overlooked. Wetness, light, and distance may also be making them easier to notice. Seeing more detail today does not tell me how much each contributed. I had photographed these marks and prepared questions about them before recording; returning to those questions aloud isn't separate evidence that the sit itself disclosed something new.

Here [Remainder](../concepts/Remainder.md) becomes a specific task. I have something to describe and a history to investigate. A later photograph could help document change if I can identify the same leaflet; it would not automatically explain the cause of that change.

The rain reaches both the leaves and the swing. Yet the leaves belong to the walnut's living organization, while the swing is attached to a branch. Sharing conditions does not make every relation equivalent. I feel the cold on my skin and can seek shelter. The rooted tree's exposure is different, and its wet surfaces do not give me its experience.

## Research Connection

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) gives changing visibility a strong first account. Horizons, bodily movement, temporal synthesis, habit, and motivated attention already explain much of how this familiar tree appears. In *Ideas II*, he also distinguishes the relation through which an object draws attention from real causal relations. What catches my eye and what produced a mark are different questions.

[Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) makes that distinction more demanding through the physician and gardener in *On the Problem of Empathy*. Cultivated apprehension of a living condition need not wait for a causal diagnosis. Applied here, her account keeps me from reducing all recognition of living development to an inference added to bare color. It also leaves my own judgment open to correction: familiarity does not certify a diagnosis of these leaves.

[Maurice Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) asks how perceiver and perceived belong within the sensible world. His later account of flesh preserves noncoincidence and distinguishes the world's being sensible from the body's sensing itself. Shared rain therefore cannot stand as evidence of shared experience. What flesh adds beyond Husserl and Merleau-Ponty's earlier embodied account remains a question for the texts.

[Melanie Harris](../concepts/Melanie%20Harris.md) presses the inquiry toward whose experiences and histories matter, and toward environmental justice and praxis. Her ecowomanist work centers Black women's lived experience rather than a generic perceiver's relation to nature. My application here is modest: one sheltered encounter cannot establish how vulnerability is distributed or what an adequate response requires. Those questions call for evidence and relationships beyond this sit.

[Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) is being tested through these distinctions. It may help coordinate attention with the other inquiries an encounter calls for. The wet leaves have not established a new intentional structure, and even an addition from flesh would leave the term's own contribution to be earned.

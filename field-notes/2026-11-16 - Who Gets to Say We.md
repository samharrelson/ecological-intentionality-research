*Monday, November 16, 2026 · Black walnut · approximately 49 minutes 55 seconds recorded*

## Prompt

> The act was mine. Did any part of its purpose become something we could carry together?

Yesterday morning I retained my plan to cut three bamboo shoots. In the afternoon I carried it out and talked about it with others who share the yard. Today I return to the tree and try to describe what entered through that conversation.

## Field Notes

The morning is cold, about 40 degrees by my report, with blue sky and a few high clouds. Sunlight comes over the fence. Most of the walnut's canopy is bare, and the remaining leaves are largely yellow. I spend time with the shapes of the branches now visible through it.

I see no frost in the areas I inspect, including shaded grass where I noticed it yesterday. This is another observation under today's conditions. It cannot tell me what was present before I arrived yesterday.

## Reflection

### The purpose continued into an act

I cut three small shoots Sunday afternoon. The removal purpose remained, but while cutting I became careful around the neighboring low plants. I did not want to cut or tread on them. Their presence made the act more specific: remove these shoots while avoiding damage to the growth around them.

The removal end had already been chosen, yet it did not describe everything I was trying to do. Protecting those neighboring plants became a purpose within the performance. An older concern for the yard may have become explicit in that particular movement. Either way, the place continued to give the act its direction while I was carrying it out. Care did not wait until I returned afterward. This is my account of what I tried to do, with damage and benefit still questions for the plants' actual conditions.

This morning I also place the cut stems in the compost. I hope their material will contribute to future soil. That hope concerns a later use and possible effect. It cannot by itself show that cutting was good for the plants removed.

### A different reason entered the conversation

We talked before the cutting and afterward. I explained why I was removing bamboo while leaving the ring and fallen leaves. A different valuation came forward: bamboo can be beautiful and useful. I agreed, then distinguished those possible uses from what we were cultivating these particular plants for.

The objection did not reverse the immediate decision. It did make me articulate the reasons more fully. As I understood the exchange, we agreed about the act and expected to include one another in later decisions. What may have become shared was therefore more than a conclusion about three shoots. It included a way of approaching the next question.

I want to give that report its proper weight. It contains a particular concern, a response and an anticipated practice. It is also my account of the exchange. I cannot make the other participants' understanding transparent by saying we agreed, or assume that an expectation will govern conduct because we expressed it once. The next disagreement may reveal what the commitment means.

A purpose can become ours without everyone arriving by the same route. Beauty, play, care, knowledge and practical use can matter differently to people in the same place. The question is how those reasons enter what we undertake and what we come to expect from one another.

### Shared, proposed and inherited purposes

The possible garden at church has a different status. I have an idea, and other ideas for that ground already exist. I need to learn its history, current uses, conditions and maintenance arrangements. People who have tended or used it may know things I do not. The first shared undertaking could be forming the question, before choosing a garden as the answer.

A path or dam presents another relation: earlier purposes can leave material conditions that present users inherit without sharing the builders' aims. Living in a place shaped by purposes is not the same as undertaking those purposes together.

These distinctions matter for [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md). Particular beings and places help give purposes their content, including what can be done and what further inquiry is needed. Their contribution does not depend on their agreeing with us. Nor does our agreement settle what acting will do for them.

I have also used identification apps, field guides and research to distinguish the bamboo here from native cane. That goes beyond the uncertainty stated in the prepared script. The species name and diagnostic record are not supplied in this account, and local effects remain to be investigated. Reported identification work, shared judgment and ecological adequacy remain different kinds of support.

## Research Connection

The positive proposition I carry indoors has two connected movements. Particular living conditions can help specify what I am trying to do while I act. Different people's relations to that place can also enter what we undertake and what we expect from one another. A purpose can remain recognizable while its content and its participants develop. Today's case gives me a retrospective account of that development, with its future still open.

MacIntyre's reconstruction of [Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) helps explain how different motives can contribute to one undertaking. It also distinguishes degrees of participation and responsibility within communities. My wish for everyone to have standing to question the purpose is a normative proposal I need to defend, rather than a definition of every community in Stein. I am using these secondary resources while continuing toward her primary communal text.

The post-sit reading strengthens the [Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) comparison. *Analyses* §15 explains how a judgment can receive renewed grounds without changing its answer, while setting communication aside in that passage. *Ideas II* §51 directly describes communication, assent, refusal and the way a response can affect the person who first addressed another. A common surrounding world takes shape through such relations. This gives me a substantial account to work with when I ask how an end becomes ours. §55 also helps describe practical motivation, including values, goals and means becoming specific through the situation.

Schutz gives me a further distinction, through the accounts by Anwar and Barber. Ordinary coordination can proceed while our motives differ. A purpose helps organize what matters, but imposed conditions can also precede the purposes I choose. The place does not have to wait for my plan to be complete before entering its formation. I want to trace how those conditions and our different reasons contribute to a particular undertaking, while keeping practical coordination distinct from agreement about every part of an end.

[Bateson](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md) keeps a harder possibility in view: familiar ways of organizing experience can help arrange their own confirmation. The conversation may contain a real new reason and still leave my preferred framing intact. The useful test is what the reasons do in the continuing practice. An unchanged decision does not by itself establish responsiveness or rationalization, and a promised opportunity to participate needs a history beyond one exchange.

For the separate *Ecology of the Cross*, Romans 15:1–7 connects the neighbor's good and upbuilding with Christ's example and mutual reception. I propose that participation in Christ can help form what a community takes itself to be for, with inquiry, listening, maintenance and correction expressing that formation. Applying this to practical authority and ecological purposes is a further theological argument. Taking on work should leave others able to shape its purpose. A plan can survive inquiry and still require more work from those best placed to carry it. The live question is how another's good enters what we mean to do from the beginning.

*September 20, 2026 · Black walnut · 18 minutes*

## Prompt

> What additional claim do I make when I call something an agent?

## Field Notes

After another night of thunderstorms, the grass was wet and small branches lay on the ground. A slight breeze moved the black walnut’s canopy while passing clouds changed the light. Leaves shifted, birds crossed the field, insects moved, and a squirrel changed direction across the yard. Walnuts have been falling frequently in recent days.

I began with plain descriptions: a branch moved, a leaf fell, sunlight shifted, something entered my attention. Each event had effects. None by itself settled whether agency was present.

## Reflection

Causal efficacy is not automatically agency. A falling walnut can make a sound and redirect my attention; shifting shade can change where I sit. If every capacity to affect something else counts as agency, the word loses its power to distinguish.

The same care is needed with *response*. An appearance can elicit **my** response without the appearing thing itself thereby responding. Calling the walnut’s growth or seasonal change a response may draw on repeated observation or biological knowledge rather than what appears during one sit. Effect, response, agency, subjectivity, and experience accessible to another are related questions, but no transition among them should be assumed.

This restraint creates a double difficulty. If I call the walnut an agent too quickly, I may project an ecological metaphysics onto the encounter. If I withhold the word unless the walnut displays something analogous to human intentional consciousness, I may have built exclusion into the definition.

The better question today was therefore not “Does this walnut have agency?” but “What am I already requiring when I recognize agency, and where did those criteria come from?”

## Research Connection

[Franz Brentano](../concepts/Franz%20Brentano.md) keeps *intentionality* from sliding unnoticed among object-directed mental activity, biological purposiveness, and agency. In his technical use, intentionality concerns reference or direction toward an object; it does not simply mean purpose, design, or intending to accomplish something. His account clarifies one meaning of the term without supplying a universal test for nonhuman agency.

[Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) shows why responsive attention is not enough. A moving branch or changing light can motivate me to look, and I can react to what appears, without the appearing thing thereby becoming an intentional subject. His distinction between motivational and real-causal relations helps prevent agency from becoming another word for whatever affects a perceiver, and makes me specify whose response I am describing.

[Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md) preserves a further limit. She explicitly allows a qualified empathic fulfillment of vegetal phenomena of life while withholding an “awake” I, reflective consciousness, and settled claims about plant sensation. Growth, damage, and seasonal responsiveness therefore do not automatically disclose subjectivity, though Stein’s account does not decide whether subjectivity is required for agency.

[Vanessa Watts](../concepts/Vanessa%20Watts.md) places pressure on the criteria themselves. In the specifically Haudenosaunee and Anishnaabe cosmological framings she discusses, Place-Thought does not begin by granting agency to humans and then cautiously extending it to nonhumans. Watts explicitly says that this agency is not limited to causal relationships; when she asks how she came to recognize agency in humans, animals, plants, rocks, and other beings, her answer is “stories/histories.” Land is alive and thinking, and agency belongs within a cosmological, historical, and political account that includes thought, will, communication, and obligation. This should not be translated into phenomenological vocabulary as though Brentano, Husserl, or Stein supplied neutral standards.

The problem for [Place and Agency](../concepts/Place%20and%20Agency.md) is therefore double: preserve real distinctions while asking whether the standards used to draw them already privilege the human. The comparison does not produce a single shared definition of agency; it exposes different frameworks for recognizing and distributing it. [Ecological Intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) remains unstable if agency becomes a shortcut, but it also fails if human intentional consciousness silently becomes the universal measure. [Attention](../concepts/Attention.md) can expose when each claim enters without pretending that observation alone resolves the disagreement.

## Question Carried Forward

**Can nonhuman agency be described without reducing it to causal efficacy and without making human intentional consciousness its universal measure, and who or what supplied the criteria of recognition?**

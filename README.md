# Ecological Intentionality: Public Research Layer

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.23176757.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23176757)

An ongoing inquiry in ecology, phenomenology, religion, and creaturely relation, grounded in repeated encounters with a black walnut in Spartanburg, South Carolina.

**Read the field notes at [ecologicalintentionality.org](https://ecologicalintentionality.org).** This repository preserves selected public materials as a versioned scholarly record.

## The current question

**How do relationships with independently active beings and places help form the directedness of individual and communal consciousness?**

*Ecological intentionality* is the working name for this inquiry. Consciousness is directed *toward* living beings and places, and it may also be directed *through* relationships with them. The proposal is that their activity and possible good can help specify what a practical purpose is for, whose future matters to it, and what could count as its fulfillment. Reception, asymmetrical reciprocity, and intimacy with remainder are concepts being tested through fieldwork and close reading.

The [affirmative workstream (#14)](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14) follows reported changes in attention, anticipation, practical purpose, and responsiveness. It asks how perception, memory, reading, prior commitments, and independently unfolding lives contribute to those changes.

Three claims require different evidence: that relationships shape the history of an intention, that another being's activity helps constitute an ongoing conscious act, and that the action benefits its intended recipient. None follows automatically from the others. Husserl's motivated and genetic intentionality remains a serious account of the documented cases, alongside Stein, Merleau-Ponty, Bateson, and other interlocutors. Ecological psychology and enactivism sharpen the question of what a living being contributes beyond the affordances available to a human observer. Vanessa Watts's Place-Thought challenges the privileged starting point of the human perceiver within its own Indigenous intellectual and political contexts.

Two questions remain distinct: what Merleau-Ponty's late flesh adds to earlier phenomenology, and what ecological intentionality adds to existing accounts. The inquiry can yield a useful account without establishing a new category of consciousness.

A theological companion, [*The Ecology of the Cross* (#5)](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/5), asks about creaturely participation and communion without possession. Its scriptural, Christological, and historical arguments require their own warrant.

## The latest case: what a purpose leaves out

[November 13, *What a Purpose Leaves Out*](field-notes/2026-11-13%20-%20What%20a%20Purpose%20Leaves%20Out.md) distinguishes **how an ecological relationship forms a purpose from how that purpose remains answerable to the lives it affects**. Care for the walnut helped form my intention to move the climbing dome, while plants already visible beneath it were left out of the planning. I also report hearing the children's reasons for keeping the ring. Their purposes preceded my questions; what changed was their explicit place in my deliberation. A jointly revised purpose or changed authority to decide hasn't yet been established.

The same sit corrects the origin of a branch I removed on October 28. It came from the neighboring oak and had lodged in the walnut. The walnut's loss of foliage made a broken oak limb visible; comparison of the breaks, texture and color supported the identification. An independently changing place helped make a mistaken attribution correctable. The fall itself wasn't witnessed, and the removal's ecological benefit remains unknown.

Reading Bateson alongside Michael Barber's account of Schutz also changes the proposed account. Selective purpose and its possible revision already have substantial explanations. Ecological intentionality must explain purposes shaped by a place even when they exclude other lives or resist correction, and ask what particular living beings and their possible goods contribute to an intention's direction. Receptivity can revise a purpose before, during or after action; a return can also responsively confirm it. The [Bateson workstream (#4)](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/4) records this bounded revision. The [dated research history](project/how-the-inquiry-developed.md) preserves the fuller dome-and-ring sequence and the earlier claims it changes.


## How the public record works

The field encounters are recurring philosophical tests. The public notes aim to distinguish what was perceived, inferred, remembered or anticipated, conceptually proposed, and left inaccessible. Later entries can correct earlier interpretations while preserving what was claimed at the time.

The website is the primary reading space. This repository holds curated field notes, concept pages, selected working essays, research history, and method documents that have already crossed a deliberate public boundary. Private journals, unpublished research, correspondence, and source PDFs stay outside this record.

The exporter uses an explicit source allowlist and a privacy guard. Publication details belong in [SETUP.md](SETUP.md) and the [public source map](PUBLIC-SOURCE-MAP.md).

The roadmap and issues preserve competing explanations, possible defeaters, revision trails, and evidence-based completion criteria. Their purpose is to make the inquiry's development inspectable. A checked criterion records a bounded achievement; it doesn't establish the whole proposal.

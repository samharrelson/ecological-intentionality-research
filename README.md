# Ecological Intentionality: Public Research Layer

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.23176757.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23176757)

An ongoing inquiry in ecology, phenomenology, religion, and creaturely relation, grounded in repeated encounters with a black walnut in Spartanburg, South Carolina.

**Read the field notes at [ecologicalintentionality.org](https://ecologicalintentionality.org).** This repository preserves selected public materials as a versioned scholarly record.

## The current question

**How do relationships with independently active beings and places help form the directedness of individual and communal consciousness?**

*Ecological intentionality* is the working name for this inquiry. Consciousness is directed *toward* living beings and places, and it may also be directed *through* relationships with them. The proposal is that their activity and possible good can help specify what a practical purpose is for, whose future matters to it, and what could count as its fulfillment. Reception, asymmetrical reciprocity, and intimacy with remainder are concepts being tested through fieldwork and close reading.

The [affirmative workstream (#14)](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14) follows reported changes in attention, anticipation, practical purpose, and responsiveness. It asks how perception, memory, reading, prior commitments, and independently unfolding lives contribute to those changes.

Three claims require different evidence: that relationships shape the history of an intention, that another being's activity helps constitute an ongoing conscious act, and that the action benefits its intended recipient. None follows automatically from the others. Husserl's motivated and genetic intentionality remains a serious account of the documented cases, alongside Stein, Merleau-Ponty, Bateson, and other interlocutors. Ecological psychology and enactivism sharpen the question of what a living being contributes beyond the affordances available to a human observer. Vanessa Watts's Place-Thought challenges the privileged starting point of the human perceiver within its own Indigenous intellectual and political contexts.

I now have four sustained working comparisons. [*What Flesh Explains*](essays/what-flesh-explains.md) and [*How a Purpose Takes Shape*](essays/how-a-purpose-takes-shape.md), dated November 17, address late flesh and ecological formation. [*A Life of Its Own*](essays/a-life-of-its-own.md), dated November 19, compares Stein and Ruyer on living individuality and distinct goods. [*Seeing a Life Develop*](essays/seeing-a-life-develop.md), dated November 22, follows the recognition of development into inquiry and practical purpose. I develop their constructive account while continuing to test the need for a new basic intentional category.

A theological companion, [*The Ecology of the Cross* (#5)](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/5), asks about creaturely participation and communion without possession. Its scriptural, Christological, and historical arguments require their own warrant.

## Latest essay: Seeing a Life Develop

In [*Seeing a Life Develop*](essays/seeing-a-life-develop.md), I compare my changing recognition of the walnut with Husserl's passive synthesis and Stein's accounts of life, development and inquiry. Husserl already explains how attention and learning work within intentional life. Stein's gardener already moves from apprehending a plant's condition toward investigating causes and seeking a response. I take those resources into the positive account.

My proposal follows the connection among a capacity acquired through relationships, a particular purpose specified through an encounter, and a benefit realized in an independently developing life. Practical purpose serves as one test within the wider inquiry into intentional life. Recognizing the ivy's apparent extension has already specified an inquiry into its identity, development and relation to the walnut. The remembered comparison supplies no measurement of growth or evidence of injury.

The published comparison and its edition-conscious review complete the final primary-comparison criterion in [#9](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/9). ## November 19 essay: A Life of Its Own

In [*A Life of Its Own: Stein, Ruyer, and Creaturely Difference*](essays/a-life-of-its-own.md), I ask how another organism's good can become knowable without its experience becoming mine. Stein already supplies an account of self-formation, individuality and natural measure. Ruyer's active self-survey and primary organic consciousness make a further metaphysical commitment. I compare those claims without treating either the walnut's appearance or ecological interaction as proof of one shared conscious individual.

Clemson Extension's documented relation between English ivy and coral bells gives the argument a particular case of different biological goods. The report of competition and shading is distinct from my interpretation of those goods and from a judgment about what to do. It does not establish injury to the vine's host in my own field setting.

The verified essay meets the two remaining bounded criteria in [#3: creaturely individuality and distinct goods](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/3). The issue remains open in Evidence review. After the separate assessments, the live formal total is **47 checked / 11 unchecked across 14 open issues**. ## The latest case: the inquiry becomes a purpose

In [November 22, *The Life I Return To*](field-notes/2026-11-22%20-%20The%20Life%20I%20Return%20To.md), I distinguish what I have learned to recognize from what has changed in the encounter. Botanical learning helps me recognize the leaf stalks. Rain changes the bark I touch. The ivy's apparent extension and the plants' apparent spread into the old footprint remain comparisons with remembered appearances.

The ivy gives my inquiry a particular object and questions whose answers could guide care. That undertaking already has a direction while the biological investigation and any management decision remain ahead. My separate report of research sustaining the winter restraint shows how inquiry can also support a retained purpose. I follow these different developments through [#14](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14), whose five formal criteria remain checked.

The theological continuation asks how participation forms the person who returns from contemplation. I retain [Bonaventure's](concepts/Bonaventure.md) Christological itinerary and its passage beyond sensible and intellectual activity. Returning to creaturely needs as a test of communal purpose is my constructive extension. [Moltmann](concepts/J%C3%BCrgen%20Moltmann.md) helps me distinguish creaturely worth, individuation and community; [Harris](concepts/Melanie%20Harris.md) keeps authority, labor and exposure within the questions a community must face. The proposed garden and pruning inquiry still require evidence before I can use either as a real conflict case.

## November 21: receiving and redirecting attention

In [November 21, *Already Underway*](field-notes/2026-11-21%20-%20Already%20Underway.md), an attached twig becomes a focus of visual and tactile attention, and a recognizable detail in a familiar image of the walnut becomes newly significant. The image is known as a representation; the living tree's ongoing development gives a different referent and practical stake. The public account leaves the twig identification open to further close documentation and makes no claim that the dome relocation benefited the root.

My constructive question concerns **consciousness directed toward the world and capable of receiving and changing direction through encounter**. A relation can help form a capacity, specify or revise a particular purpose, and supply conditions for the purpose's fulfillment in another life. Husserl already describes affection preceding attentive turning, Brentano distinguishes intentional reference in different modes, and Bateson's ecological circuits explain feedback and learning. The positive account must state what a particular living beneficiary's organization and good contribute. I do not infer that the tree intends toward me or that these cases demonstrate a new basic intentional act.

See [#14](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14) for the affirmative formation question, [#1](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/1) for the strongest Husserlian reconstruction, and [#4](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/4) for Bateson's challenge. The separate theological inquiry [#5](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/5) asks how participation in Christ can form shared purposes and obligations. This continuation adds no completed criterion.

## November 20: a capacity formed through particular lives

[November 20, *The Lives That Form Us*](field-notes/2026-11-20%20-%20The%20Lives%20That%20Form%20Us.md) helps me name a capacity developed through the practice: I have become readier to reconsider an arrangement through a life affected by it. The exposed root brings back the climbing-dome move I had already reported on November 11. Today's reflection interprets that history; it does not record another move or establish that the root benefited.

I distinguish acquiring a capacity from exercising it toward a particular life. A habit can persist between visits and travel into another setting. Adequate judgment about this root still requires learning its conditions. Perception, remembered encounters, inquiry and prior commitments help specify a purpose whose success also depends on a life that continues independently of my intentions. Husserl's motivation and habituality and Bateson's ecological learning remain substantial resources for explaining this formation.

Gregersen gives the theological argument a positive account of formation. In §I of his 2001 essay, his reading of Luther connects receiving grace and faith with becoming able to act in love and justice. The 2015 introduction describes participation in divine life as differing with the recipient. I cite Luther and Aquinas here through Gregersen; this review did not independently check their passages. These resources help me develop the proposal that participation in Christ can form a community's capacity to learn, deliberate and share responsibilities.

Gregersen's extended incarnational scope includes trees, grass, soil and moisture, while leaving final salvation as a separate question. My hope for transformed communion needs its own argument. His account of suffering also requires scrutiny: a community cannot describe another creature's injury as its own self-giving.

This continuation strengthens [#14](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14) and [#5](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/5) without adding a completed criterion. The real creaturely and institutional conflict case remains open.

## How the public record works

The field encounters are recurring philosophical tests. The public notes aim to distinguish what was perceived, inferred, remembered or anticipated, conceptually proposed, and left inaccessible. Later entries can correct earlier interpretations while preserving what was claimed at the time.

The website is the primary reading space. This repository holds curated field notes, concept pages, selected working essays, research history, and method documents that have already crossed a deliberate public boundary. Private journals, unpublished research, correspondence, and source PDFs stay outside this record.

The exporter uses an explicit source allowlist and a privacy guard. Publication details belong in [SETUP.md](SETUP.md) and the [public source map](PUBLIC-SOURCE-MAP.md).

The roadmap and issues preserve competing explanations, possible defeaters, revision trails, and evidence-based completion criteria. Their purpose is to make the inquiry's development inspectable. A checked criterion records a bounded achievement; it doesn't establish the whole proposal.

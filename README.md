# Ecological Intentionality: Public Research Layer

[![DOI](https://zenodo.org/badge/DOI/10.5281/zenodo.23176757.svg)](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23176757)

This repository is a public research layer for an ongoing inquiry in ecology, phenomenology, religion, and creaturely relation.

The work is grounded in repeated field encounters, currently centered on a black walnut tree in Spartanburg, South Carolina. The field practice is not treated as an illustration added after the philosophy is finished. It is one of the places where philosophical claims are tested, corrected, narrowed, and sometimes abandoned.

The primary public field-notes site is:

**https://ecologicalintentionality.org**

This repository serves a different purpose. It makes selected research materials easier to inspect as a versioned scholarly record: public field notes, concept pages, methodological documents, reading orientation, and the changing shape of the inquiry.

## Current research pressure

**Ecological intentionality isn't just phenomenology with more objects. It's an inquiry into the ecological formation of intentional life itself.**

I am developing a **positive account of conscious directedness within ecological relationships**. Consciousness is directed *toward* living beings and places, but it may also be directed *through* relations with independently active lives. Those relations can help specify what a practical intention is *for*, whose possible good matters, what future it anticipates, and what could count as its fulfillment. **Reception, asymmetrical reciprocity, and intimacy with remainder** are working concepts in this account, not results established by naming them.

Repeated Black Walnut encounters have changed my reported attention, expectations, practical judgments, and plans, sometimes outside the original sits. They involve perception, reading, memory, prior commitments, and the walnut's independent development. The constructive question concerns **present intentional life as well as its history**: how another being's activity and possible good might participate in forming a definite yet revisable purpose, without that being sharing the human intention or losing its own independent future.

This documented first-person transformation is philosophically important whether or not Husserl's genetic intentionality, Stein's nuanced account of otherness, Merleau-Ponty's flesh, or Bateson's ecological learning can explain its structure. The challenge of those accounts is essential, but **the whole research program cannot be reduced to defeating Husserl**. A stronger claim that the other organism is a new constitutive factor *within a particular conscious act* still requires independent argument. Vanessa Watts's Place-Thought challenges the even more basic assumption that the human perceiver should be the privileged starting point; her distinct Indigenous intellectual and political contexts must not be treated as portable confirmation of the European philosophical proposal.

[**The affirmative formation workstream (#14)**](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14) now leads the public roadmap, alongside [the still-essential Husserl rival test (#1)](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/1). The difference is between **the phenomenon to be explained** and **the question whether an existing theory explains it adequately**.

Ecological intentionality began as a name for a disciplined form of creaturely attention: attending to another being as relationally given, irreducibly other, and never exhausted by its appearance within human experience.

The inquiry has since moved toward a more ambitious constructive question. I am developing ecological intentionality as a positive philosophical proposal about how conscious directedness emerges, develops, and changes within relations among differently organized beings whose activities, histories, and goods cannot be reduced to the purposes of the perceiver.

The distinction I am investigating is not only between being *conscious of an ecology* and being *ecologically constituted*. It is also between recognizing that our intentions are influenced by relationships and explaining how those relationships participate in the formation and realization of particular conscious intentions.

This proposal is not yet an established philosophical structure. It must be tested against existing accounts of intentionality, embodiment, learning, and relational constitution.

### Two independent philosophical questions

1. **Late flesh:** What, if anything, does Merleau-Ponty's late ontology of flesh explain beyond Husserl's layered account of intentionality, embodiment, passive synthesis, and intersubjectivity, and beyond Merleau-Ponty's earlier phenomenology?
2. **Ecological intentionality:** What positive account of conscious directedness, if any, is needed when intentional life is understood as developing within relationships among beings with their own forms of organization, activity, and potentially conflicting goods? What does such an account explain that Husserl, Stein, Merleau-Ponty, Bateson, Ruyer, process philosophy, and existing ecological approaches do not already explain?

These questions are related, but answering the first does not establish the second.

### Where the fieldwork is taking the argument

The Black Walnut Field Notes have become a recurring test of these questions. The encounters distinguish what is perceptually given, what is inferred through biological or historical knowledge, what is remembered or anticipated, and what remains inaccessible.

The October and early November 2026 sits have introduced another question: whether sustained relationship with the walnut is reorganizing how I encounter beings and situations beyond the backyard, including other humans.

This concerns more than acquiring information or developing environmental concern. It involves changes in perceptual salience, anticipation, responsiveness, and what I come to intend or become willing to revise.

But those changes may already be explained by Husserlian motivation and habituality, Merleau-Ponty's embodied perception, or Bateson's ecological learning and distributed mental processes. The research must distinguish developmental influence, participation in an ongoing intentional act, and the normative adequacy of an action. None can be assumed from the others.

### November 11–12: done isn't finished, and the return

In the [November 11 sit, *Done Isn't Finished*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-11), I report having moved the climbing dome that morning. The action is complete. Its benefit to the walnut is unknown and may stay unknown for years. Where the dome had stood, a ring of low plants became something I had to decide about, and I committed to leaving it through the winter. I'd seen those plants for two years. I had planned the move for days without once asking what would become of them, because my care was aimed at the root. **A purpose directed toward one beneficiary can leave other visible lives out of consideration until the finished action raises the question.** I withdrew my own spoken claim of "a real case of goods in conflict" as ahead of the evidence.

The [November 12 sit, *Who Asked for the Ring*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-12), is the first morning I came back to a yard I'd already changed. I tried a fuller shape for the account there. An ecological intention has three moments: an **aim** that organizes what counts as relevant, an **act** that changes the situation the aim was formed in, and a **return** in which the purpose comes back into the presence of something that can correct it. Around any purpose there are three positions: who it's for, who it touches without meaning to, and who it leaves out. My working claim is that the return is how a life moves between those positions, as the ring did. A return that only ever confirms what I expected would count against it, and so far I have one clear case.

Two returns took place away from the grass. Reading Clemson Extension's guidance on liriope made a late-winter cutback thinkable as care, so the means of the winter commitment are open while its purpose stands. And the ring exists because I was asked not to mow there. I carried that out for two years without asking why, then gave the practice a new reason on my own. A practice carried on together doesn't show a purpose held together, which is where Stein's account of communal experience presses.

None of this is new as a bare temporal structure. Husserl's lectures on willing already distinguish an action fulfilled in the doing from a resolve that reaches past it. Stein already separates knowing a possible good from its realization. Merleau-Ponty's account of institution already describes an event that opens a field and calls for a sequel. What I'm left to argue is what the return answers to: another creature's actual condition and other people's actual reasons, neither of which my purpose authored. The theological strand turned to Leviticus 25 and to John 1:14 (ὁ λόγος σὰρξ ἐγένετο καὶ ἐσκήνωσεν ἐν ἡμῖν), with Moltmann, Gregersen and Maximus as readings still in progress.

### November 9–10: received direction and a future that remains open

The [November 9 sit, *Directed Through This World*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-09) describes a practical intention to relocate a children's climbing dome near a visible walnut root, along with an earlier trial establishing that I believe I can move it. The trial concerns **practical capacity**. The intended end concerns a possible arrangement that accommodates play and the walnut's continuing life. No root injury, completed relocation, or ecological benefit was established.

In the [November 10 sit, *A Future Not Mine Alone*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-10), I anticipated rapid leaf loss but was still surprised by its extent and pace. My expectation became more specific, while the dome-care project remained intended and unperformed in the recording. **Changed anticipation, sustained intention, revised means, and a genuinely changed end require different evidence.**

These encounters contribute to a positive proposal: an intention can be definite about a beneficiary and possible future while remaining answerable to another living being's independent development and the evidence of what would help or harm it. That is a claim about the direction and corrigibility of conscious purpose, not proof of a new basic act-structure. A related question concerns how a congregation might develop a genuinely shared *we*-intention within the lives of a place; that remains a prospective philosophical and theological inquiry, not a reported church outcome. See the [current inquiry](project/where-the-inquiry-stands.md) and [affirmative workstream #14](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14).

### November 7: the walnut helps shape the history of intentional life

The published [November 7 sit, *Whose Good Governs the Act?*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-07) develops the positive finding and makes its limitations more demanding. The living walnut, unlike a known LEGO tree, has a history of independent growth and vulnerability that makes my intentions of care answerable to something I don't create by perceiving it. A photograph of the *real* tree can still mediate that care, so medium, intended beneficiary, practical motive and the living being's independent goods must be distinguished rather than treated as a simple alive/dead contrast.

Today's reflection also corrects the earlier mowing story: the original plan served *soil care and safe access* as well as tidiness, while leaving leaves whole is now intended partly for habitat. Different beings and practical ends have to be weighed rather than assimilated into one harmonious good. A climbing ivy vine is directly visible; the identification of species and the ecological consequences require more evidence.

**Live evidence-based roadmap steps:** a published, charitable [Husserl reconstruction](concepts/Husserl.md) meets a narrowly scoped completion criterion for [#1](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/1), without proving that Husserl fails. A specific public distinction between coexistence and genuinely different organismic goods meets one limited conceptual criterion in [#3](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/3), without an actual measured conflict. The main positive [formation problem #14](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14) now has a bounded longitudinal evidence matrix and a provisional five-part account; its Husserl/Bateson/Watts comparative criterion remains open.

### November 6: an ecological commitment and the operative end of an act

The [November 6 public field note, *When Another Life Changes the End*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-06), adds a concrete, defeasible example. After considering another weekend of mowing walnut leaves **to tidy the yard**, I read ecological guidance about intact leaf litter, overwintering habitat, and the disputed generality of juglone effects. **Before** the morning sit I decided to leave the fallen leaves whole in the naturalized area. During the recording I described the earlier tidiness motive and the renewed importance of the walnut, soil organisms, insects, birds, and other lives. I concluded that I thought what the immediate decision was *for* had changed.

This is not a claim that ecological care was invented overnight: an October 28 sit had already affirmed leaving litter for winter habitat. A *standing commitment* can endure while a different purpose governs a particular proposed act; later learning may re-prioritize the goods that determine that act. But the earlier motive is reconstructed retrospectively, the weekend's restraint had not yet been carried out, and particular biological benefits remain unmeasured. A witnessed leaf falling during the sit and the prior biological research are **different** sources of evidence; the leaf and squirrels did not cause the earlier decision.

A change of operative purpose is also **not evidence by itself for a new constitutive kind of consciousness**. Husserl's motivated practical intentionality remains a central rival, now joined by Roberta De Monticelli's analysis of plans and standing commitments and Michael Barber's reconstruction of Alfred Schütz's projected actions. James Jardine's study of Stein's editorial role in the transmitted *Ideas II* §56 also complicates a simple Husserl-versus-Stein historical narrative; manuscript-level verification remains unfinished. The living beneficiaries' independent goods are relevant to *what could make the action adequate*, not automatic proof that their activity constitutes the person's intending.

The parallel theological question is whether a congregation's enacted purposes can be corrected even where its professed commitments already include care for creation. This is a **constructive question**, not a completed congregational case, a claim of ecological benefit, or a Christological conclusion inferred from falling leaves. See the [public inquiry overview](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/inquiry) and [research roadmap](RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md).

### November 5: from explaining change to justifying care

The [November 5 field sit, *The Yellow Sea and the Ends of Care*](field-notes/2026-11-05%20-%20The%20Yellow%20Sea%20and%20the%20Ends%20of%20Care.md) sharpens a concrete distinction. A familiar route and unexpectedly open canopy generated a question about why leaves fell; a correct causal explanation would **not by itself** determine whether leaving, moving, or mulching their litter would benefit the walnut, its neighbors, or the soil community. The inquiry now separates **what happened**, **what available actions might do to particular lives**, and **which goods and beneficiaries should guide an intended action**. The actual plant comparison remains unperformed, and no incompatible organismic goods have been demonstrated.

This supplies an increasingly specific **beneficiary-sensitive account of practical purpose**, not yet evidence that another life constitutes the conscious act. Husserl's motivated and habitual intentionality, Stein's physician and gardener examples, contemporary phenomenologies of agency, and [Peirce's account of inquiry and agapic growth](concepts/Charles%20S.%20Peirce.md) are serious explanatory predecessors and rivals. The next decisive question is: **Can another being's independently unfolding good alter the *end* of an intention, rather than only its information, means, or later standard of success?** A strong rival account could explain everything documented so far.

A possible future Presbyterian class in religion and ecology provides a separate theological and institutional question: could learning about a place and its affected communities revise what a congregation understands its mission to be *for*? The class has been proposed, not approved or conducted, and no study or ecological outcome is being claimed. Biblical and doctrinal warrant for the *Ecology of the Cross* remains distinct from field observation and institutional certification. Follow the [current inquiry](project/where-the-inquiry-stands.md), the [public sources](project/sources-and-reading.md), and the [open roadmap](RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md) for the continuing tests.

### Current philosophical interlocutors

The central conversation includes:

- **Edmund Husserl:** Intentionality, constitution, passive synthesis, embodiment, motivation, and the horizons of experience.
- **Edith Stein:** Empathy, non-primordiality, living form, creaturely individuality, and the limits of knowing another's life.
- **Maurice Merleau-Ponty:** Embodiment, flesh, chiasm, reversibility, *écart*, and the relationship between early phenomenology and late ontology.
- **Gregory Bateson:** Distributed mind, ecological circuits, memory, recursive learning, and the boundaries of individual mental processes.
- **Charles S. Peirce:** Surprise, inquiry, developing practical meaning, and the tension between independence and harmony in agapic growth. Formal abduction requires its own source comparison.
- **Raymond Ruyer:** Organismic form, immanent organization, absolute survey, and the relationship between self-relation and living activity.
- **Ecological psychology and enactivism:** Gibson's affordances, Koffka's distinction between behavioral and geographical environments, and work on skilled engagement and social practices raise a relevant question about how meaning takes shape. The developing comparison asks what the living other's own activity and possible good contribute beyond the affordances available to the human observer; no full comparative reading is claimed here.
- **Whiteheadian and process phenomenology:** Whether relational becoming and process already account for what ecological intentionality proposes.
- **Indigenous relational thought, ecowomanism, and ecological theology:** Vanessa Watts, Robin Wall Kimmerer, Melanie Harris, and others challenge accounts that begin with a detached human perceiver or overlook the historical, political, and material conditions of ecological relations.

Recent work on the plurality of nonhuman experiential worlds further complicates these questions. Ecological belonging does not establish shared phenomenal consciousness, and biological organization does not, by itself, establish an experiential world.

### A developing theological companion

Alongside this philosophical inquiry, *The Ecology of the Cross* is being developed as an affirmative theology of creaturely participation.

Its central question is how incarnation, crucifixion, and resurrection might illuminate forms of communion that neither erase creaturely difference nor reduce vulnerable lives to instruments of another's purposes.

This theological proposal requires its own Christological, scriptural, and historical arguments. It cannot be established through ecological observation alone, nor should ecological interdependence be confused with redemption.

### What remains unresolved

Ecological intentionality may ultimately identify a distinct structure of intentional relation, offer a constructive account of existing structures, clarify a transformative configuration of intentional capacities, or prove unnecessary as a separate category.

The inquiry does not assume its own novelty.

Its current task is to formulate a defensible positive proposition, identify what would distinguish it from rival accounts, and allow fieldwork and textual evidence to revise or defeat it.

Two working formulations remain important:

> **Difference need not mean separation; belonging need not mean coincidence.**

And:

> **Ecological intentionality does not begin by asking how consciousness possesses the world, but how consciousness comes to be through relations with a world it can never possess.**

These are propositions to investigate, not conclusions the research has already secured.

## Publication model

The repository records material that has **already** crossed a deliberate public boundary; it does not decide what becomes public. Only the currently approved Essays index and two established working essays are included; other files in the public Essays folder are not automatically mirrored. The local exporter reads only an explicit allowlist under `Public/Tracking`, converts links only when their targets are also public, and flattens unresolved or non-public Obsidian links to plain text. A privacy guard runs before commits are pushed, and GitHub Actions validates the public layer again after each push.

Implementation details and local commands live in [`SETUP.md`](SETUP.md) and [`PUBLIC-SOURCE-MAP.md`](PUBLIC-SOURCE-MAP.md) so the repository front page can remain centered on the research rather than the plumbing.

## Black Walnut sits: dedicated RSS

A separate, **sits-only** RSS feed is generated from the curated public Home index and already-exported field-note metadata. It includes the **full text** of each curated public sit for reading directly in Inoreader or NetNewsWire, while retaining a short preview. It does not announce Sources-page edits, thinker-page revisions, or other site maintenance. Photos and private recordings remain off-feed. Once GitHub Pages is enabled, subscribe at:

[**Black Walnut Field Notes RSS**](https://samharrelson.github.io/ecological-intentionality-research/sits.xml)

The website itself remains on Obsidian Publish. The feed is a separate, minimal static endpoint. See [SETUP.md](SETUP.md) for the one-time Pages configuration and the existing publish command.

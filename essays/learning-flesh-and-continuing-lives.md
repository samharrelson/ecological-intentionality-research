*Merleau-Ponty, Kirkpatrick, and the Reach of a Continuity Argument*

*November 23, 2026 · Working comparison · Version 1*

I develop this comparison beside [What Flesh Explains](what-flesh-explains.md). The earlier essay argues for late flesh's account of sensible belonging and noncoincidence. Here I reconstruct [Kirkpatrick's](../concepts/Andrew%20Trevor%20Kirkpatrick.md) strongest continuity case beside [Merleau-Ponty's](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md) primary texts and ask what its process interpretation requires. My account of ecological intentionality must grant that explanation its force before proposing a further contribution.

## The encounter and the positive proposal

I return from a morning of teaching with fallen leaves and a magnifying glass still prominent in my thinking. [The November 23 sit](../field-notes/2026-11-23%20-%20What%20We%20Learn%20to%20See.md) follows that return. Looking through the glass made leaf patterns easier to inspect. At the walnut, fallen compound-leaf stalks now have a significance I recall missing for years. The recording also supplies a particular moment in the remembered lesson: a question about falling leaves redirected my explanation toward both biological attachment and gravity. These are three different contributions: altered optical access, learned recognition and conversational specification.

My positive proposal connects a history of acquired capacities with the specification of a present undertaking and its independent conditions of fulfillment. Relationships can form the person who attends; an encountered feature or another person's question can make an inquiry more definite; what happens in the lives concerned can determine whether the intended good occurs. Here the intention is to explain and investigate leaf structure and falling, on the timescale of a lesson and a later sit. The history of learning spans earlier visits and research. A future care judgment about this tree has a further evidential timescale.

This account concerns several forms of intentional life: seeing, remembering, imagining, judging and undertaking. A change in practical explanation is a tractable case within that wider inquiry. I do not infer the constitution of an ongoing conscious act from a developmental influence or a standard of successful care.

## The strongest continuity reconstruction

**1. Embodied access already has a history.** Early Merleau-Ponty describes the cane becoming an instrument through which the blind person perceives, and learning as a change in bodily capacities and perceptual style. The distinction between an object attended to and an incorporated means of access matters. A lens changing visibility on one occasion does not establish its durable incorporation into a bodily habit. The early account nevertheless provides the conceptual resources for investigating such acquisition. My magnifier encounter gives me a concrete case for testing those existing resources.[^early]

**2. Reflection begins within the world it describes.** Kirkpatrick's reconstruction treats the incomplete reduction and rejection of objective thought as methodological commitments that continue across the early and late works. The introduction presents the early account as dialectical, bringing transcendental and existential dimensions into relation. The continuity claim is therefore deeper than repeated vocabulary: an account of experience must recognize the involvement of its own act of description.[^kirk]

**3. Self-criticism can enact continuity while revising a commitment.** I find an important resource for Kirkpatrick in the early text itself. On Landes p. 426, the tacit cogito has a fleeting hold on itself, and its recovery requires perceptual exploration and speech. The paragraph ends by making expression necessary for it to be a cogito. The late working notes question the tacit cogito and explain how describing silence already transforms it through language. Kirkpatrick reads this self-criticism chiefly as a difficulty of expression within an ongoing philosophical method. His strongest reply therefore draws on the early account's own dependence on expression. I need to identify the depth of the revision while granting that continuity.[^cogito][^kirk]

**4. Flesh gives worldly involvement ontological scope.** The seer belongs to the visible, and touching belongs to the tangible. Noncoincidence prevents this belonging from becoming identity. The later account asks how sensing and sensed differentiate within a sensible world. It develops what the early bodily account already accomplishes, while changing the explanatory scope. My hand gesture offers a description to compare with that account; it does not establish the tree as a sensing counterpart.[^late]

**5. The process reading extends the relation toward natural becoming.** Kirkpatrick's chapter 5 connects co-prehension and co-naissance with the “I can.” He proposes a broader sensorial “can” and distinguishes subjectivity from reflective consciousness. The cane provides a strong test because an artifact may enter a bodily capacity. Its possible incorporation does not ensure actual incorporation; person and cane retain different styles of being. Kirkpatrick nevertheless attributes latent intentional potentiality to the cane before it enters a human body schema. His account therefore includes a metaphysical claim about natural capacities alongside an account of acquired human skill. Style, dehiscence and divergence preserve differences within that extension.[^style]

**6. Reflective access remains incomplete.** Kirkpatrick's sensorial-agent and ontological-return discussions preserve the transformation that occurs when reflection describes prereflective activity. His continuity account can explain formation, received direction and distance together. I also find a useful control in Merleau-Ponty's *Nature* lectures: nature continues through appearances that never exhaust it, and its activity differs from the activity of consciousness or mind. Whitehead enters this comparison through Kirkpatrick and those lectures. A direct reconstruction of Whitehead's own system remains a separate task.[^nature]

I have reconstructed two possible tensions with the strongest replies available from the selected texts. The first concerns how deep a revision continuity can accommodate. The second concerns the warrant for a wider metaphysical commitment while preserving the differences the text recognizes. The reconstruction leaves me with a qualified judgment about the reading and a clearer account of the questions that survive it.

## What ecological intentionality can contribute

I concede that acquired capacities, mediated perception, a worldly history and incomplete self-access already have substantial explanations here. I cannot claim originality by naming them ecological. The further candidate contribution is a connected account of how independently living beings participate differently in the formation, present specification and possible fulfillment of intentional undertakings.

The comparisons need fixed questions. If the task is recognizing a compound leaf, a diagram, photograph or fallen leaf may succeed. If it is assessing this walnut's current relation to a climbing plant, evidence of the actual beings and their conditions becomes necessary. A photograph can mediate that evidence, so bodily presence is not the sole route. If it is benefiting the walnut, success depends on effects in its continuing life and cannot be established by a convincing representation or my completed action alone. An artificial screen can change illumination; that optical role by itself does not establish the distinctive contribution of a living tree. A known replica may teach structure, while a mistaken belief that it is alive can sustain an intention whose supposed living beneficiary does not exist.

The distinction concerns the role of the referent and the task's conditions of adequacy. It remains compatible with much of Merleau-Ponty's and Kirkpatrick's account. Non-assimilation presently functions as an ontological and normative constraint on my explanation; it is not yet a demonstrated mechanism of constitutive realization. The constructive task is to show how the connected account improves an explanation of an actual sequence, including cases where habits mislead or evidence leaves an end unchanged.

The encounter presses the texts to distinguish optical access, learned recognition, another person's contribution and a living recipient's good. The texts press my project to stop presenting received direction or incomplete self-access as missing resources. My remaining burden is to establish the necessity and explanatory yield of that further connection.

## Qualified verdict and next test

The continuity case is strong for the reported magnifier encounter and learned recognition. Late flesh articulates belonging and noncoincidence at an ontological level. Kirkpatrick gives that development a process interpretation with explicit resources for difference. I retain the wider extension as a metaphysical proposal requiring further warrant and do not describe it as disproved by this sit.

My next test is to track one specific inquiry: what evidence of the vine–walnut relation changes or confirms its object, beneficiary, end, means or grounds? A later outcome must be distinguished from evidence that my intervention caused it.

The comparison gives me a strong account of continuity, two surviving interpretive pressures and a more definite task for ecological intentionality. I want to explain what the connected account contributes when an undertaking develops through relations with a particular continuing life.

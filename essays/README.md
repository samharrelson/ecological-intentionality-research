*Working arguments in ecological intentionality.*

The Black Walnut Field Notes record particular encounters: what I perceived, remembered, inferred, questioned, and could not determine. The [concept pages](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) follow the philosophical vocabulary and thinkers shaping the inquiry. This section has another purpose. It gathers **extended arguments** that draw those encounters and texts together into propositions a reader can question, correct, or reject.

I publish these as working essays whose claims remain open to criticism and revision. Dates and versions matter. I want readers to see what the argument claimed at a particular point, what evidence it relied on, and what later required revision.

## Working essays

### [Who Has a Say in the Record](who-has-a-say-in-the-record.md)

*Argumentative Genealogy and Reusable Revision in Ecological Inquiry*

**November 27, 2026 · Working essay · Version 1**

I follow corrections to the mowing account, the role of return and the history of recognition to ask what a failed inference can contribute to another inquiry. I compare the practice with environmental phenomenology, reflexive autoethnography and open notebook science, examine my authority over the public record, and propose a reader's test of what its dated revisions make available for criticism and reuse.

### [A Commitment with a Future](a-commitment-with-a-future.md)

*Mariandale, Communal Purpose, and the Lives Affected by a Decision*

**November 25, 2026 · Working case assessment · Version 1**

I follow a documented negotiation between campus redevelopment and land preservation at Mariandale. An enduring commitment gained lasting institutional form through an easement. I test that interpretation against ordinary institutional deliberation, distinguish the partly reconstructed housing history, and bring Moltmann and Harris to the question of whose lives and burdens enter a community's judgment.

### [What Fuller Understanding Requires](what-fuller-understanding-requires.md)

*Stein, Husserl, and the Different Accomplishments of Inquiry*

**November 24, 2026 · Working comparison · Version 1**

I distinguish Stein's three modalities from the question of which beings can be empathically apprehended, then compare her qualified vegetal account with Husserl's appresentation and epistemic/practical fulfillment. Following a particular leaflet gives me a case in which an inquiry becomes more definite while its end stays the same. I grant the predecessors' explanatory strength and identify the further work for ecological intentionality.

### [Learning, Flesh, and Continuing Lives](learning-flesh-and-continuing-lives.md)

*Merleau-Ponty, Kirkpatrick, and the Reach of a Continuity Argument*

**November 23, 2026 · Working comparison · Version 1**

I reconstruct Kirkpatrick's strongest account of continuity from embodied learning into late flesh and a wider process interpretation. The magnifying glass, learned recognition of fallen stalks and a remembered classroom question give the comparison particular cases. I examine the early text's dependence on expression and the extension from bodily ability toward natural subjectivity, then state what remains for ecological intentionality to explain.

### [A Life of Its Own](a-life-of-its-own.md)

*Stein, Ruyer, and Creaturely Difference*

**November 19, 2026 · Working philosophical essay · Version 1**

I ask how I can know something of another organism's good without possessing its experience. Stein already gives me an account of individual living form; Ruyer makes a further claim about immanent organization and primary consciousness. I compare those claims through the vine at the walnut and a documented interaction between two plants. The argument distinguishes what supports each life from what I ought to do, while asking how those independent goods can help specify a purpose.

### [What Flesh Explains](what-flesh-explains.md)

*Embodiment, Belonging, and Noncoincidence*

**November 17, 2026 · Working philosophical essay · Version 1**

I ask what late Merleau-Ponty's flesh adds to Husserl's bodily sensing and early Merleau-Ponty's embodied perception. Beginning with my October 20 contact with the walnut, I argue that flesh articulates the sensible belonging within which perceiver and perceived differentiate. Noncoincidence belongs within that relation. I defend that ontological contribution while keeping the continuity objection and the further demands of ecological explanation in view.

### [How a Purpose Takes Shape](how-a-purpose-takes-shape.md)

*Ecological Formation and Its Strongest Explanations*

**November 17, 2026 · Working philosophical essay · Version 1**

I follow the dome, ring and bamboo through changes in practical possibility, intended beneficiaries, action and later inquiry. Holding those differences constant, I compare Husserl's motivated acts, Bateson's distributed circuits and Watts's challenge to a human-centered starting point. The constructive claim concerns how relationships help specify what a purpose is for and what could fulfill it. The record supports that inquiry while leaving the need for a new basic intentional category open.

### [Whose Good?](whose-good.md)

*Ecological Intentionality and the Formation of Practical Purpose*

**November 3, 2026 · Working philosophical essay · Site version 1**

What makes an intention one of care *for* another living being? A swing removed from a black walnut, a spider that prompted delay, a handmade artificial tree, and fallen foliage raise a constructive proposal: practical purposes can become more specific and remain corrigible through encounters with living beings whose goods aren't defined by the intending person.

The proposal is affirmative, but its stronger claim remains unsettled. A beneficiary's actual condition can test the success of care; that doesn't, by itself, establish that the beneficiary's ongoing activity constitutes the conscious intention.

## Earlier constructive statement

### [Consciousness in a World We Cannot Possess](consciousness-in-a-world-we-cannot-possess.md)

**October 24, 2026 · Initial affirmative proposition**

This shorter statement proposes *co-constitution without co-intention*: the possibility that other beings' independently unfolding activities participate in the formation or realization of consciousness without sharing the human subject's intentions. *Whose Good?* develops the more limited, testable account of practical purpose without withdrawing that more ambitious question.

## Questions the essays must face

1. Does the beneficiary-sensitive account explain anything beyond Husserl's motivation, passive synthesis, valuation, and layered structures of intentionality?
2. How can I investigate the different possible goods of organisms without assuming harmony, asserting conflict without evidence, or mistaking a description for another being's own experience?
3. What would show that another life contributes to the **ongoing realization** of an intentional act rather than influencing its formation, its practical motivation, or the eventual success of care?

My [current inquiry](../project/where-the-inquiry-stands.md) and [dated research history](../project/how-the-inquiry-developed.md) document how these questions change. The [open GitHub research board](https://github.com/users/samharrelson/projects/1) preserves proposed tests and objections without treating an essay's appearance as proof of its claims.

## About versions and sources

The essays include public-facing descriptions and links to sources and field notes. Their citations may still need edition-level verification before use in a committee paper. A later revision should identify what changed and why; it should not quietly erase a serious objection or make an earlier claim look more warranted than it was.

The website is the primary reading space. After a page has been deliberately published and checked here, selected public material can also enter the [versioned research repository](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research). Private working drafts and raw journals remain separate.

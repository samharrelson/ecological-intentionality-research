# Essays

*Working arguments in ecological intentionality.*

The Black Walnut Field Notes record particular encounters: what I perceived, remembered, inferred, questioned, and could not determine. The [concept pages](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md) follow the philosophical vocabulary and thinkers shaping the inquiry. This section has another purpose. It gathers **extended arguments** that draw those encounters and texts together into propositions a reader can question, correct, or reject.

I'm publishing these as working essays, not as established findings or finished statements of a theory of consciousness. Dates and versions matter. I want readers to see what the argument claimed at a particular point, what evidence it relied on, and what later required revision.

## Working essays

### [Whose Good?](whose-good.md)

*Ecological Intentionality and the Formation of Practical Purpose*

**November 3, 2026 · Working philosophical essay · Site version 1**

What makes an intention one of care *for* another living being? A swing removed from a black walnut, a spider that prompted delay, a handmade artificial tree, and fallen foliage raise a constructive proposal: practical purposes can become more specific and remain corrigible through encounters with living beings whose goods aren't defined by the intending person.

The proposal is affirmative, but its stronger claim remains unsettled. A beneficiary's actual condition can test the success of care; that doesn't, by itself, establish that the beneficiary's ongoing activity constitutes the conscious intention.

## Earlier constructive statement

### [Consciousness in a World We Cannot Possess](consciousness-in-a-world-we-cannot-possess.md)

**October 24, 2026 · Initial affirmative proposition**

This shorter statement proposes *co-constitution without co-intention*: the possibility that other beings' independently unfolding activities participate in the formation or realization of consciousness without sharing the human subject's intentions. *Whose Good?* develops the more limited, testable account of practical purpose without withdrawing that more ambitious question.

## Questions the essays must face

1. Does the beneficiary-sensitive account explain anything beyond Husserl's motivation, passive synthesis, valuation, and layered structures of intentionality?
2. How can I investigate the different possible goods of organisms without assuming harmony, asserting conflict without evidence, or mistaking a description for another being's own experience?
3. What would show that another life contributes to the **ongoing realization** of an intentional act rather than influencing its formation, its practical motivation, or the eventual success of care?

My [current inquiry](../project/where-the-inquiry-stands.md) and [dated research history](../project/how-the-inquiry-developed.md) document how these questions change. The [open GitHub research board](https://github.com/users/samharrelson/projects/1) preserves proposed tests and objections without treating an essay's appearance as proof of its claims.

## About versions and sources

The essays include public-facing descriptions and links to sources and field notes. Their citations may still need edition-level verification before use in a committee paper. A later revision should identify what changed and why; it should not quietly erase a serious objection or make an earlier claim look more warranted than it was.

The website is the primary reading space. After a page has been deliberately published and checked here, selected public material can also enter the [versioned research repository](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research). Private working drafts and raw journals remain separate.

*Ecological Formation and Its Strongest Explanations*

*November 17, 2026 · Working essay · Version 1*

I first drafted this section on November 16 and offer this revision for criticism.

I am developing ecological intentionality as an account of how conscious purposes take shape within relationships with independently active lives and places. Those relationships can help specify what I intend, whose good enters the purpose, how I act, and what future I anticipate. The walnut practice gives this proposal a sequence to explain. It also requires me to distinguish the formation of a purpose, its answerability to evidence and others' reasons, and its adequacy to the lives affected. An intention can be ecologically formed while remaining selective or mistaken.

My decision to move the climbing dome developed through several distinguishable moments. On November 9, I tested that practical possibility while considering the walnut's possible good and continued play. On November 11, I reported moving it. These were different achievements: discovering an ability, intending an intervention, and performing it. None established root damage beforehand or benefit afterward. The purpose reached toward the walnut's future, but that future remained independent of my intention.

The ring of plants in the dome's former footprint then became an explicit beneficiary of a commitment to leave it through winter. The plants had already been visible and previously left in place. What changed was their practical consideration within the purpose I now articulated. The bamboo supplied a different case. On November 15, three shoots gave an established removal preference present targets. I retained both the means and the end. Ecological formation included opposition to growth as well as care for it.

On November 16, I reported cutting the shoots the previous afternoon. Protecting neighboring plants became part of what I tried to accomplish during cutting. Conversation before and afterward introduced a different valuation of bamboo's beauty and usefulness. I reported agreement about the immediate act and an expectation of participation in later decisions. This supplied evidence of practical specification and an anticipated shared undertaking, without establishing identical motives, durable shared authority, or ecological benefit. On November 17, I reported researching bamboo's uses together the previous evening. The objection prompted further inquiry and changed my regard, while the cutting remained completed and no cultivation plan had been adopted.[^1]

Across these cases, I ask the same questions: what became different in the intention, what brought that difference about, what does an explanation require, and what remains unaccounted for? Each account must explain the same changes.

Husserl offers a substantial explanation. In the *Analyses Concerning Passive and Active Synthesis*, activity can become sedimented into passivity, while affection, attentive turning, feeling, desire, and willing remain distinguishable. Being drawn toward something does not mean welcoming it or deciding to preserve it. The shoots can solicit attention within a history of opposition; the ring can be seen without entering the practical concern that governs my plan.[^2]

*Ideas II* extends this account into practical and communal life. Bodily possibility, habitual anticipation, values, ends, and means help explain how an intention becomes determinate. Communication allows assent, refusal, and counterproposal to affect the person who first addresses another. These resources can explain protection becoming explicit during cutting and another person's reasons altering the expected future of our decisions.[^3] The account requires a history of meaningful motivation, not only a physical cause. These achievements do not alone show that another organism's ongoing activity constitutes the conscious act, rather than helping explain its history and specify its content or fulfillment. The present sequence leaves that stronger claim open.

Bateson challenges any claim that ecological formation begins with my proposal. His relevant circuit crosses the boundary of the individual organism; memory and differences that guide action need not be located wholly inside the person. The arrangement of shoots and neighboring plants can enter the adjustments through which cutting proceeds. Learned contexts also help explain why the walnut becomes a beneficiary while the ring initially remains outside the purpose governing the move.

His account of self-validating contexts makes the unchanged bamboo decision especially useful. My familiar way of organizing the yard might help produce the agreement I expect. Yet an unchanged end does not establish that learning has stopped, and replacing a premise does not demonstrate Learning III. Bateson also recognizes discrepant organismic purposes; I cannot claim that naming particular lives or conflicting goods supplies something absent from his account.[^4] His explanation requires tracing differences, feedback, and learned contexts across the relevant circuit. The explanatory units differ. Husserl reconstructs an act's motivated sense within lived and communal experience; Bateson follows differences through a circuit that can exceed any participant. They can be complementary. A tension arises if circuit and conscious act are identified: system-wide learning doesn't establish one conscious subject, and describing my motives doesn't delimit that system.[^3][^4]

Watts questions the terms on which I have begun. Her account of Place-Thought, articulated through Haudenosaunee and Anishinaabe cosmological histories, begins from land as alive and thinking. It resists separating an inert setting from the human activity that gives it meaning. Nonhuman agency exceeds causal influence on a human observer.[^5] Applied to the same bamboo sequence, her challenge asks why the plants should enter the account principally through what they contribute to my purposes. Even expanding the circle of beneficiaries can leave that authority untouched.

Her challenge carries ontological, historical, and political commitments that my first-person record cannot independently establish. I also cannot translate Place-Thought into passive synthesis or ecological feedback without losing the disagreement. Watts therefore changes what the comparison must answer: whether my method makes human intentional experience the measure of relationships whose reality exceeds it. I can acknowledge that limit without claiming that the walnut sit verifies her situated account.

The positive contribution I can presently defend concerns the relation between intentional specification and independent fulfillment. Moving the dome and caring for the walnut can describe the same movement while setting different standards of success. The move can be completed while care fails. Conversely, evidence that relocation is unnecessary could change the means while preserving care as the end. The living beneficiary matters to what the act is for, not only to a later judgment about whether I behaved well. Husserl and Bateson provide resources for explaining this; their adequacy remains a question for the argument.

Ecological intentionality brings these relations into a sustained account of how purposes form, become specific, and remain open to what their objects and beneficiaries require. Its claim of formation would weaken where I could trace no contribution from the encountered relation to the purpose's target, beneficiary, means, or possible future. Establishing a distinct explanation would require showing what the strongest existing accounts leave inadequately distinguished. Its account of answerability fails wherever I count every reversal as correction and every retained decision as confirmation. The next test is what contrary evidence or another participant's reason can actually change, including the terms of a future decision.

My verdict is therefore affirmative and qualified. The sequence supports an account of ecologically specified directedness, including specification during action and the possible formation of shared purposes. It does not yet require a new basic intentional category or establish that the strongest predecessors cannot explain it. I can pursue that contribution while keeping formation, answerability, and ecological adequacy distinct. Another life can help give my purpose its shape without sharing that purpose, and its continuing reality can exceed even a purpose formed in care.

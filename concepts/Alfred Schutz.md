Alfred Schutz helps me describe how a practical world becomes differently relevant as plans and circumstances change. His work has a particular role in the inquiry into [ecological intentionality](Ecological%20Intentionality.md): it gives a substantial existing explanation of changed attention and action, against which the ecological proposal must become more precise.

## Conditions, interests and relevance

In “The Well-Informed Citizen,” Schutz describes conditions that support planning and execution while remaining largely taken for granted. Interests belong to changing systems and can conflict even within one person's life. He uses the roles of father, citizen and church member to make that difference concrete. Regions of relevance overlap rather than remain neatly separated.[^schutz]

His distinction between intrinsic and imposed relevances helps me separate what follows from a chosen interest from what circumstances or other people make consequential. These are analytic distinctions that often mix in life. Shared interests do not absorb all of each participant's purposes, and another person's concern can affect me without becoming mine.

The November 19 meal case gives this account something definite to explain. An altered arrangement preserved food provision while leaving the conversation I valued unavailable. My ecological question concerns how different material and living relations specify what is intended and what would fulfill it. The fact that a disruption changes my plans is already intelligible through Schutz. It cannot alone establish an additional constitutive claim.

## Learning through other people's accounts

The outage also makes me ask why I trust one account rather than another. Schutz distinguishes knowledge received through others from the additional weight an account gains through social approval. His eyewitness, insider, analyst and commentator describe different relations between access, interests and explanation. They are mixed ideal types, not a ladder of trustworthy people.[^schutz19knowledge]

I can report what came from my own faucet. A utility notice speaks from a different position, while an explanation of the failure requires evidence I may not possess. Acceptance within a group can make a claim feel settled without supplying that evidence. A claim can also fit my existing concerns and still exceed what I know.

This gives my public record a practical discipline: identify what each source can support and leave the causal claim proportionate to it. Official attribution deserves attribution, not an assumption of infallibility. The distinction helps me investigate a shared world without pretending to occupy every position within it.

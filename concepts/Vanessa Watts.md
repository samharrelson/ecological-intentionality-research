Vanessa Watts enters these Field Notes through Place-Thought and her account of agency among human and nonhuman worlds. Her work challenges an architecture in which a human subject encounters nonhuman objects while land remains inert setting or contextual background.

Her role here is not to confirm a pre-existing theory of Ecological Intentionality. Watts develops Place-Thought through specifically Haudenosaunee and Anishnaabe cosmological framings and an Indigenous intellectual and political argument concerning land, agency, colonialism, and the separation of epistemology from ontology. She explicitly refuses to reduce nonhuman agency to causal relationships and locates its recognition within stories/histories. She also speaks of the land’s “intentions” and “land’s intentionality,” language whose overlap with phenomenological intentionality must not be mistaken for conceptual identity. The field notes must allow her argument to challenge their starting terms without translating it immediately into European phenomenology or extracting “relationality” as a portable abstraction.

## Works in this project

- “Indigenous Place-Thought & Agency Amongst Humans and Non-Humans (First Woman and Sky Woman Go on a European World Tour!)”: Place-Thought, land as alive and thinking, human and nonhuman agency, stories / histories, colonial disruption, and the critique of reducing nonhuman agency to causal efficacy.

I turn to Franz Brentano to keep *intentionality* connected to the different ways mental acts are directed toward objects. I distinguish kinds of act and ask how an act is also conscious of itself. His account of secondary consciousness gives that last question a different form from a further act of inner observation.

Brentano helps me ask what changes when *ecological* modifies intentionality. In the field practice I distinguish perceiving, remembering, judging, anticipating and valuing before asking whether their ecological configuration requires a new term. Deliberate practical purposes belong within this wider directed life. Biological responsiveness and agency raise further questions of their own.

**November 21: redirected intentional life and the limits of the genealogical claim.** In [Already Underway](../field-notes/2026-11-21%20-%20Already%20Underway.md), a living twig made a detail in a familiar representation stand out. The experience helps me ask how intentional life can receive direction and change through what it encounters. In *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint*, Book II, chap. I, §5, Brentano distinguishes reference to an object in presentations, judgments and affective acts. This already exceeds voluntary practical choice. The case therefore asks how the beneficiary's independently developing life enters an intention's content and practical fulfillment, rather than assuming that being redirected is absent from the genealogy. I also need to distinguish Brentano's original object-reference from later accounts of worldly influence and ecological feedback. An image can redirect attention; the walnut's development gives organism-specific conditions to what I call care.

I bring Andrew Trevor Kirkpatrick into this inquiry through his 2020 dissertation, *Whitehead and Merleau-Ponty: Toward a Process Phenomenology*. His continuity argument follows relations among body, habit and world from Merleau-Ponty's earlier work into flesh. It gives my account of received and redirected intentional life a substantial predecessor to reconstruct.

His discussion of the cane helps me ask how an artifact can participate in forming an embodied ability. His broader process interpretation also retains difference through style, divergence and the distinction between subjectivity and consciousness. I need to examine how he warrants the move from embodied capacities to a wider account of nature. The known image and living twig in my fieldwork provide different contributions to compare within that argument.

## Whitehead through this comparison

I am approaching Whitehead here through Kirkpatrick's reconstruction. That mediation belongs to the citation and to the scope of the claim. The source check covers selected arguments in the 2020 dissertation; it does not establish a new direct reading of Whitehead or the later book edition.

In [Learning, Flesh, and Continuing Lives](../essays/learning-flesh-and-continuing-lives.md), I reconstruct that continuity argument beside [What Flesh Explains](../essays/what-flesh-explains.md). Early Merleau-Ponty's tacit cogito already depends on expression; Kirkpatrick reads the later self-criticism chiefly as an expressive difficulty within a continuing method. The cane discussion also attributes latent intentional potentiality before incorporation while preserving the difference between possible and actual bodily acquisition. I examine both commitments, their strongest replies and the questions that survive the comparison.

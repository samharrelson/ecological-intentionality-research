**I'm developing ecological intentionality as a positive account of how individual and shared conscious directedness is received, formed, sustained and transformed through ecological relationships. The world isn't only what consciousness is directed toward. Its independent lives and processes may participate in the direction of consciousness itself, without becoming exhausted by what I intend.** Other beings need not share our intentions to participate in shaping our possibilities of intending, yet their own activities, histories, and goods must not be assimilated to ours. This remains a candidate account of ecological *constitution*, not an established universal field of consciousness, an attribution of human intention to trees, or a synonym for ethical attention.

**November 9: an act with a direction and a tested practical possibility.** In [today's public sit draft](../field-notes/2026-11-09%20-%20Directed%20Through%20This%20World.md), I report trying the climbing dome before the recording and naming the possible good of the walnut as part of an intended relocation. That's not just greater ecological awareness: I can distinguish the **end I intend** from what I now think I **can do**, from **whether I do it**, and from **whether it actually helps the walnut**. An existing phenomenology of action and motivation, including Husserl's *Ideas II* §60(a), already describes the practical “I can” and the world's motivating character. The ecological question I'm developing concerns whether the particular independently living beneficiary and its possible good participate in specifying what this act is *for*. That's an affirmative theory to argue, not a finding that Husserl missed an obvious act.

**Intimacy with remainder:** returning and learning make the relationship more particular, but the walnut continues beyond its appearance and my intended uses. Looking at bark can make that difference concrete: its surface is visible, its internal processes are not being observed as they occur, and its life remains other than my representation. Remainder means more than ignorance that research could clear up; it also names the other life that is never consumed by the act of intending it. This doesn't prove plant phenomenal experience, even though questions about nonhuman directedness belong to the wider metaphysical research.

**Communal horizon:** the related question is not only how *I* intend differently, but how ecological relations might participate in what a congregation becomes capable of **consciously intending together**, beyond a new environmental project appended to fixed institutional ends. That requires a real case in communal life and distinct ecological and theological inquiry.

Earlier phases of this inquiry emphasized a disciplined form of creaturely attention: attending to beings as relationally given, irreducibly other, and never exhausted by their appearance within human experience. That phenomenological and methodological achievement remains, but it no longer determines the ceiling of the constructive theory. The major unresolved question is whether the ecological relations are *constituents* of conscious activity, not only its causes, objects, incentives, conditions of successful care, or occasions for learning. [The October 24 sit](../field-notes/2026-10-24%20-%20A%20Purpose%20the%20Tree%20Does%20Not%20Share.md) gives that distinction one concrete intention to test. Husserl, Ruyer, Bateson, Ingold, Watts, Harris, and others are interlocutors and independent challenges, not a preselected synthesis.

**Extended argument:** The new [Essays](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/essays) section gathers working philosophical proposals separately from daily sits and concept notes. Its first essay, [*Whose Good?*](../essays/whose-good.md), sets out the beneficiary-sensitive account of practical purpose, the strongest rival explanations, and the remaining burden of showing whether another being's activity actually constitutes an intention. It develops, without claiming to have proved, the earlier [co-constitution proposal](../essays/consciousness-in-a-world-we-cannot-possess.md).

**November 7 constructive pressure, in my own terms:** In [Whose Good Governs the Act](../field-notes/2026-11-07%20-%20Whose%20Good%20Governs%20the%20Act.md) I no longer describe the previous mowing plan as just tidying; I had also hoped to benefit the soil and maintain safe access. Several goods already governed the projected action. After reading about whole leaf litter, a different beneficiary may have taken priority, but the act still needs an account of its practical motivation and eventual consequences.

I also say something more affirmative than “ecological care is important”: **this actual living walnut has changed what I intend in a way I don't think a LEGO counterpart would.** Its life, vulnerability and independent course of development are not authored by my perception. I propose *non-substitutable living answerability* as a candidate feature of ecological intentionality, while asking whether that amounts to a new structure of **conscious activity** or instead to a relation whose referent and success norm cannot be replaced by a mere artifact. A photograph can mediate care for the living tree. The medium's nature, the referent's nature, and the constitution of my intending cannot be collapsed. The strongest Husserlian account may already explain a change of plan, valuation and learning. I must identify the additional act-level contribution, rather than dismissing motivation as inadequate because the organism is alive. See [Husserl's November 7 reconstruction](Husserl.md).

The ivy and violets beneath the walnut make this proposal ethically harder in a useful way. **One perceptual scene is not one biological good.** The observed vine, app-assisted species identification, possible competition, native/introduced status and a management recommendation have different evidential sources. I can recognize an invasive plant as living without announcing that its spread benefits the walnut or that any plant is morally blameworthy. An actual conflict among these particular plants remains unverified.

**November 6 constructive test: what governs this act?** In [When Another Life Changes the End](../field-notes/2026-11-06%20-%20When%20Another%20Life%20Changes%20the%20End.md), I report having considered mowing the leaves again to tidy the yard but, after reading about intact leaf litter and the lives it can shelter, intending to leave them whole. That decision was made **before the day's sit**, in which squirrels dislodged leaves and I noticed fresh material lying over older brown litter. My October 28 field notes already expressed concern for insects, birds and soil life. The new question is therefore not whether I suddenly acquired ecological values. It is whether those values became the **operative purpose** of this particular decision where tidiness had previously governed the proposed mowing. A continuing commitment and a newly prioritized immediate end can coexist. The first-person closing claim supports that possibility, but a plan reversal and reported purpose change are not yet proof that another organism is a constituent of my conscious act. The strongest alternatives include Husserl's motivated practical activity and existing agency theories of plans, reasons and standing commitments. The tree's independent good can correct what qualifies as care even if its organismic processes remain outside my experience. The distinction between **what I mean to do**, **why I actually do it**, **who would benefit**, and **what happens afterward** is now the critical test.

**November 5 constructive refinement:** The yellow ground and unexpectedly open canopy prompted a causal question: how did this pattern of falling leaves develop? But even a correct explanation would not determine whether I should leave, move, or mulch them, or whose good that decision should serve. The [November 5 sit](../field-notes/2026-11-05%20-%20The%20Yellow%20Sea%20and%20the%20Ends%20of%20Care.md) distinguishes an **explanatory inquiry**, a **beneficiary-sensitive investigation** of available actions, and a **normative deliberation** over plural goods. **Care may make inquiry the next practical end, and inquiry may then revise how the original purpose is specified.** This is a genuinely constructive structure to test, but Stein's physician and gardener, Husserl's motivated practical world, and phenomenologies of agency already provide strong alternatives. An adequately specified end is not yet evidence that another living being constitutes the present conscious act. A proposed congregational course may later provide a separate institutional test, but only if it is actually undertaken and documented with appropriate ecological, historical, and human warrants.

**November 4 constructive step:** In [Rain and Unfinished Purposes](../field-notes/2026-11-04%20-%20Rain%20and%20Unfinished%20Purposes.md), I didn't change the litter practice or determine what benefits the plants. I made a more particular commitment: investigate the litter and the surrounding lives before deciding what care requires. **Inquiry can itself be a mediating practical purpose**, guided by independently living beneficiaries, without research success being identical to their flourishing. [Stein's](Edith%20Stein.md) distinction between knowing a possible good and actually realizing it (*Finite and Eternal Being* V §16) already explains an important part of this sequence. I still need to establish whether ecological intentionality contributes anything beyond those resources and whether organismic activity participates in realizing an act of consciousness.

**November 3 affirmative refinement:** In [A Purpose Answerable to Life](../field-notes/2026-11-03%20-%20A%20Purpose%20Answerable%20to%20Life.md), I distinguish the bodily performance, the intended beneficiary, and the evidence needed for the performance to count as adequate. A handmade artificial object can rightly matter through its maker or shared history without possessing a biological good of its own. A living tree's actual condition, by contrast, may correct what I thought my action would do *for that life*. My developing account of **ecological specification and corrigibility** asks how several independently organized lives help give particular practical purposes their content, limits, and conditions for reconsideration. This is a positive account under construction, not yet proof of a new act-constituting structure. [Husserl's](Husserl.md) *Ideas II* §50 is a strong existing comparator because the personal surrounding world already includes values, uses, and made works.

**November 2 constructive specification:** [What Care Becomes](../field-notes/2026-11-02%20-%20What%20Care%20Becomes.md) takes up the question of whether a familiar practice, leaving fallen foliage beneath the walnut, actually benefits this tree. It keeps a proposed act of care open to biological correction. [Hal King's](Hal%20King.md) reading of Stein also shows that empathy with plants already encompasses investigation and intervention; the argument I now need to build concerns **plural living goods shaping which practical end becomes determinate, how it is pursued, and when it is revised**. That proposal remains distinct from proof that another life constitutes an individual act of consciousness.

**November working formulation:** “Ecological intentionality may concern how the purposes of consciousness become determinate through responsiveness to several independently living beings, whose distinct lives make different actions, delays, and revisions appropriate. Their participation need not involve shared intention, and their actual good remains capable of correcting my understanding of care.” This names the positive argument I'm beginning to construct, not a conclusion that I've identified a wholly new faculty of consciousness. I want to explain the structure of purposive response, not only describe an admirable attentional habit.

**November 1 positive construction:** In [After the Swing](../field-notes/2026-11-01%20-%20After%20the%20Swing.md), a practical purpose becomes more determinate across several living relationships: family play, care for a spider that led me to wait, care for the walnut that later supported removal, and renewed inquiry after seeing bark indentations. I am developing ecological intentionality as the **specification, enactment, and revisability of intentional ends through encounters with differently organized and non-co-intending lives**. This is a positive process to explain, more than an instruction to pay attention. I must still distinguish replacing an end from specifying it or changing its means and timing; the fact that I cared does not demonstrate that the tree benefited or that a novel conscious structure has been established.

**October 25 constructive test:** [The Branch and the Purpose](../field-notes/2026-10-25%20-%20The%20Branch%20and%20the%20Purpose.md) returns to an intention not yet fulfilled. The swing's use brings human memories and purposes into the encounter; the tree has its own developmental conditions, which I have not diagnosed. Ruyer's account of externally organized tools and living organs sharpens what must be explained: **reliance on another living form is not yet proof that the other's life constitutes my intending**. The positive theory has to specify that constitutive relation or revise its ambition. The methodological alternatives traced below remain live objections from earlier stages, not the predetermined endpoint of the inquiry.

**October 29 continuation:** In [What the Leaves Change](../field-notes/2026-10-29%20-%20What%20the%20Leaves%20Change.md), I distinguished a surprise that captured my gaze, a deliberate decision not to touch nearby leaves, my change in mowing practice, and my report that I now approach people and places differently. They may arise through different processes. Bateson's ecological circuit and memory, Husserl's motivated attention, and Merleau-Ponty's learned bodily orientation already explain considerable reorganization without adding a new category. The stronger question is whether this walnut's *independent continuing life* helps realize a particular intention in a way those explanations cannot accommodate. A falling leaf and a cautious gesture don't decide that question. [Bateson](Gregory%20Bateson.md) now has his own source page for this comparison.

These field notes function as one of the places where the developing proposal is tested rather than treated only as an illustration. In the earlier September–October methodological phase, an important question was whether ecological intentionality named anything phenomenologically new or instead a disciplined coordination of already available intentional structures. That possibility remains a serious **rival explanation**, but the present positive philosophical proposal asks more broadly whether independently organized lives can participate in the realization of intending without sharing its purposes. It is not restricted to what phenomenology permits us to describe. The Sept. 25–27 sits add a stronger constraint: ecological relation must preserve **integrity without isolation; relation without dissolution; good without guaranteed harmony**. Relations may condition which possibilities a living being can actualize without thereby constituting its norm, and strong collective coordination must not silently be redescribed as one organism. **Do not let ecology borrow the unity of the organism without argument.** Sept. 28 widens the test: if the term survives, it may need to coordinate phenomenological clarification with biological, historical, legal, institutional, political, metaphysical, and ethical inquiry. Husserl prevents phenomenology from being reduced to bare sensory description; the open problem is whether ecological intentionality names a disciplined coordination among methods with different evidential tasks rather than a new intentional structure. Sept. 29 adds a living-boundary discipline: **conditioning is not incorporation, exchange is not flourishing, and permeability is not indistinction.** Oct. 2 adds Harris’s praxis test while Merleau-Ponty’s discussion within *Phenomenology of Perception* blocks an easy contrast between embodiment and history. His social world and conditioned freedom already make embodied existence historical; Harris presses further by asking whose experience and memory guide ecological knowing, how unequal access and harm are analyzed, and how inquiry moves toward environmental justice and praxis. Oct. 3 makes the terminological pressure explicit: the emerging method coordinates perception, memory, biology, historical evidence, community knowledge, ethical judgment, and praxis. That may be a disciplined ecological orientation rather than a new intentional structure; the name should remain revisable. Oct. 4 shifts the goal from perceiving more connection to differentiating kinds of relation, participants, scales, vulnerabilities, and goods. A post-sit return to Merleau-Ponty makes the pressure sharper: flesh already preserves noncoincidence and rejects a larger-organism model, so the remaining problem is not only whether belonging preserves difference but whether it can distinguish creaturely goods. **Belonging is not beneficence, and a shared world is not yet one shared good.** Oct. 5 adds another evidential ladder: a shared condition does not establish identical local exposure; differently situated exposure does not establish patterned vulnerability; patterned vulnerability still requires historical and structural explanation before an injustice claim is warranted; and warranted diagnosis is not yet accountable response. Oct. 6 turns that pattern into a more explicit hypothesis: ecological intentionality may be less a new structure than a **discipline of transitions** among perception, memory, anticipation, biological development, metaphysical living form, historical explanation, and stronger ontological or normative claims. The term earns its keep only if it coordinates these transitions without mistaking one level for the next. A related possibility now emerges from [Remainder](Remainder.md): **transition can move something from one kind of remainder to another**. What is hidden can become visible, what is visible can become remembered, and what becomes perceptually available can still leave biological, historical, experiential, or normative questions unresolved. Oct. 7 gives the hypothesis a concrete field test: changing position fulfilled one hidden branch profile while leaving physiological process and historical explanation untouched. Ecological intentionality may therefore train not the overcoming of remainder but the ability to recognize **how remainder changes status as an encounter develops**, what warrant was gained, and which inquiry must follow. The October 7 source pass raises the burden again: Husserl already explains bodily profile-fulfillment, and a May 1960 Merleau-Ponty working note already distinguishes several kinds of invisibility. If the term earns its keep, it will have to do more than rename either achievement. October 8 adds an identity constraint: **similarity is not identity, and serial observation is not an established individual history.** It also separates bodily capacity from ethical warrant. A disciplined ecological inquiry has to know not only when a relation changes, but what evidence establishes the change and what further claim still does not follow.

October 13 adds a redundancy test for the methodological proposal itself. [Bryan Smyth](Bryan%20Smyth.md) already reads early Merleau-Ponty as a practical and unfinished transcendental project. Distinguishing kinds of warrant is therefore insufficient: the inquiry must show how one claim can be corrected by another kind of evidence, and whether that correction changes a description, a practice, or the framework's terms.

## November 8: formation, mediation and what actually follows from the evidence

This morning's [sit](../field-notes/2026-11-08%20-%20What%20This%20Life%20Makes%20Possible.md) strengthens the positive proposal in a bounded way. I report actually **retaining fallen leaves** rather than only proposing it, while the possible **relocation of a climbing dome** near a visible root remains unperformed. The relation with the walnut, ecological reading, previous commitments, memory and the actual uses of the yard all enter the practical history. An organism's contribution need not be unique to its species or unmediated to matter, but I cannot infer a sole cause or a new conscious act-type from that history.

The [public Issue #14 process-tracing matrix](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14#november-8-2026-bounded-longitudinal-evidence-matrix-first-published-process-trace) documents the sequence across settings and marks that bounded evidence criterion complete. The next philosophical question concerns the **work each explanation performs**: Husserlian formation of personal habituality, Bateson's learning across contexts, and Watts's challenge to the priority of a perceiving human subject are not interchangeable. The account must also distinguish a living other's independent organization, its practical good, the representation through which it is encountered, and the intentional form of a human act. There is no guarantee that all those differences require one new category.

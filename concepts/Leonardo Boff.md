I bring Leonardo Boff into the [November 23 sit](../field-notes/2026-11-23%20-%20What%20We%20Learn%20to%20See.md) through *Cry of the Earth, Cry of the Poor*. His cosmic Christology gives the cross and resurrection a definite role in my question about creaturely participation. Incarnation places Christ within material history; the passion presses an account of development to attend to particular victims; resurrection concerns the Crucified One and those whose lives a story of general progress might leave behind.

## The creature within cosmic fulfillment

Chapter 9, “The Cosmic Christ,” moves from the historical Jesus and cosmic incarnation to the passion of the world and resurrection. Boff's claim about the rescue of all beings, including the past, is a substantive account of final fulfillment. I need to examine what warrants that claim and how the history of each creature remains significant within it.

This brings Boff into conversation with [Niels Henrik Gregersen](Niels%20Henrik%20Gregersen.md). Gregersen describes a close congruence between deep incarnation and universal salvation while denying that the first logically entails the second. His account of participation according to the recipient's mode also keeps different creaturely lives in view. I want to hold those demands together when I ask what resurrection promises for creatures.

Boff's language of plenitude and cosmic communication leaves a further question for my argument: how does a particular finite history belong within the fulfillment he describes? I want the account to preserve the creature's life and losses while articulating what transformation means.

## My constructive use

I propose that participation in the crucified and risen Christ can form communities whose purposes are answerable to the lives they receive and affect. The cross brings power, injury and the distribution of burdens into that proposal. Resurrection grounds hope beyond what a community achieves through its own work.

This is my constructive extension of the theological sources. The walnut's appearance and my enjoyment of teaching supply no proof of redemption or successful communal formation. They give me occasions to ask what my purposes are for, whose lives enter them and how their fulfillment could be judged.

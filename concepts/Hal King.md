# Hal King

Hal King enters this inquiry through his article [“Propagating Plant Empathy with Edith Stein”](https://doi.org/10.3197/whppp.63876246815941), published in *Plant Perspectives* on October 5, 2026. I'm bringing him into the public record because he makes an important philosophical move that my own account must take seriously: recognizing the vitality of a plant is already more complicated than seeing a neutral object, and vegetal empathy can develop through *practices* of gardening, propagation, and tending to specific conditions.

King reconstructs and extends **Edith Stein's** account of plant life. He examines perceptual empathy, the appearance of spontaneous movement and organic development, and intuitive empathy, which is informed by but not reducible to imagination. In his discussion of a spider plant's propagation, the growing cutting exposes vulnerability over time. He also considers situated encounters with water, soil, sunlight, wilting, and flourishing. These are practical encounters, not a bare inference that a plant must be alive.

The decisive challenge he poses to my own proposal is that **Stein already gives the gardener a practical role**. In *On the Problem of Empathy*, the gardener apprehends a plant's condition, investigates its causes, and seeks ways to influence it. King develops that resource. I therefore can't claim a new philosophical structure merely because attention becomes responsible action. A strong account of plant empathy may already describe much of that transition.

**My constructive question remains:** how do the different goods of *several* independently living beings help specify the content and timing of a human purpose, including waiting, acting, and revising an earlier judgment? In the [November 2 black walnut sit](../field-notes/2026-11-02%20-%20What%20Care%20Becomes.md), I returned to the swing removal, the earlier spider's web, the bark marks, and the assumption that leaving fallen foliage near the tree is beneficial. These are concrete invitations to work out a positive account of practical ends, not demonstrations that King or Stein are insufficient.

**What this source does not establish:** plant experience transparently accessible to human empathy; injury caused by the swing straps; whether a particular gardening action helps the walnut; or a novel fundamental structure of consciousness. King and Stein are resources for constructing a good explanation, not obstacles whose contributions need to be minimized.

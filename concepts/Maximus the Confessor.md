I bring Maximus into the inquiry through a question about mediation: how can participation join creatures without making them instruments of my purpose?

In *Ambiguum* 41, he describes a human vocation within five divisions of reality and its fulfillment in Christ. This supplies a theological resource for the developing Ecology of the Cross. It also resists an easy translation into ecological language. An ordered ascent, the overcoming of certain divisions and the distinction of created from uncreated have to be read on their own terms.

My proposed application is that a community participating in Christ might let its purposes become answerable to fellow creatures through inquiry, restraint and repair. I still have to develop that argument. In §§6 and 9, Maximus describes Christ gathering all things in himself, which makes me ask how participation in that mediation could form human purposes without authorizing human control. The comparison with [Vanessa Watts](Vanessa%20Watts.md) sharpens the question of a human mediator's authority while keeping their accounts distinct.

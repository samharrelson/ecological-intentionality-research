Raymond Ruyer enters the larger project through living form, self-survey, finality, and the question of whether organismic unity requires a metaphysical account stronger than phenomenological constitution alone.

The October 22 sit gives him a direct role: tracing a tree's visible branching structure differs from arguing for its own unity or self-survey. His account rejects a supplementary internal observer and advances a substantive claim about primary consciousness. It cannot be established by the appearance of a branch end. Husserl's and Stein's resources remain strong comparators; what Ruyer adds still has to be argued.

## Works in this project

- *Neofinalism*, trans. Alyosha Edlebi (2016): organismic form, self-survey, finality, and value. Chapter 9 (especially pp. 94, 99–101) advances a claim about active organization and primary consciousness that a tree photograph cannot verify. Chapter 5 (pp. 36–37) distinguishes living organs from manufactured tools that can nonetheless be organized by form. That helps sharpen the swing-strap comparison: human-made organization is not identical to membership in this walnut's living body. Source status: selected indexed passages in the English text, not yet page-image collation or complete endorsement of Ruyer's theory.

I read Melanie L. Harris through her account of ecowomanism, Black women’s lived experience, eco-memory, environmental justice, intersectional analysis, earth-honoring faith, transformation and praxis.

Her work changes the questions I bring to ecological inquiry. I need to ask whose lived experience, memory and traditions guide ecological knowing, how race, gender, property, labor, political power and environmental harm shape access and vulnerability, and how inquiry moves toward justice and praxis. I read her account on its own terms. Merleau-Ponty's attention to history and social situation makes that comparison more demanding.

## Works in this project

- *Ecowomanism: African American Women and Earth-Honoring Faiths*: ecowomanist method, eco-memory, earth-honoring faith, environmental justice, and praxis.
- “Ecowomanism: An Introduction”: Black women’s lived experience, womanist intersectional analysis, ecological justice, agency, and earth community.

## From the sits

- [November 25, 2026: Room to Continue](../field-notes/2026-11-25%20-%20Room%20to%20Continue.md): *Ecowomanism*, ch. 2, 41–46, helps me question whose account could revise a favorable interpretation of conservation at Mariandale. I distinguish a community limiting its own authority from one placing costs on people who cannot refuse them. I develop those questions in [A Commitment with a Future](../essays/a-commitment-with-a-future.md). Accounts from people seeking homes or maintaining the place could change my judgment of whose good the arrangement serves.

- [November 24, 2026: Following This Life](../field-notes/2026-11-24%20-%20Following%20This%20Life.md): I bring *Ecowomanism*, ch. 2, 37–38 and 41–46, to the question of who can define, revise or decline a communal project. Her Black ecowomanist account of history, agency and power requires more than inviting people to supply labor. The church-ground proposal still needs ecological and institutional evidence.

- [November 22, 2026: The Life I Return To](../field-notes/2026-11-22%20-%20The%20Life%20I%20Return%20To.md): I bring Harris's environmental-justice method to the proposed garden's starting questions. Whose knowledge guides it, who can revise it, who provides the labor and who bears an unwanted cost? *Ecowomanism* (Orbis, 2017), 19–24, 44–46 and 57–58, grounds the methodological comparison. The garden remains a proposal, and I need to let the answers change its design.

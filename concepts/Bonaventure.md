I bring Bonaventure into this inquiry through *The Soul's Journey into God*. His attention to God through and in creatures belongs within an account of creation, contemplation and a passage enabled by Christ. Prayer, grace and love shape that itinerary. I read a creature's relation to God as preceding the use I make of it.

I also need to preserve a difficulty within the comparison. The culminating passage moves beyond sensible objects and intellectual activity. I propose that contemplation can form participants who return to ecological and communal responsibilities, and need to explain how that return relates to his itinerary. The cross has an active place in the transformation I am interpreting; the ecological application requires its own argument.

**Source and status:** *The Soul's Journey into God*, trans. Ewert Cousins, in *Bonaventure: The Soul's Journey into God, The Tree of Life, The Life of St. Francis* (Paulist Press, 1978), I §§10–14, 63–67; II §1, 69, and §§11–13, 75–78; prologue §§3–4, 55–56; VII §§1–6, 110–15.

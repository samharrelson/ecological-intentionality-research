description: Notes on attention as an ecological and phenomenological practice.
---
By attention, I mean more than concentration. I want to allow a creature or place to appear with increasing determinacy without assuming that greater clarity means greater possession. These recent sits also remind me that my attention should become more corrigible, not merely more confident: seeing more or correcting an interpretation does not by itself tell me how to care well. The Sept. 28 power-line sit adds an epistemic limit with a Husserlian correction: practical-cultural sense can itself be genuinely given, but the contingent history, law, institutions, and politics through which a place acquired its present form cannot be recovered by looking harder alone. The Oct. 2 sit sharpens the handoff: attention can disclose the need for historical inquiry without disclosing the history itself, and a better-formed perceiver is not yet accountable praxis. Oct. 3 makes the evidential restraint concrete at the walnut’s trunk: a visible knot or wound can warrant inquiry without identifying its own cause. **Visible trace is not self-explanatory history.** Oct. 5 applies the same restraint to differentiated leaflets: color, orientation, holes, marks, light, and shadow can be directly available while their developmental and causal histories remain open. **Difference is given before its explanation is.** Oct. 7 makes attention transitional: moving can fulfill one hidden profile while leaving biological process or historical explanation untouched. Attentiveness should therefore name what changed status, what warrant was gained, and which mode of inquiry must come next. Oct. 8 adds a continuity check: repeated attention does not establish that two similar appearances belong to the same individual object. **Similarity is not identity.** Attention must be willing to lose the elegant story when the evidence cannot carry it.

**November 6: an encounter cannot retroactively cause its own preparation.** Sunshine replaced the expected cloud cover; the moving squirrels and a falling walnut leaf redirected attention; a falling acorn interrupted a theological thought. One leaf's witnessed motion offers a different warrant from the inferred histories of the ground's many other leaves. The decision to leave whole leaf litter, however, had been made **before the sit**, through ecological reading. In [the November 6 record](../field-notes/2026-11-06%20-%20When%20Another%20Life%20Changes%20the%20End.md), sustained attention, remembered foliage, research-based hypotheses and a changed intended action can be connected without assigning them the same cause. Attention can set a question and make a concern salient without itself proving ecological benefit or the constitution of the next act.

**November 5 transition:** Coming around a familiar corner, I encountered an unexpectedly open canopy and a yellow-covered ground. This was not just an instance of distraction. It brought an established perceptual expectation into contact with a changed scene, which in turn prompted new causal hypotheses and a more specific possible next act: investigate what leaving or moving the litter would do. The distinction must remain explicit: a perceptual surprise is not a verified explanation, and an explanation is not a norm for care. A droplet visible on a leaf can recall scientific understanding of surface forces and plant physiology without showing internal water transport or the tree's experience. These are different things knowledge contributes to the act of looking. [November 5 sit](../field-notes/2026-11-05%20-%20The%20Yellow%20Sea%20and%20the%20Ends%20of%20Care.md).

**November 7: looking down changes the questions, not the biological facts by itself.** I approached the walnut under blue sky, noticed the yellow and darkened leaf layers, then turned deliberately to the ivy, violets and lilyturf around the trunk. Repeated looking led me to find different plants and to ask about light, soil, spread and neighboring lives. But app-assisted naming, reported reference-book comparisons, current perceptual appearance and actual ecological effects each have their own warrants. The previous mowing plan's full purpose is also harder to reconstruct than a tidy-yard story; soil and human access mattered alongside appearance. Attention can surface a competing good and lead to further inquiry. It doesn't manufacture a plant's biological interest or show that a proposed act will help it. [November 7 sit](../field-notes/2026-11-07%20-%20Whose%20Good%20Governs%20the%20Act.md).

## November 8: attention develops through the relation

In [*What This Life Makes Possible*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-08), attention is not only a practice I intentionally direct toward the walnut. Its *future saliences* appear to have developed through earlier encounters, remembered change, reading and concern. I meant to inspect a branch and found myself attending to the groundcover instead. That shift is an observed reorientation within a scripted, learned practice, **not** proof that the tree by itself caused it. The positive problem concerns the history through which some features can now present themselves as practical questions, while Husserlian habituality and Batesonian contextual learning remain strong explanatory rivals.

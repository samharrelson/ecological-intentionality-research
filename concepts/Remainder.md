---
description: Notes on what remains inaccessible within ecological encounter.
---
Remainder names what exceeds any particular act of perception, description, empathy, or interpretation. It is not merely a failure to know. Increasingly, these notes ask whether remainder may be one of the ways genuine otherness becomes positively disclosed.

Remainder also needs to be differentiated by kind. Some absence is horizonal and can become present through another perceptual profile. Some is temporal or epistemic and can be approached through memory, repeated encounter, inference, or scientific knowledge. A stronger remainder would belong to another center in a way that cannot be overcome by accumulating more perspectives or information. The September 23 sit adds an ethical question: whether remainder also requires **care without immunity from correction**: response that remains revisable without pretending that uncertainty itself guarantees adequacy. By September 25, remainder also names something normative: individual, specific-form, and place-wide goods may not line up neatly, and relation itself does not guarantee harmony. September 28 adds a possible **historical and structural remainder**: a power line, fence, or scar can be culturally meaningful within experience while the policy, property relation, decision, or inherited harm shaping it requires another mode of inquiry. September 29 adds physiological processes that are unavailable to unaided perception at this scale but scientifically investigable. October 2 distinguishes personal recollection from a place’s past and from community memory: histories outside one perceiver’s memory may be recoverable through records or held in practices and stories to which that perceiver lacks immediate access. October 3 locates one historical remainder within present form: a trunk can bear consequences of growth and encounter without making their determinate causes transparent. **The past is present enough to matter and absent enough to require inquiry.** October 4 adds a normative remainder within belonging itself: lives can inhabit one field and real relations while their exposures, experiences, and goods remain differentiated. **Belonging need not imply coincidence, beneficence, or one harmonized good.** October 5 asks whether remainder can name a positive differentiation internal to relation rather than only an absence or withheld access. On that proposal, intimacy occurs partly through noncoincidence. October 6 adds a temporal form: sustained familiarity can make remembered absence and anticipated change more determinate without making the whole life present at once. **Familiarity may temporalize remainder rather than eliminate it.** A further hypothesis follows: **transition can move something from one kind of remainder to another.** A branch hidden by foliage can become perceptually present after leaf fall; the canopy that was present can become remembered; an anticipated event can become actual; a visible trace can remain historically unexplained. October 7 tests that hypothesis through bodily movement. A concealed branch segment became visible while underground process and place history remained unavailable by the same movement. The result is more exact than “mystery remains”: **a transition can resolve one indeterminacy while leaving another line of inquiry untouched or making a new question salient. The remainder has a history.** Ecological intentionality may therefore train not the overcoming of remainder but recognition of **how remainder changes status as an encounter develops**. Husserl’s horizonality and temporal synthesis remain the strongest control on whether this names anything more than a useful description of changing modes of givenness. The October 7 source pass adds another challenge from Merleau-Ponty: in a May 1960 working note, he already distinguishes hidden or inactual aspects that could become visible from other dimensions of invisibility and warns against placing them under one undifferentiated category. The question for remainder is therefore no longer only whether absence comes in different kinds, but whether sustained encounter reveals something philosophically useful about **how those kinds change status over time**. These distinctions must not flatten different kinds of absence. Institutional history may be recoverable through archives, biological process through experiment, and another subject’s experience, where experience is warranted, remains non-primordial in a different and stronger sense. **Methodological unavailability ≠ irreducible alterity; personal memory ≠ the measure of a place’s past.**

A theological question now opens from this dynamic account, but it remains an extension rather than a conclusion of the field notes. The October 7 sit gives it a cleaner working formulation: **redemption does not erase remainder; it transforms the relation in which remainder remains.** October 8 immediately adds a necessary limit: **transformation of relation is not yet redemption.** Relations change in ordinary, destructive, and morally indifferent ways too, so the theological sources must specify what makes a transformation redemptive. The corresponding restraint remains essential: **transformation of remainder does not retrospectively justify the harm that produced a wound.**

## Current work: Comps II

The second comprehensive examination now develops two distinct affirmative tasks while keeping rival explanations in view: what late Merleau-Ponty's ontology of flesh positively explains beyond Husserl and early Merleau-Ponty, and what ecological intentionality might explain about the formation, realization, and correction of purposes within relations with other living beings. Creaturely difference, participation, place, history, and accountability matter to both, but none settles their originality in advance. November's constructive work starts by stating a positive mechanism and its implications before turning to the strongest competing account.

These sources test whether cruciformity belongs only to the formation of a human perceiver or whether incarnation and participation reach into materiality, biological vulnerability, evolutionary suffering, and creaturely becoming.

### Place, memory, and structural accountability

- [Harris, Melanie L.](../concepts/Melanie%20Harris.md) *Ecowomanism: African American Women and Earth-Honoring Faiths*. Maryknoll, NY: Orbis Books, 2017.
- [Harris, Melanie L.](../concepts/Melanie%20Harris.md) “Ecowomanism: An Introduction.” *Worldviews: Global Religions, Culture, and Ecology* 20, no. 1 (2016): 5–14.
- Manigault-Bryant, LeRhonda S. *Talking to the Dead: Religion, Music, and Lived Memory among Gullah/Geechee Women*. Durham, NC: Duke University Press, 2014.

These texts matter because perceptual attention cannot by itself establish the histories of race, gender, land, dispossession, memory, and structural power that make a place what it is. Merleau-Ponty’s own account of social and historical situation makes the comparison more demanding: Harris and Manigault-Bryant are not here to add “history” to an otherwise ahistorical phenomenology, but to press questions of whose experience and memory guide ecological knowing, what evidential practices establish particular histories, and how inquiry moves toward accountability and praxis.

## Adjacent conversations

These readings are not all part of the formal Comps II contract. They are included because they are actively challenging, extending, or clarifying the public concepts that emerge from the sits. The selections below are **reading priorities and possible objections**, not a claim that I've completed every text or that its argument has already survived close examination.

### November 2026: building the affirmative account

As November begins, I'm moving from identifying conceptual possibilities to writing a positive account of **how the purposes of consciousness become more determinate in relations with several independently living beings**. The swing, spider's web, and walnut leaf litter raise different questions about an adopted end, delaying action, and allowing biological knowledge to revise what I take good care to require. No single observation proves a novel form of consciousness.

**Current readings that directly shape the construction:**

- [Edmund Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md), *Ideas Pertaining to a Pure Phenomenology and to a Phenomenological Philosophy, Second Book: Studies in the Phenomenology of Constitution*, translated by Richard Rojcewicz and André Schuwer (Kluwer, 1989), especially **§50**, on the person in a surrounding world of value, use, products, and works. My November 3 artificial-tree comparison takes this as a strong existing account of why made things have meaning beyond their material properties. A selected primary passage has been checked in the connected research collection; verify the printed page before quoting it in a paper.
- [Edith Stein](../concepts/Edith%20Stein.md), *On the Problem of Empathy*, III.5(i), with particular attention to the gardener who apprehends a plant's condition, investigates its causes, and acts upon that understanding. The 1989 ICS Publications edition used in my connected research collection has relevant pages 68–71; other editions may differ.
- [Hal King](../concepts/Hal%20King.md), “[Propagating Plant Empathy with Edith Stein](https://doi.org/10.3197/whppp.63876246815941)” (*Plant Perspectives*, 2026). King extends Stein's resources through plant propagation and situated care; recognizing a plant and moving toward intervention cannot automatically be my claimed philosophical innovation. Selected sections have been checked; the full critical reading remains open.
- John J. Drummond, **“Acting, Choosing, and Deliberating,”** in Christopher Erhard and Tobias Keiling, eds., [*The Routledge Handbook of Phenomenology of Agency*](https://www.routledge.com/link/link/p/book/9781315104249) (Routledge, **2021**), **376–387** (chapter 25). The title order, 2021 copyright and page range match the checked printed contents and connected Zotero copy. A selected search excerpt is not a completed reading; the chapter still requires close study.
- [Gregory Bateson](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md), “Form, Substance, and Difference,” on memory and circuits extending beyond the human body. This is a substantial resource for understanding situated learning, not a shortcut to shared phenomenal consciousness.

**November 7: the living tree, the model, and why a stronger affirmative argument still owes a comparison.** The [November 7 field record]([2026-11-07 - Whose Good Governs the Act](../field-notes/2026-11-07%20-%20Whose%20Good%20Governs%20the%20Act.md)) now identifies an actual perceptual/practical act: looking at the ivy climbing the walnut while considering whether to refrain from intervening, investigate further, or eventually manage it. The field record also preserves the researcher's affirmative claim that **a living walnut shapes his intentions in a way a known artificial counterpart would not**. But the content and object of an intention, the life history of its referent, the motive for it, and the biological criteria of successful care should not be collapsed. A photograph of an actual living tree may mediate an intention to care for that life even though the picture does not grow.

**Local biological source correction:** Clemson Extension, [“Ivy” (2024)](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/ivy/) and [“English Ivy Control”](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/english-ivy-control/) explain why English ivy is treated as invasive in South Carolina, not why this particular vine has caused harm; Clemson also treats [Virginia creeper as a native vine](https://hgic.clemson.edu/weed-of-the-month-virginia-creeper/) and describes [how to distinguish eastern poison ivy](https://hgic.clemson.edu/factsheet/how-to-identify-and-control-poison-ivy-toxicodendron-radicans/), another native. **November 6: operative purposes and the limits of the Husserl/Stein contrast.** In [the November 6 field case](../field-notes/2026-11-06%20-%20When%20Another%20Life%20Changes%20the%20End.md), a previously contemplated mowing for tidiness was displaced by a commitment to leave whole walnut leaves for overwintering habitat. The broad commitment to ecological care was not itself newly invented. These additional sources directly challenge the thesis that changing an immediate purpose requires a new kind of intentional act:

- Roberta De Monticelli, **“The Phenomenology of Rational Agency,”** in Christopher Erhard and Tobias Keiling, eds., *The Routledge Handbook of Phenomenology of Agency* (Abingdon, Oxon: Routledge, **2021**), **362–375**, chapter 24. The selected connected-Zotero excerpts examine commitments, plans, reason, values, enduring policies, and the agent's practical responsibility. They support an existing account of how a continuing ecological commitment could govern a newly revised plan. **Indexed primary chapter excerpts and printed contents checked; full chapter not read contiguously.**
- Michael Barber, **“Action in the Phenomenology of Alfred Schütz,”** same volume, **121–133**, chapter 10. Selected excerpts distinguish imagined projects from concrete actions and an action's *in-order-to* motive. They ask what future completed action I originally imagined and how I subsequently described the revised one. **Indexed source excerpts and printed contents checked, not a completed study of Schütz's own writings.**
- James Jardine, *Empathy, Embodiment, and the Person: Husserlian Investigations of Social Experience and the Self* (Cham: Springer, **2022**), *Phaenomenologica* 233, **p. 180 n. 25** (working locator; [book information](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-84463-9)). Jardine cautions that significant parts of the familiar *Ideas II* §56 motivation treatment may owe editorial elaboration to **Edith Stein**, while extensive motivation research remains Husserl's own. This complicates any easy genealogy in which Stein first recognizes a lack in a static, disembodied Husserl. **Selected original indexed footnote, not yet image-collated with Hua IV/V or checked as complete manuscript history.**
- The classical primary comparator remains Husserl, *Ideas II* **§§55–56**, in the 1989 Rojcewicz/Schuwer English edition: motivated attention, valuing, active reaction and goal-directed action are already part of his account. The selected passages are available in Zotero, but a full rereading and exact English-page collation are still needed.

**Ecological reading behind the plan, not a site-specific outcome:** [Clemson Extension on retaining fall leaves](https://hgic.clemson.edu/this-month-in-your-garden-november-2025/), [Xerces Society on undisturbed leaves as winter habitat](https://xerces.org/blog/leave-the-leaves), and [Iowa State Extension on walnut-material use](https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/faq/can-i-use-woodchips-walnut-mulch-my-landscape) give general reasons for leaving intact litter in a naturalized place. Penn State Extension still urges caution around some walnut-sensitive species. Research-informed plans and measured biological benefits must remain distinct.

**November 4 primary-text refinement:** Stein's *Finite and Eternal Being: An Attempt at an Ascent to the Meaning of Being*, translated by Kurt F. **November 5: sources that can defeat an easy novelty claim.** Peirce's “Evolutionary Love” (*The Monist* 3 [1893]: 176–200; selected pp. 176–178 and 187–188 checked in the indexed primary text and [archived Arisbe transcription](https://web.archive.org/web/20070520131053/http://www.cspeirce.com/menu/library/bycsp/evolove/evolove.htm)) makes independent becoming and harmony a substantive tension. Chapter 11, “Commons Commitments: Ecological Ethics,” especially pp. 218–219. Hart reads Luke's Good Samaritan as a summons to **become a neighbor**, and explicitly extends the ethical discussion toward earth and biotic communities. Philippians 2, Colossians 1, Romans 8, and 1 Corinthians 15 are distinct biblical warrants requiring close exegesis, not proofs supplied by the walnut.

### Newly important comparisons (October 2026)

These sources sharpen two separate burdens: what late flesh adds beyond Husserl and early Merleau-Ponty, and what ecological intentionality contributes beyond already developed ecological philosophy and methods. I want to read them for their strongest objections, not as additional authorities agreeing with a theory I've already settled.

**Distributed mind, living form, and intentional life**

- [Bateson, Gregory](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md). *Steps to an Ecology of Mind: Collected Essays in Anthropology, Psychiatry, Evolution, and Epistemology*. 1972. Reprint, Northvale, NJ: Jason Aronson, 1987. I read “Form, Substance, and Difference” on October 28 and am testing its tree–person–axe circuit against my October 29 sit. Printed **pp. 465–467** in this reprint are especially important for explanatory boundaries, memory, and the way mental pathways extend beyond the skin. My open question is how a participant's own living organization and possible good figure in a particular conscious intention. I can't claim relationally constituted mind as a novelty when Bateson already presents this powerful account. Different editions have different pagination; the selected excerpts are not a complete quotation audit.

**Plural worlds and process phenomenology (new October 30 sources)**

- James, Simon P. “On the Plurality of Nonhuman Worlds.” *The Monist* (published October 27, 2026), advance article, 1–10. https://doi.org/10.1093/monist/onag029. I'm reading this as a challenge to equating the existence of an organism with the possession of an experiential world. James distinguishes phenomenal consciousness from a cohesive experiential world and does not settle plant consciousness or infer moral value from diversity alone. His argument does not tell me what my walnut or our visiting squirrel experiences.
- Kirkpatrick, Andrew Trevor. *Whitehead and Merleau-Ponty: Toward a Process Phenomenology*. PhD diss., Deakin University, 2020. I have the 2020 thesis in Zotero and have checked its abstract and contents, including the proposed early/late continuity and process reading of flesh. A distinct book of the same title appeared from SUNY Press in October 2026. The two versions should not have their page numbers conflated. I need to read Kirkpatrick's continuity arguments and objections before treating his position as established.

**Husserl, Stein, and living forms**

- Jardine, James. *Empathy, Embodiment, and the Person: Husserlian Investigations of Social Experience and the Self*. Cham: Springer, 2022. https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-84463-9. Chapters 3–5 are a priority: they reconstruct Husserl's accounts of empathy, nature, and animate embodiment. **Test:** Does Husserl already supply distinctions I have been tempted to attribute uniquely to Stein or ecological intentionality?
- [King, Hal](../concepts/Hal%20King.md). “[Propagating Plant Empathy with Edith Stein](https://doi.org/10.3197/whppp.63876246815941).” *Plant Perspectives* (published online October 5, 2026). King's analysis of the plant as living body, spontaneous movement, intuitive empathy, and the gardener's work with soil, water, sunlight, and changing plant conditions already extends into practical care. It poses an especially substantial prior-work challenge to any claim that my philosophy alone can move from recognizing vegetal life to intervention. My closer comparison is still in progress, and none of this licenses an attribution of awake consciousness to the walnut.
- Pustarfi, Laura. “An Ecophenomenological Dendrochronology: Shlain's *Dendrofemonology* through a Lens of Stein's Temporality.” *Cogent Arts & Humanities* 13, no. 1 (2026): article 2697670. https://doi.org/10.1080/23311983.2026.2697670. Her reading of Tiffany Shlain's artwork joins Stein, feminist historical memory, tree rings, and arboreal temporality. **Question:** What does a tree's material history disclose, and what happens when artistic interpretation and lived temporality are distinguished from an unwitnessed individual history? The botanical record isn't identical to the history represented in an artwork.

**Methods, more-than-human ethics, and accountability**

- Kaell, Hillary. “A Multibeing Manifesto.” *Journal of the American Academy of Religion* 93, no. 3 (2025): 351–371. https://doi.org/10.1093/jaarel/lfaf070. Her attention to *Spartina*, coastal fieldwork, and multiple human and nonhuman knowers makes this a particularly important methodological comparison. **Test:** What does sustained philosophical sitting omit unless the inquiry follows the being into ecological, social, and historical relationships?
- Wren, Heather. “Reconceptualising Empathy for an Ethical Approach to Environmental Decision-Making.” *Environmental Values* (published online September 28, 2026). https://doi.org/10.1177/09632719261492961. A developing account of relational, embodied environmental empathy. Its proposed link to ethical responsiveness may already anticipate parts of what I'm calling a discipline of ecological attention. That argument must still explain how attunement relates to warranted decisions rather than assuming care follows directly from feeling.
- Nawrath, Maximilian, Eléonore Maitre-Ekern, Bård Hobæk, and Isabel Seifert-Dähnn. “An Interdisciplinary Perspective on the Interpretation of Legal-Scientific Concepts in Rights-of-Nature Initiatives.” *People and Nature* (2026). https://doi.org/10.1002/pan3.70436. This tests the passage from relational description to legal and institutional responsibilities. **Question:** How do ecological knowledge, contested concepts, and legal recognition interact without treating ecosystems as organisms or assuming personhood settles questions of care?

**Symbols and theology: a distinct, later question**

- Marder, Michael, and Luis Garagalza. “The Vegetality of the Symbol.” *Religions* 17, no. 10 (2026): article 1163. https://doi.org/10.3390/rel17101163. Their proposal that symbolic life has a vegetal dimension is a worthwhile hermeneutical counterpoint. The use of seed, meristem, and organic development as philosophical figures does not establish what this particular walnut experiences or what any ecological loss means theologically.
- Johnson, Elizabeth A. *Creation and the Cross: The Mercy of God for a Planet in Peril*. Maryknoll, NY: Orbis Books, 2018. [Publisher](https://orbisbooks.com/products/creation-and-the-cross-paperback). A major theological prior-work challenge, to be read alongside Gregersen rather than after the theological case is already decided. **Question:** What does the proposed Ecology of the Cross contribute beyond Johnson's account of cosmic redemption, creaturely value, and divine mercy? Her criticism of satisfaction atonement also cannot be presumed to settle the distinct Luther–Stein question.

These works have different evidential jobs. In particular, **vegetal empathy is not plant consciousness, symbolic interpretation is not a botanical diagnosis, and ethical sensitivity is not yet a justified decision**. The newer reading should make the distinctions more demanding, not supply a shortcut through them.

### Place, agency, reciprocity

- [Watts, Vanessa](../concepts/Vanessa%20Watts.md). “Indigenous Place-Thought & Agency Amongst Humans and Non-Humans (First Woman and Sky Woman Go on a European World Tour!).” *Decolonization: Indigeneity, Education & Society* 2, no. 1 (2013): 20–34.
- [Kimmerer, Robin Wall](../concepts/Robin%20Wall%20Kimmerer.md). *Braiding Sweetgrass: Indigenous Wisdom, Scientific Knowledge and the Teachings of Plants*. Minneapolis: Milkweed Editions, 2013.

Watts and Kimmerer are important to [Place and Agency](../concepts/Place%20and%20Agency.md), but they should not be treated as Indigenous confirmations of a pre-existing European phenomenology. Their work presses the project to ask whether relation begins from a human perceiver at all, and what obligations follow when place and nonhuman agency are taken seriously on their own terms.

### Genealogies of intentionality, habit, and self-presence

- [Brentano, Franz](../concepts/Franz%20Brentano.md). *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint*. Translated by Antos C. Rancurello, D. B. Terrell, and Linda L. McAlister. London: Routledge, 1973/1995.
- Ravaisson, Félix. *Of Habit*. Translated by Clare Carlisle and Mark Sinclair. London: Continuum, 2008.

[Brentano](../concepts/Franz%20Brentano.md) and Ravaisson help clarify two genealogies running behind the project: the modern account of intentionality and inner awareness, and the Aristotelian line of activity, habit, inclination, and living form that runs toward Bergson.

### Merleau-Ponty: reversibility, difference, and time

- Davis, Duane H., and William S. Hamrick, eds. *Merleau-Ponty and the Art of Perception*. Albany: State University of New York Press, 2016.
- [Kaushik, Rajiv](../concepts/Rajiv%20Kaushik.md). *Merleau-Ponty between Philosophy and Symbolism: The Matrixed Ontology*. Albany: State University of New York Press, 2019. Kaushik's diacritical reading of *écart* became an explicit hypothesis in the October 5 walnut sit. His claim that divergence is constitutive needs to be tested against Merleau-Ponty's own discussions of resemblance and against Husserl's explanatory resources.
- Morris, David, and Kym Maclaren, eds. *Time, Memory, Institution: Merleau-Ponty’s New Ontology of Self*. Athens: Ohio University Press, 2015.
- [Smyth, Bryan](../concepts/Bryan%20Smyth.md). “Merleau-Ponty and the ‘Naturalization’ of Phenomenology.” *Philosophy Today* 54, SPEP Supplement (2010): 153–162. His reading of early Merleau-Ponty has already become a concrete challenge in the October 13 sit: a methodological appeal to embodiment and practice may not distinguish ecological intentionality after all.

These works are currently serving as secondary controls rather than substitutes for the primary texts. Davis and Hamrick help trace chiasmatic reversibility back through Husserl’s touching / touched analysis; Kaushik presses the stronger reading of *écart* as constitutive divergence; Morris and Maclaren foreground the continuity from early temporality and transition synthesis toward the later ontology. Each claim remains answerable to Merleau-Ponty’s own texts.

### Place, field, and comparative philosophy

- Nishida Kitarō. *An Inquiry into the Good*.
- Loughnane, Adam. Work on Merleau-Ponty and Nishida, especially the relation among *basho*, motor-perceptual faith, expression, and place.

Nishida’s *basho* offers a distinct comparative route for thinking about a field in which subject and object arise relationally. It should not be collapsed into either Merleau-Ponty’s flesh or Watts’s Place-Thought.

### Continuity, love, and development

- Peirce, Charles S. “Evolutionary Love.” *The Monist* 3 (1893): 176–200.

Peirce’s synechism and agapism are being explored as a possible cosmological conversation partner for relation, continuity, novelty, and differentiation. He remains a pressure point rather than a structural anchor.

## A note on use

The field notes are not meant to become miniature literature reviews. Most sit pages therefore carry only the concepts that genuinely emerged from the encounter. This page holds the wider scholarly conversation in one place so that the public archive can remain close to observation while still making its intellectual debts visible.

As the project develops, sources will move between these sections, new ones will appear, and some may fall away. The bibliography is therefore part of the research process rather than a final statement of canon.

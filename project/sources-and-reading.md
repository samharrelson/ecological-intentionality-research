## Current work: Comps II

The second comprehensive examination now develops two distinct affirmative tasks while keeping rival explanations in view: what late Merleau-Ponty's ontology of flesh positively explains beyond Husserl and early Merleau-Ponty, and what ecological intentionality might explain about the formation, realization, and correction of purposes within relations with other living beings. Creaturely difference, participation, place, history, and accountability matter to both, but none settles their originality in advance. November's constructive work starts by stating a positive mechanism and its implications before turning to the strongest competing account.

## Adjacent conversations

These readings are not all part of the formal Comps II contract. They are included because they are actively challenging, extending, or clarifying the public concepts that emerge from the sits. The selections below are **reading priorities and possible objections**, not a claim that I've completed every text or that its argument has already survived close examination.

### November 2026: building the affirmative account

As November begins, I'm moving from identifying conceptual possibilities to writing a positive account of **how the purposes of consciousness become more determinate in relations with several independently living beings**. The swing, spider's web, and walnut leaf litter raise different questions about an adopted end, delaying action, and allowing biological knowledge to revise what I take good care to require. No single observation proves a novel form of consciousness.

**November 26: receiving direction, remembering and enjoying shared life.** In [What Returning Carries](../field-notes/2026-11-26%20-%20What%20Returning%20Carries.md), I follow how acquired distinctions and a recovered journal entry enter present inquiry. The theological reflection joins received agency with the worth of shared existence.

- Franz Brentano, *Psychology from an Empirical Standpoint*, English ed. Linda L. McAlister, trans. Antos C. Rancurello, D. B. Terrell and Linda L. McAlister (Routledge, 1995; electronic edition, 2009), Book II, chap. I, §5: directedness through presentations, judgments and affective acts.
- Edmund Husserl, *Analyses Concerning Passive and Active Synthesis*, trans. Anthony J. Steinbock (Kluwer, 2001), §21, 130–133, especially the familiar melody at 131; §49, 275–276: anticipation, enjoyment, willing, familiarity and affection.
- Niels Henrik Gregersen, “The Cross of Christ in an Evolutionary World,” *Dialog* 40, no. 3 (2001): 192–207, §I, “How Illusory are Human Capacities?”: his reading of received grace and participation in Christ enabling love and justice.
- Jürgen Moltmann, *God in Creation*, trans. Margaret Kohl (Fortress Press, 1993), XI, 285–286; compare 287: grateful existence, enjoyment beyond achievement and rest shared with those who bear the costs of work.

**November 24: fuller understanding and different fulfillments.** In [Following This Life](../field-notes/2026-11-24%20-%20Following%20This%20Life.md) and [What Fuller Understanding Requires](../essays/what-fuller-understanding-requires.md), I distinguish recognizing a leaflet, understanding another's experience, explaining an event and realizing a benefit. - **Stein:** *On the Problem of Empathy*, trans. Waltraut Stein, 3rd rev. ed. (ICS Publications, 1989), II §2(a), 6–7; II §2(c), 10–11; III §5(i), 68–71. The three modalities occur on printed p. 10. **November 23: learning, continuity and creaturely fulfillment.** In [What We Learn to See](../field-notes/2026-11-23%20-%20What%20We%20Learn%20to%20See.md) and [Learning, Flesh, and Continuing Lives](../essays/learning-flesh-and-continuing-lives.md), I reconstruct the strongest continuity account and give the theological proposal a focused comparison.

- **[Boff, Leonardo](../concepts/Leonardo%20Boff.md).** *Cry of the Earth, Cry of the Poor*. Translated by Phillip Berryman. Maryknoll, NY: Orbis Books, 1997. Chapter 9, “The Cosmic Christ,” especially “The Passion of the World and the Cosmic Christ” and “Resurrection: Revolutionizing Evolution,” connects the crucified and risen Christ with particular victims and the promised rescue of all beings.

**November 18: shared preparation and what it serves.** In [What We Are Trying to Do](../field-notes/2026-11-18%20-%20What%20We%20Are%20Trying%20to%20Do.md), I'm asking how an undertaking begins to take shape through different contributions. ## A note on use

The field notes are not meant to become miniature literature reviews. Most sit pages therefore carry only the concepts that genuinely emerged from the encounter.

# Issue navigation and metadata

*Established November 12, 2026 · Applies to research issues #1–14*

The [research issues](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues) preserve eight ongoing workstreams and six dated, qualified findings. Their metadata helps readers locate a kind of record or a subject across the inquiry. The [research board](https://github.com/users/samharrelson/projects/1/views/4) carries workflow status; the issue bodies carry arguments, evidence, limits and progress criteria.

## Label vocabulary

Each research issue has exactly one `kind:` label and usually one or two `topic:` labels. A topic identifies a main subject of the issue; it does not name every thinker or concept mentioned in the body. A qualified finding retains its stated scope and remaining tests.

| Label | Description |
| --- | --- |
| `kind: workstream` | An ongoing research question with explicit progress criteria; not a settled conclusion. |
| `kind: qualified finding` | A dated, bounded public result or correction; remaining limits and tests stay explicit. |
| `topic: intentionality` | Formation, constitution, motivation, and individual or communal conscious directedness. |
| `topic: phenomenology` | Perception, embodiment, time, empathy, and what experience can or cannot disclose. |
| `topic: creaturely difference` | Living form, individuality, alterity, and the plurality of creaturely goods. |
| `topic: theology` | Ecology of the Cross: scriptural, Christological, doctrinal, and communal warrants. |
| `topic: research method` | Observation, inference, source verification, historical inquiry, and tests of claims. |
| `topic: public scholarship` | Public curation, revision history, provenance, privacy, and scholarly reuse. |
| `topic: care and reciprocity` | Gift, permission, reciprocity, practical care, and evidence of benefit or harm. |

The two record types have distinct green and gold colors; topic labels share a muted green. Label names and descriptions carry the meaning, so color is not required to understand them.

Use the [labels directory](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/labels) to open a filtered issue list. The prefixes keep the two questions separate: **What kind of record is this? What is it principally about?** A `kind: workstream` label continues to describe an inquiry when its board status moves from Investigating to Evidence review.

## November 30 argument checkpoint

The [November 30 argument checkpoint](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/milestone/1) is due **November 30, 2026**. It groups **#1–5 and #14** for a review and synthesis of the Comps 2 argument. Each workstream should yield a bounded account of:

- what the argument can presently claim;
- the evidence supporting that claim;
- the strongest surviving objection or alternative explanation;
- the next unresolved test.

The checkpoint concerns the state of the argument at that date. An ongoing research question can remain open after its review. GitHub's milestone completion percentage counts closed issues; the warrant for a philosophical claim remains in the argument and its evidence.

The separate methodological workstreams **#6–7** and historical findings **#8–13** have no newly assigned deadline. Their documented evidence periods and remaining tests continue to govern what they can establish.

## How to use the other sidebar fields

| Field | Use in this research record |
| --- | --- |
| Assignee | Identifies responsibility for maintaining the issue. Sam currently maintains all fourteen. |
| Project status | Shows where the record is in the review workflow. Use the existing board statuses rather than adding duplicate status labels. |
| Milestone | Groups a specified review or writing deliverable with a meaningful date. Specify its scope in the milestone description. |
| Native issue type and additional fields | Optional. Add a field when it supplies information the labels, board status or issue body do not already provide. The `kind:` labels carry the research-record distinction. |
| Relationships | Use parent/sub-issue or blocking relationships for an actual division of work or prerequisite. Use the existing issue cross-links for evidence and conceptual connections that run in several directions. |
| Development | Link implementation work when an issue actually requires a code change or pull request. |

The affirmative inquiry in #14, the phenomenological comparisons in #1–2, and the theological argument in #5 retain distinct burdens. Their relations should not be represented as a requirement that one theory be defeated before the constructive work can proceed.

## November 13 workflow clarification

The project status **Evidence review** marks a working account and its evidence as ready for scrutiny. It does not close the research question. On November 13, #14 moved from Investigating to Evidence review after its public account and longitudinal record were extended. Its remaining comparative criterion stays open.

The project's **Auto-close issue** workflow had been configured to close an issue when it entered Evidence review. That workflow is now disabled. The transition briefly closed #14; it was reopened, and its final state is open in Evidence review. All fourteen research issues remain open. Closure should be a deliberate decision based on the issue's stated scope, distinct from a workflow-stage change.

## Maintenance

Keep this vocabulary small. Add a label when it supports a useful recurring filter. Keep bibliographic leads, remaining evidence requirements and dated revisions in the issue body, where their qualifications can be stated.

The generic software labels remain available for repository maintenance. The research labels replace `documentation` and `enhancement` on #5; they do not reclassify the theological argument as a software feature.

When the metadata scheme changes, update this guide alongside the relevant labels or assignments. The [research roadmap](../RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md) remains the overview of substantive work, and [How the Inquiry Developed](how-the-inquiry-developed.md) remains the intellectual history.

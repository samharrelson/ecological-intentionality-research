*Updated November 23, 2026 · Black Walnut Field Notes · An argument in progress*

I've been returning regularly to a black walnut in Spartanburg, South Carolina, keeping dated recordings, observations, photographs and research notes. The practice began with a phenomenological question about how another living being appears. It has opened a further question about **how the world participates in the directedness of individual and communal consciousness**. I want to explain how independent lives and material processes shape what I notice, imagine, judge and undertake, and how their continuing conditions bear on the fulfillment or correction of a purpose.

That is the affirmative starting point of [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md). I follow the formation of capacities, their exercise in a particular undertaking and the independent lives whose conditions help determine its success. The changing walnut, remembered encounters, reading, biological research and prior commitments enter that history. The stronger claim about the ongoing constitution of a conscious act requires a further argument. [The positive formation question is now public Issue #14](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14).

The [Essays section](../essays/README.md) carries the working philosophical argument. This page states where the inquiry stands. For dated developments, earlier claims, setbacks and revisions, see [How the Inquiry Developed](how-the-inquiry-developed.md) and the individual Field Notes.

## The affirmative question

**Intentional life is directed toward the world and can receive direction through encounters with it. Its purposes may be formed and redirected through the independent lives and processes of other beings, while those beings continue beyond what I know, intend or control.**

That is the affirmative working proposal. Ecological intentionality concerns conscious directedness **toward and through** ecological relationships. It includes reception: I don't author every possibility or limit that becomes significant in a practical act. It includes asymmetrical reciprocity and **intimacy with remainder**: real relations may form purposes while other lives continue beyond what I know, intend or control. Historical learning is one aspect, but the question also concerns **present intentional activity, anticipated futures, and genuinely shared communal purposes**. The proposal does not require one fused consciousness in a community or a humanlike co-intender in a tree.

The issue is whether a living other's independent activity and possible good can participate in specifying a particular intended end, rather than only supply a new object of concern or an after-the-fact standard for success. That is a philosophical hypothesis I am constructing and testing, not a demonstrated novel act-type.

The black walnut has a life I did not author. Its development and possible goods are not reducible to the meanings I form about it. My account must nevertheless distinguish its independently existing life from my beliefs, memories, representations, learned habits and intentions. A picture of a living walnut can mediate a relationship to that actual life; an artificial model can also reorganize expectations. The contrast between living and artificial things is consequential, but it cannot by itself establish a new intentional structure.

Husserl's genetic and motivated intentionality, Edith Stein's accounts of living forms and others, Merleau-Ponty's late flesh, Bateson's ecological learning and other ecological theories are serious resources and rivals. I develop the positive account through these resources and test what each explanation can warrant. The [research roadmap](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md) records open tests rather than predetermined victories.

## The two phenomenological burdens

The October 21 examination outline asks two related questions that remain distinct. **What does late Merleau-Ponty's flesh explain beyond Husserl and early Merleau-Ponty?** And **what, if anything, does ecological intentionality explain beyond those accounts?** Both remain valuable tests, but now serve the **positive constructive question about conscious directedness received through relationships with independently active lives and material processes**, rather than making rivalry the entire argument.

Late flesh may change the ontological starting point of perception, emphasizing differentiation within a sensible field. That doesn't follow just from a hand touching bark, and it doesn't show that the walnut shares my experience. The separate ecological proposal must account for particular organisms, possible conflicting goods, biological inquiry, historical conditions, and the constitution or transformation of actual purposes. See [Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md), [Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md), and [the rival-theory issues #1–4](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md).

The two November 17 working essays give these comparisons a sustained form. In [What Flesh Explains](../essays/what-flesh-explains.md), I defend late flesh's account of sensible belonging and internal differentiation while facing the resources already available in Husserl and early Merleau-Ponty. In [How a Purpose Takes Shape](../essays/how-a-purpose-takes-shape.md), I hold the field sequence constant while comparing motivated intentional acts, ecological circuits and Watts's challenge to the starting terms. My present verdict supports an account of ecologically specified purposes; it does not yet require a new basic intentional category.

In [A Life of Its Own](../essays/a-life-of-its-own.md), my November 19 working essay, I develop the creaturely difference this account requires. Stein already describes individual living form and distinguishes knowledge of a possible good from its realization. Ruyer's account of primary consciousness makes a further metaphysical claim. I can argue that independent living goods help specify beneficiaries, means and possible fulfillment without treating that further claim as established. A documented plant interaction supplies a concrete comparison; the effects of the vine on my own walnut remain an open question.

## What the record supports

In [the November 23 sit](../field-notes/2026-11-23%20-%20What%20We%20Learn%20to%20See.md), I return from teaching and distinguish three contributions to an inquiry: a magnifying glass alters access, learned distinctions change the recognition of fallen leaf stalks, and a remembered question about falling leaves changes what I explain next. The episode gives the specification claim a concrete conversational sequence. My authority over the lesson and the children's lasting learning remain separate questions.

The companion [Learning, Flesh, and Continuing Lives](../essays/learning-flesh-and-continuing-lives.md) reconstructs Kirkpatrick's continuity account step by step. Early Merleau-Ponty's tacit cogito already depends on expression, while Kirkpatrick reads the later self-criticism chiefly through that continuing difficulty. His process interpretation also preserves difference while extending sensorial capacity across natural becoming. I examine those commitments and their strongest replies. The remaining questions concern the depth of the revision and the warrant for wider subjectivity; the comparison gives received direction and incomplete self-access their full place in the predecessor account.

In [the November 22 sit](../field-notes/2026-11-22%20-%20The%20Life%20I%20Return%20To.md), I distinguish three sources of change: reading alters my recognition of fallen leaf stalks, rain changes the bark I touch, and remembered ivy length prompts a particular inquiry. I identify the vine in the field as English ivy and want to investigate its relation to the walnut. The first practical result is a more definite question about care. The apparent extension remains a remembered comparison.

In [Seeing a Life Develop](../essays/seeing-a-life-develop.md), I hold that inquiry steady across Husserl and Stein. Husserl already explains returning inquiry, temporal horizons and the difference between confirming an observation and realizing a value. Stein's gardener already connects apprehending life with investigating causes and seeking a response. I build from those resources to explain how a living relationship forms a capacity, helps specify an undertaking and contributes independent conditions of benefit.

The next test can identify what evidence changes: the beneficiary I include, the end I seek, the means I choose or my grounds for continuing restraint. Keeping those possibilities distinct makes the account answerable to a case while allowing a well-grounded purpose to continue. My comparison reaches a bounded verdict about the inherited explanations; the wider constitutive claim remains open.

In the November 21 sit, [Already Underway](../field-notes/2026-11-21%20-%20Already%20Underway.md), I inspect an attached twig and identify a couple of structures as buds. Seeing them draws my attention to a matching detail in a familiar representation, which in turn helps me think about the tree's future. I know the image is an image. It can redirect my attention while having no developmental life like the walnut's. The case makes a positive claim more demanding: intentional life can receive direction, yet being redirected alone does not establish a new intentional structure or a two-sided conscious purpose shared with the tree.

The source comparison matters. [Brentano](../concepts/Franz%20Brentano.md) distinguishes reference to objects in modes of presentation, judgment and affect. [Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md) already describes affection before attentive turning and the persistence of habit; [Bateson](../concepts/Gregory%20Bateson.md) describes contextual learning and wider feedback. I am constructing an account that follows another living being through the formation of a capacity, the specification or revision of a practical purpose, and the independent conditions in which its possible good could be realized. A photograph or tattoo may mediate that relation. The walnut's own development helps determine what care could accomplish, without proving that the organism itself constitutes my act of consciousness.

The bud identification is my field report, open to further close documentation. Neither that identification nor spring leaves alone can establish whether the dome relocation benefited the root. This is the boundary between a corrected observation, a completed movement, a good actually realized and evidence that the good occurred. My final statement for November 30 will need to defend those connections while keeping the stronger constitutive claim open.

In the November 20 sit, [The Lives That Form Us](../field-notes/2026-11-20%20-%20The%20Lives%20That%20Form%20Us.md), I name a capacity formed through repeated encounter: reconsidering an arrangement through a life affected by it. The exposed root recalls the dome move already reported on November 11. Moving beside the trunk gives me a different perceptual episode, including a lighter bark feature whose history I still don't know. The remembered action and the present observation contribute differently to my account.

I distinguish acquiring a capacity from exercising it toward this particular life. Some habits of inquiry can travel to another place; an informed judgment about this root still needs knowledge of its conditions. Relationships can form selective or damaging habits as well as attentive ones. My proposal concerns how directedness develops, while the adequacy of care requires further evidence. Husserl's account of acquired faculties and Bateson's learning supply substantial explanations that help me state the remaining question: what does the independently continuing life contribute to the present judgment or act? The [sit's research discussion and source notes](../field-notes/2026-11-20%20-%20The%20Lives%20That%20Form%20Us.md#research-connection) develop the comparison.

The earlier November 19 sit, [What Holds a Purpose Together](../field-notes/2026-11-19%20-%20What%20Holds%20a%20Purpose%20Together.md), follows an undertaking through changed conditions. During the water disruption, the church provided dinner for pickup while the ordinary gathering could not happen. Food was available, but the conversations I value around the tables remained unavailable. One purpose could be carried through without fulfilling everything I sought in the evening. A water delivery likewise gave a continuing commitment a particular task.

I also report that the leaf inquiry became a brief shared observation and a notebook entry on November 18. We had prepared the activity; now we have begun it. We did not discover new creatures, and I cannot infer their absence from that short session. The next question is how each participant can shape the questions and method, and what our observation establishes about the place.

These cases sharpen three questions within the positive account: what enables an undertaking, what gives it direction, and what would fulfill it? Material conditions, understood reasons and another organism's continuing life contribute differently, sometimes in more than one way. A shared activity can begin before its authority or ecological adequacy is settled. A retained end can become more definite without being replaced.

The November 17 case, [Considered and Not Followed](../field-notes/2026-11-17%20-%20Considered%20and%20Not%20Followed.md), gives a reason a consequence even though it did not prevail in the immediate decision. On Tuesday I reported that we researched bamboo's uses together on Monday evening. The alternative valuation prompted further inquiry and changed my regard. Sunday's cutting remained completed, and I had not adopted a new cultivation plan. I can now distinguish answering an objection from a later development through which it continues to matter.

The preceding [Who Gets to Say We](../field-notes/2026-11-16%20-%20Who%20Gets%20to%20Say%20We.md) records a different development. During Sunday's cutting, protecting neighboring plants became part of what I was trying to do. The removal end continued, while care acquired a particular object and practical bearing. The encounter can help specify an intention during its performance, as well as through a later return. The [November 15 morning case](../field-notes/2026-11-15%20-%20What%20Can%20Reach%20a%20Habit.md), in which I explicitly kept both the means and end of a plan still to be performed, remains accurate.

The conversation before and after cutting also produced, in my account, an expectation that we would include one another next time. The later research shows a consequence of another valuation; it does not yet establish shared authority over future decisions. The November 17 reported common name, “golden bamboo,” extends my account of identification work without supplying diagnostic verification. Agreement, identification and the ecological effects of cutting require different support.

The leaf inquiry has a developing chronology. An individual look beneath the leaves preceded the proposal to investigate together. The November 18 morning recording reports research and preparation; the November 19 recording reports the brief shared session that followed on November 18. I preserve those recording times rather than rewriting the earlier preparation as though the observation had already occurred.

The tactile exercise tests a separate claim. I felt distinguishable contacts while attention shifted. Merleau-Ponty's *The Visible and the Invisible*, p. 142, allows simultaneous touching and being touched in a handshake; pp. 147–148 concern attaining the touching hand as touching. Concurrent pressure and incomplete coincidence therefore require distinct descriptions. My report can refine how I use his example without proving the ontology of flesh or another organism's experience.

The strongest sources now help explain these developments more fully. Husserl's *Analyses* §15, pp. 103–104, distinguishes renewed grounds from a changed judgment; *Ideas II* §51, pp. 200–210, already describes communication, assent, refusal and mutual determination. Stein's primary “Individual and Community,” I§3(b), distinguishes understanding another's wish from being moved by it, while I§1 distinguishes a communal experience from its adequacy to its object. Schutz's “Symbol, Reality and Society,” p. 316, gives two people the same bird as a reference while their projects differ. Bateson's learning theory keeps the problem of self-validating contexts in view. I use these passages to explain how contributions can matter differently within a developing purpose. The editions and reading pointers are gathered in [Sources and Reading](sources-and-reading.md).

The November 19 source review also corrects how I spoke about the walnut's good. Stein's non-primordiality does not mean I can only guess what might support another being. In *Finite and Eternal Being* V §16, 312–313, she distinguishes knowledge of a possible good from its attainment; §17, 315–316, differentiates goods with the beings concerned. I can investigate the walnut's conditions without possessing its experience. What I know, what I intend and what an intervention actually accomplishes remain different questions.[^inquiry19stein]

Schutz's “The Well-Informed Citizen,” 124–128, gives a substantial explanation of altered practical relevance. Pages 131–133 also help me distinguish the grounds of a report from the force of its social acceptance. Husserl's communication and motivation and Bateson's relevant circuit strengthen the explanation further. I am using **partial fulfillment** for what a practical undertaking accomplishes and leaves undone; I have not equated it with the technical phenomenological senses of fulfillment or adequacy.[^inquiry19relevance]

The darker yellow leaves on November 18 also bring Husserl's account of association and recollection into the inquiry. In the *Analyses*, §§37–39, pp. 229–234, a present appearance can awaken an earlier encounter without making remembered experience identical with present perception. The color I see now, the brighter yellow I remember and the further loss I anticipate enter today's description differently. This gives the philosophical account a particular perceptual change to explain.

The broader history already distinguishes several kinds of change. The October swing removal and November dome relocation are reported acts with effects still to be assessed. Reading revised the intended treatment of fallen leaves. The exposed ring entered explicit deliberation after the dome move even though I had seen and protected it earlier. The oak attribution changed when the break became visible through the walnut's bare canopy. Earlier oak care, familiarity with the walnut and opposition to bamboo show that a relation can matter to a purpose without being equally intimate or welcomed. [How the Inquiry Developed](how-the-inquiry-developed.md) and the dated sits retain that full chronology.

My affirmative proposal connects the formation of a purpose, its continuing specification and revision, and the different evidence needed to judge its fulfillment. A purpose can leave visible lives out. A return can reopen it, and inquiry can also confirm it. The absence of a changed answer is not itself a defeater; nor is a richer explanation sufficient evidence that the answer remains responsive. The particular lives concerned continue beyond what I intend, and their good is not settled by the agreement through which an undertaking becomes ours.

For the separate *Ecology of the Cross*, I am developing how participation in Christ might form the purposes a community carries together. Romans 15 connects the neighbor's good and mutual reception with Christ's example. The ecological extension requires its own theological argument. The garden remains a prospective application, with the place's history, users, living conditions and maintenance still to be learned.

The public record also needs to show its own limits. [Who has a say in the record?](../method/about-the-practice.md#who-has-a-say-in-the-record) analyzes what selection, representation, community knowledge and publication do to the inquiry. That is a bounded methodological contribution, distinct from demonstrating the theory's novelty or the intervention's ecological adequacy. The [research roadmap](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md) keeps those different tasks visible.


## A separate theological question

November 23 brings [Leonardo Boff's](../concepts/Leonardo%20Boff.md) cosmic Christology into the proposal. His account of cross and resurrection asks how the particular victims of cosmic history enter the fulfillment he affirms. I propose that participation in the crucified and risen Christ can form communal purposes answerable to the lives they receive and affect. The cross brings power, injury and burdens into the account; resurrection grounds hope beyond the community's achievement. With [Gregersen](../concepts/Niels%20Henrik%20Gregersen.md), I distinguish incarnation's reach from the further warrant for final salvation and ask how different creaturely histories remain significant within fulfillment. This is a theological argument with its own sources; the classroom encounter gives me an occasion to examine my vocation.[^inquiry23theology]

November 22 brings [Bonaventure](../concepts/Bonaventure.md) into the account of contemplation and return. His attention to God through and in creatures belongs to a journey enabled by Christ crucified. I want to explain how participation forms someone who returns to particular lives and communal work, while taking the itinerary's passage beyond sensible objects seriously. With [Moltmann](../concepts/J%C3%BCrgen%20Moltmann.md), I compare the Spirit's creativity, community and individuation, alongside Sabbath and the demands of work on land.

I also distinguish incomplete knowledge, another life's independence, creature–Creator difference and damaged relations needing repair. My account of reconciliation has to explain which relation it proposes to transform. [Harris](../concepts/Melanie%20Harris.md) changes the practical starting questions: whose knowledge guides an inquiry, who can revise its purpose, who does the work and who bears its burdens. The garden and the remembered utility-pruning relationship remain prospective cases for that fuller investigation. The [November 22 reflection](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-22) carries the selected source references and the distinction between those texts and my proposal.

The *Ecology of the Cross* asks how a community formed through Christ's life, cross and resurrection might reconsider its purposes among vulnerable human and more-than-human neighbors. Congregations may profess ecological care while other ends govern concrete choices about property, maintenance, finances or mission. Whether their shared horizons can change through encounter and inquiry is a promising question, **not a completed congregational study**.

On November 20 I develop a more positive account of participation through [Gregersen](../concepts/Niels%20Henrik%20Gregersen.md). In his reading of Luther, receiving grace and faith enables active responsiveness to others' needs. His 2015 introduction also treats participation in divine life as differing with its recipient. I draw on both resources when I propose that participation in Christ can form a community's capacities to learn, deliberate and share responsibility while other creatures retain their own goods.[^inquiry20gregersen]

I keep incarnation's reach distinct from final salvation. Gregersen includes trees, grass, soil and moisture in the shared conditions of incarnation, but denies that deep incarnation logically settles universal redemption. My hope for transformed communion therefore needs its own argument. His account of suffering's place in evolution remains a difficulty to engage, and his language of preservation within divine unity cannot be equated without argument with my nonpossessive account. I am developing the theological claim while the garden remains a proposal, with no established communal transformation or ecological outcome.

Christian theological claims require textual, doctrinal and historical grounds of their own. The black walnut cannot prove incarnation, kenosis, reconciliation or resurrection. Nor may self-emptying become a justification for ecological injury or material dispossession.

The November 11 sit opens one further line. Leviticus 25 describes a sabbath for the land in which what comes up of itself (τὰ αὐτόματα ἀναβαίνοντα, 25:5) is left unreaped, and Moltmann's *God in Creation* reads that sabbath year as the land's own feast. I'm treating the winter commitment as an analogy to that practiced limit on possession, and no more than an analogy. The chapter's institution is communal and scheduled, and its social hierarchies have to be faced along with its rest. The November 12 sit asks whether the incarnation's scope bears on that same ring. John 1:14 says the Word became flesh (σάρξ) and dwelt (ἐσκήνωσεν, from σκηνόω, to pitch a tent) among us (ἐν ἡμῖν). Gregersen's account of deep incarnation develops a creaturely scope from Jesus' particular embodied life while refusing to call every event an incarnation of God. I'm also carrying Maximus's distinction between a nature's λόγος and its τρόπος (mode) as a question about whether transformed relation can leave creaturely difference intact. Both are reading obligations. Neither is a result from the yard.

The November 13 sit adds Luke 13:6–9. An owner reports repeated visits seeking fruit, and a gardener asks him to leave the tree another year (ἄφες αὐτὴν καὶ τοῦτο τὸ ἔτος), proposing digging and manure. The story does not narrate that cultivation or its outcome. The verb ἀφίημι belongs to the same word family as ἄφεσις, release, in Luke 4:18 and the Greek of Leviticus 25:10. This opens a possible theological connection, not proof that “leave it” means “forgive” here or that Luke intends an allusion to Leviticus. The seventh-year land sabbath and fiftieth-year jubilee are distinct provisions. The gardener retains possible cutting in verse 9, so patient cultivation has not displaced every judgment about fruitfulness. The checked editions and Wirzba's separate theological reading are recorded in the sit's source notes.

The November 14 comparison with [Maximus](../concepts/Maximus%20the%20Confessor.md) makes mediation a more definite question. *Ambiguum* 41 gives humans a unifying vocation fulfilled in Christ, with an ordered ascent and the overcoming of some divisions. Its account of Christ gathering all things in himself (§§6, 9) requires me to explain how participating in that mediation differs from claiming human control over other creatures. A targeted check of *Ambiguum* 42 §26 also supplies a primary source for the nature/mode distinction, in the context of incarnation and divine innovation. It does not show that ordinary ecological care leaves a plant's biological condition unchanged. These sources sharpen the theological proposal without making Maximus its predetermined anchor.

The November 15 sit brings Romans 15:1–3 and 7 into this question. Paul connects bearing with others and seeking the neighbor's good with Christ's example, and mutual welcome with Christ's reception. My constructive proposal is that participation in Christ can help form a community's purpose so that others' good enters from the beginning. Extending that claim to other creatures requires further theological argument and knowledge of affected lives. A possible church-garden proposal gives me a place to investigate who shapes the end, whose reasons matter and what costs the community itself will bear; it is not yet a record of shared decision or ecological benefit. [Romans 15, SBLGNT](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans+15&version=SBLGNT).

The November 18 sit brings Matthew 25:31–46 alongside this proposal. The care narrated there acquires a further Christological significance when the king identifies himself with those served. That helps me distinguish a later understanding of an act from its earlier purpose. Luke 10:25–37 also keeps neighborliness connected to enacted responsibility. I am developing a creaturely extension through the argument about incarnation, while asking what naming the walnut a neighbor requires me to learn and undertake. The theological interpretation needs to remain answerable to the particular life it concerns. [November 18 reflection and sources](../field-notes/2026-11-18%20-%20What%20We%20Are%20Trying%20to%20Do.md).

The November 19 meal case gives me a further question from [1 Corinthians 11:17–34](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1%20Corinthians%2011%3A17-34&version=NRSVUE): how can a practice's received purpose question the arrangements through which it is carried? I propose that participation in Christ can form a community's purposes before a disruption and help guide its response. An ordinary congregational dinner supplies a comparison; its changed arrangement does not prove either failure or adequate care for everyone. Extending this account to other creatures calls for knowledge of their particular lives and goods.

## What remains open

The strongest philosophical burden is to show whether a relationship with another independently organized life helps **constitute** intentional directedness, rather than only causally influencing learning, motivating a new belief, specifying a beneficiary, or setting an external norm of adequate care. Actual organism-specific benefits and possible conflicts of goods require further ecological evidence. Community formation requires its own social and historical investigation. Theological participation requires a separately warranted Christology and a case that can withstand concerns about power, suffering and whose voices count.

The account of the method now has its own sustained comparison in [What this practice inherits](../method/about-the-practice.md#what-this-practice-inherits). I compare the same two correction histories through environmental phenomenology, reflexive autoethnography and open notebook science. That explains what I inherit and what a reader might reuse; the usefulness of the procedure still needs an outside reader's test.

My present work is to **write the constructive argument while continuing to test it**, with each encounter contributing what its evidence can support.

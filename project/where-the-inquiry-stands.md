*Updated November 10, 2026 · Black Walnut Field Notes · An argument in progress*

I've been returning regularly to a black walnut in Spartanburg, South Carolina, keeping dated recordings, observations, photographs and research notes. The practice began with a phenomenological question about how another living being appears. It has opened a further question about **how the world participates in the directedness of individual and communal consciousness**: not only what we notice or learn about living beings, but how independent lives and material processes may shape what we are consciously directed toward, what our purposes are for, and what could fulfill or correct those purposes.

That is the affirmative starting point of [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md). It is not only a practice of closer attention or a better environmental ethic. My ways of noticing and acting have changed during this inquiry, but the changing walnut, remembered encounters, reading, biological research and prior commitments all enter that history. A documented change in my purposes is not yet proof that the tree constitutes a distinct conscious act or that an existing philosophical account cannot explain it. [The positive formation question is now public Issue #14](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14).

The [Essays section](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/essays/whose-good.md) carries the working philosophical argument. This page states where the inquiry stands. For dated developments, earlier claims, setbacks and revisions, see [How the Inquiry Developed](how-the-inquiry-developed.md) and the individual Field Notes.

## The affirmative question

**The world is not only what consciousness is directed toward. Its independent lives and processes may participate in the very directedness of consciousness, even while remaining irreducibly other.**

That is the affirmative working proposal. Ecological intentionality concerns conscious directedness **toward and through** ecological relationships. It includes reception: I don't author every possibility or limit that becomes significant in a practical act. It includes asymmetrical reciprocity and **intimacy with remainder**: real relations may form purposes while other lives continue beyond what I know, intend or control. Historical learning is one aspect, but the question also concerns **present intentional activity, anticipated futures, and genuinely shared communal purposes**. The proposal does not require one fused consciousness in a community or a humanlike co-intender in a tree.

The issue is whether a living other's independent activity and possible good can participate in specifying a particular intended end, rather than only supply a new object of concern or an after-the-fact standard for success. That is a philosophical hypothesis we are constructing and testing, not a demonstrated novel act-type.

The black walnut has a life I did not author. Its development and possible goods are not reducible to the meanings I form about it. My account must nevertheless distinguish its independently existing life from my beliefs, memories, representations, learned habits and intentions. A picture of a living walnut can mediate a relationship to that actual life; an artificial model can also reorganize expectations. The contrast between living and artificial things is consequential, but it cannot by itself establish a new intentional structure.

Husserl's genetic and motivated intentionality, Edith Stein's accounts of living forms and others, Merleau-Ponty's late flesh, Bateson's ecological learning and other ecological theories are serious resources and rivals. The goal isn't to defeat predecessors before constructing an argument. It is to develop a positive account and establish what each explanation can and cannot warrant. The [research roadmap](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md) records open tests rather than predetermined victories.

## The two phenomenological burdens

The October 21 examination outline asks two related questions that remain distinct. **What does late Merleau-Ponty's flesh explain beyond Husserl and early Merleau-Ponty?** And **what, if anything, does ecological intentionality explain beyond those accounts?** Both remain valuable tests, but now serve the **positive constructive question about conscious directedness received through relationships with independently active lives and material processes**, rather than making rivalry the entire argument.

Late flesh may change the ontological starting point of perception, emphasizing differentiation within a sensible field. That doesn't follow just from a hand touching bark, and it doesn't show that the walnut shares my experience. The separate ecological proposal must account for particular organisms, possible conflicting goods, biological inquiry, historical conditions, and the constitution or transformation of actual purposes. See [Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md), [Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md), and [the rival-theory issues #1–4](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md).

## What the record supports

The [November 10 sit, *A Future Not Mine Alone*](../field-notes/2026-11-10%20-%20A%20Future%20Not%20Mine%20Alone.md) develops an additional temporal dimension of directedness. I anticipated rapid seasonal leaf fall, yet the lower canopy's actual thinning surprised me in its *extent and pace*. That adjusts an expectation without showing that the dome-care intention changed: in the recorded sit, the relocation remained unperformed. An intentional future can be definite about the beneficiary and the project while still open to what independently unfolding lives may require of its fulfillment. This is a **constructive philosophical account in progress**, not a demonstrated new basic conscious act. Mark 4:26–29, introduced in the prepared sit, raises a separate theological question about action and growth beyond the sower's control; it does not establish a congregational outcome.

The [November 9 sit, *Directed Through This World*](../field-notes/2026-11-09%20-%20Directed%20Through%20This%20World.md) provides a new **prepared public draft** whose publication remains pending at the time of this source edit. It reports that before recording I tested the children's climbing dome enough to judge I could move it, then stated an intention to relocate it for the walnut's possible good. This distinguishes an experienced **practical “I can”** from an intended end and from the independent evidence needed to know whether a move actually benefits the tree. **The move has not been reported completed in this sit**, and root injury has not been diagnosed. A second conscious act of looking at bark raises a different kind of remainder: visible surface, inferred living processes and the continuing independence of the organism. The stronger belief I voiced about conscious activity in trees, rivers, rocks and soil is a **metaphysical proposition for further argument**, not a directly seen result.

Repeated visits, revised questions and several practical decisions document a developing relation, with different evidentiary strengths. The removal of a swing was enacted; changing the plan for walnut leaf litter was documented before the November 8 report of leaving leaves in place for a bounded interval. A possible relocation of play equipment near a root remains **prospective**, with no diagnosis of injury. None of these cases measures the walnut's benefit, establishes the inhabitants of the litter, or proves the necessity of a new philosophy of consciousness. See [November 1](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/field-notes/2026-11-01%20-%20After%20the%20Swing.md), [November 6](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/field-notes/2026-11-06%20-%20When%20Another%20Life%20Changes%20the%20End.md), [November 7](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/field-notes/2026-11-07%20-%20Whose%20Good%20Governs%20the%20Act.md), and [November 8](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/field-notes/2026-11-08%20-%20What%20This%20Life%20Makes%20Possible.md).

The inquiry has also produced six smaller, qualified [public findings](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md#six-dated-qualified-public-findings), including differences among kinds of absence, temporal recognition of livingness, a correction to an earlier interpretation of Stein on vegetal empathy, the distinction between co-givenness and organismic unity, the difference between perceptual givenness and permission, and the need for historical sources beyond visible traces. These corrections matter without proving plant consciousness or a special act-type. The public method distinguishes observation, inference, remembered expectation, interpretation and inaccessible remainder.

## A separate theological question

The *Ecology of the Cross* asks how a community formed through Christ's life, cross and resurrection might reconsider its purposes among vulnerable human and more-than-human neighbors. Congregations may profess ecological care while other ends govern concrete choices about property, maintenance, finances or mission. Whether their shared horizons can change through encounter and inquiry is a promising question, **not a completed congregational study**.

Christian theological claims require textual, doctrinal and historical grounds of their own. The black walnut cannot prove incarnation, kenosis, reconciliation or resurrection. Nor may self-emptying become a justification for ecological injury or material dispossession. See the [affirmative theological research issue #5](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/5) and the dated research history for the theological development.

## What remains open

The strongest philosophical burden is to show whether a relationship with another independently organized life helps **constitute** intentional directedness, rather than only causally influencing learning, motivating a new belief, specifying a beneficiary, or setting an external norm of adequate care. Actual organism-specific benefits and possible conflicts of goods require further ecological evidence. Community formation requires its own social and historical investigation. Theological participation requires a separately warranted Christology and a case that can withstand concerns about power, suffering and whose voices count.

For the present, the work is to **write the constructive argument while continuing to test it**, not ask each morning's walnut visit to prove novelty. The [public roadmap](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md) and dated research history document how the questions change.

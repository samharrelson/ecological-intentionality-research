*Updated November 8, 2026 · Black Walnut Field Notes · An argument in progress*

I've been returning regularly to a black walnut in Spartanburg, South Carolina, keeping dated recordings, observations, photographs and research notes. The practice began with a phenomenological question about how another living being appears. It has opened a further question about **how encounters with independently living beings participate in the history of what individuals and communities can notice, value and intend**.

That is the affirmative starting point of [ecological intentionality](../concepts/Ecological%20Intentionality.md). It is not only a practice of closer attention or a better environmental ethic. My ways of noticing and acting have changed during this inquiry, but the changing walnut, remembered encounters, reading, biological research and prior commitments all enter that history. A documented change in my purposes is not yet proof that the tree constitutes a distinct conscious act or that an existing philosophical account cannot explain it. [The positive formation question is now public Issue #14](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14).

The [Essays section](../essays/whose-good.md) carries the working philosophical argument. This page states where the inquiry stands. For dated developments, earlier claims, setbacks and revisions, see [How the Inquiry Developed](how-the-inquiry-developed.md) and the [individual Field Notes](../field-notes/).

## The affirmative question

Can conscious directedness be understood as historically formed through asymmetrical relationships with other living beings that do not share our intentions? The proposal concerns both individual habits and the possibility of genuinely communal horizons of attention and purpose, without attributing one fused consciousness to a community or treating a nonhuman being as a human-style co-intender.

The black walnut has a life I did not author. Its development and possible goods are not reducible to the meanings I form about it. My account must nevertheless distinguish its independently existing life from my beliefs, memories, representations, learned habits and intentions. A picture of a living walnut can mediate a relationship to that actual life; an artificial model can also reorganize expectations. The contrast between living and artificial things is consequential, but it cannot by itself establish a new intentional structure.

Husserl's genetic and motivated intentionality, Edith Stein's accounts of living forms and others, Merleau-Ponty's late flesh, Bateson's ecological learning and other ecological theories are serious resources and rivals. The goal isn't to defeat predecessors before constructing an argument. It is to develop a positive account and establish what each explanation can and cannot warrant. The [research roadmap](../RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md) records open tests rather than predetermined victories.

## The two phenomenological burdens

The October 21 examination outline asks two related questions that remain distinct. **What does late Merleau-Ponty's flesh explain beyond Husserl and early Merleau-Ponty?** And **what, if anything, does ecological intentionality explain beyond those accounts?** Both are still active, but they now serve the broader constructive question about the historical formation of intentional life.

Late flesh may change the ontological starting point of perception, emphasizing differentiation within a sensible field. That doesn't follow just from a hand touching bark, and it doesn't show that the walnut shares my experience. The separate ecological proposal must account for particular organisms, possible conflicting goods, biological inquiry, historical conditions, and the constitution or transformation of actual purposes. See [Husserl](../concepts/Husserl.md), [Merleau-Ponty](../concepts/Maurice%20Merleau-Ponty.md), and [the rival-theory issues #1–4](../RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md).

## What the record supports

Repeated visits, revised questions and several practical decisions document a developing relation, with different evidentiary strengths. The removal of a swing was enacted; changing the plan for walnut leaf litter was documented before the November 8 report of leaving leaves in place for a bounded interval. A possible relocation of play equipment near a root remains **prospective**, with no diagnosis of injury. None of these cases measures the walnut's benefit, establishes the inhabitants of the litter, or proves the necessity of a new philosophy of consciousness. See [November 1](../field-notes/2026-11-01%20-%20After%20the%20Swing.md), [November 6](../field-notes/2026-11-06%20-%20When%20Another%20Life%20Changes%20the%20End.md), [November 7](../field-notes/2026-11-07%20-%20Whose%20Good%20Governs%20the%20Act.md), and [November 8](../field-notes/2026-11-08%20-%20What%20This%20Life%20Makes%20Possible.md).

The inquiry has also produced six smaller, qualified [public findings](../RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md#six-dated-qualified-public-findings), including differences among kinds of absence, temporal recognition of livingness, a correction to an earlier interpretation of Stein on vegetal empathy, the distinction between co-givenness and organismic unity, the difference between perceptual givenness and permission, and the need for historical sources beyond visible traces. These corrections matter without proving plant consciousness or a special act-type. The public method distinguishes observation, inference, remembered expectation, interpretation and inaccessible remainder.

## A separate theological question

The *Ecology of the Cross* asks how a community formed through Christ's life, cross and resurrection might reconsider its purposes among vulnerable human and more-than-human neighbors. Congregations may profess ecological care while other ends govern concrete choices about property, maintenance, finances or mission. Whether their shared horizons can change through encounter and inquiry is a promising question, **not a completed congregational study**.

Christian theological claims require textual, doctrinal and historical grounds of their own. The black walnut cannot prove incarnation, kenosis, reconciliation or resurrection. Nor may self-emptying become a justification for ecological injury or material dispossession. See the [affirmative theological research issue #5](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/5) and the [dated research history](how-the-inquiry-developed.md) for the theological development.

## What remains open

The strongest philosophical burden is to show whether a relationship with another independently organized life helps **constitute** intentional directedness, rather than only causally influencing learning, motivating a new belief, specifying a beneficiary, or setting an external norm of adequate care. Actual organism-specific benefits and possible conflicts of goods require further ecological evidence. Community formation requires its own social and historical investigation. Theological participation requires a separately warranted Christology and a case that can withstand concerns about power, suffering and whose voices count.

For the present, the work is to **write the constructive argument while continuing to test it**, not ask each morning's walnut visit to prove novelty. The [public roadmap](../RESEARCH-ROADMAP.md) and [dated research history](how-the-inquiry-developed.md) document how the questions change.

*First, the seeing. Then, the keeping.*

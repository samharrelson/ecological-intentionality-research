# Ecological Intentionality: Public Research Roadmap

*Opened October 30, 2026. An evolving set of philosophical and theological questions, not a schedule of conclusions.*

This roadmap accompanies the [visual research board](https://github.com/users/samharrelson/projects/1/views/4), the [Black Walnut Field Notes](https://ecologicalintentionality.org), the [current inquiry](project/where-the-inquiry-stands.md), and the [dated research history](project/how-the-inquiry-developed.md). It presents a small set of **public research problems** that readers can follow, discuss, and see revised as the work develops. The board is a navigational view; the issues contain the full research questions and source links.

**The affirmative starting point is the ecological directedness of intentional life.** Dated Black Walnut Field Notes document changes in what I notice, anticipate, value and intend, including reported transfer into ordinary situations beyond the tree. That history matters, but the larger constructive question concerns **present individual and shared conscious directedness**: whether relationships with independently active lives and material places can participate in specifying what an intention is *for*, its possible futures and its fulfillment, while those realities remain irreducibly other. This positive problem deserves an account *before* asking whether an established philosophical framework is inadequate. The strongest explanations from Husserl, Stein, Merleau-Ponty, Bateson, Ruyer, Indigenous relational thought and other sources remain serious tests, not obstacles to be dismissed. The work must still distinguish recorded change, its multiple possible causes, normatively adequate care, and a distinctive constitutive claim. In parallel, *The Ecology of the Cross* asks a separate constructive theological question about incarnation, crucifixion, resurrection, and creaturely participation.

## November 9–12: an enacted purpose, its return, and two bounded criteria

Four sits carried one practical project from a stated intention to the morning after it was carried out. - **[November 9, *Directed Through This World*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-09):** I named an intention to move the children's climbing dome for the walnut's possible good and reported testing whether I could move it. Being able to act, what the act is for, doing it, and whether it helps are four different things. The trial changed only the first.
- **[November 10, *A Future Not Mine Alone*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-10):** the canopy thinned faster than I'd pictured. That corrected an expectation. It didn't change what I intended, and the dome hadn't moved.
- **[November 11, *Done Isn't Finished*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-11):** I moved the dome. Whether that helps the tree is unknown. A ring of low plants where it had stood became something I had to decide about, and I committed to leaving it through winter. I'd seen those plants for two years and had planned the move for days without considering them, because my care was aimed at the root. I withdrew my spoken claim of "a real case of goods in conflict" as ahead of the evidence. Husserl's lectures on willing and Stein's *Finite and Eternal Being* V §§16–17 already separate a completed action from a fulfilled end, so that distinction is inherited.
- **[November 12, *Who Asked for the Ring*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-12):** I tried a fuller shape for the account: three moments (aim, act, return) and three positions around a purpose (who it's for, who it touches without meaning to, who it leaves out). In this sequence, attention after the move helped bring the ring into explicit consideration. Return is one way a purpose can remain answerable; receptivity can also revise a purpose before and during action. A return that confirms an expectation or commitment can be responsive if the inquiry admits evidence capable of challenging it. Confirmation alone is not a defeater. Reading Clemson's guidance on liriope made a late-winter cutback thinkable as care, with no decision taken. Remembering that the practice of not mowing there began with someone else's request showed me that I'd changed the reason for a shared practice without asking. I narrowed two of my own spoken claims in the public note. Merleau-Ponty's account of institution is the predecessor this now has to be compared against.

## November 8 evening: a positive account and two earned methodological/negative results

The central question has sharpened. **Ecological intentionality investigates whether individual and communal conscious directedness can be received, specified and transformed within relationships with independent lives and material places, rather than being only directed *toward* ecological objects.** A positive proposal now integrates **reception, directedness, asymmetrical reciprocity and intimacy with remainder**. The other lives and processes at stake may participate in shaping an intentional end and the future it projects without becoming conscious co-intenders or being exhausted by human aims.

This is a **public working account (v0.1)**, not proof of a new basic act-type or of the failure of phenomenology. ## Preserve what failed, not only what survived

A useful public research record includes substantive counterarguments, discarded interpretations, and negative outcomes **with their reasons**. Treat every consequential revision as an accountable transition:

**Earlier claim → field observation or source → strongest objection/counterexample → decision to retain, narrow, withdraw, or suspend → remaining uncertainty → dated public record (when safe and warranted).**

The public issue is a navigable record of the question; its checkboxes indicate publicly evidenced deliverables, not philosophical proof. Update an issue only for work its criteria actually describe. A question may move between board statuses, gain a better formulation, or be superseded. Do not rewrite away the previous rationale merely to make the new position look inevitable. When a question no longer distinguishes anything, explain the reason publicly and close or mark it superseded rather than creating an appearance of steady cumulative success.

The underlying field journal, intimate encounters, consent-sensitive knowledge, exploratory drafts, and unpublished research remain private. A record of revision can be transparent without being exhaustive. Publication is a governed scholarly choice, not an automatic Git synchronization rule.

This is itself an unresolved methodological research problem, tracked in [issue #7](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/7); relevant comparisons with open notebooks, reflexive field research, and established provenance practices are still required.

## How to read the status of a research question

The public board groups questions by these research stages. The status indicates the stage of investigation, not how likely a theory is to be correct. Issue states are not philosophical verdicts.

- **Open question:** a problem is clearly stated, but work has not yet produced a provisional answer.
- **Investigating:** sources, field examples, and serious objections are being compared.
- **Evidence review:** an emerging response is being tested against its strongest rivals and possible defeaters.
- **Public finding:** a dated, qualified public analysis is ready to link and scrutinize.
- **Revised or rejected:** a formulation was narrowed, defeated, or superseded; the history of the change remains visible.

An issue can remain open even when public notes or partial findings are published. Closing an issue may mark a question as answered, revised, superseded, or no longer useful; closure is not proof of a theory.

## Evidence and source policy

Each issue should say what is being claimed, what an existing theory might already explain, and what observation or textual argument could challenge the candidate claim.

Field encounters are not retroactive illustrations. In reporting them I distinguish what was perceptually given, what was inferred, what was remembered or anticipated, what was interpreted through a concept, and what remained inaccessible. Biological, phenomenological, metaphysical, ethical, and theological claims have different warrants.

A source may be a **reading target**, a **selected-passage check**, or a **completed and page-verified reading**. Those statuses should not be blurred, and bibliographic or interpretive revisions are part of the public research record.

## Public and private boundaries

This is **not** a copy of my private doctoral research notebook or my task list. Public issues link to curated public notes and sources, not raw field recordings, family details, private correspondence, Zotero storage, or unpublished Comps material. The GitHub Project, when configured, will organize these issues; it will not serve as a publishing gate or decide what crosses the private/public boundary.

Readers are welcome to offer substantive references, counterarguments, and corrections through the linked public issues. Disagreement and failed hypotheses are meaningful findings when the evidence supports them.

The purpose of the roadmap is to make the inquiry more answerable to others, not to make it appear more settled.

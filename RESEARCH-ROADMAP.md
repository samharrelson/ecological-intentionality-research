# Ecological Intentionality: Public Research Roadmap

*Opened October 30, 2026. An evolving set of philosophical and theological questions, not a schedule of conclusions.*

This roadmap accompanies the [visual research board](https://github.com/users/samharrelson/projects/1/views/4), the [Black Walnut Field Notes](https://ecologicalintentionality.org), the [current inquiry](project/where-the-inquiry-stands.md), and the [dated research history](project/how-the-inquiry-developed.md). It presents a small set of **public research problems** that readers can follow, discuss, and see revised as the work develops. The board is a navigational view; the issues contain the full research questions and source links.

**The affirmative starting point is the ecological formation of intentional life.** Dated Black Walnut Field Notes now document changes in what I notice, anticipate, value and intend, including reported transfer into ordinary situations beyond the tree. The walnut has not just become more meaningful to me; its independently developing life and the history of our encounter have participated in the formation of my intentional habits and practical purposes. This positive phenomenon deserves an account *before* the question of whether any established theory is inadequate. The strongest explanations from Husserl, Stein, Merleau-Ponty, Bateson, Ruyer, Indigenous relational thought and other sources remain serious tests, not obstacles to be dismissed. The work must still distinguish recorded change, its multiple possible causes, normatively adequate care, and a distinctive constitutive claim. In parallel, *The Ecology of the Cross* asks a separate constructive theological question about incarnation, crucifixion, resurrection, and creaturely participation.

## November 7: the affirmative formation question now leads, and two bounded criteria advance

The [published November 7 field note, *Whose Good Governs the Act?*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-07) and the now-public [Husserl reconstruction](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/blob/main/concepts/Husserl.md) support a new ordering of the investigation. I am not only asking whether the walnut has acquired additional significance *as an object of perception*. In repeated encounter its independent seasonal development and the inquiry it prompted have helped reshape what I notice, anticipate and intend. [October 29's public note](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-10-29) also reports changed attention to other drivers and altered practices elsewhere in the yard; [November 1](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-01) documents the removal of the swing after a delay, and [November 6](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-06) records the revision of a weekend leaf-litter plan. These are different evidentiary forms: first-person accounts, intentions, and enacted actions. They support a meaningful cumulative phenomenon, **not** a controlled proof of the walnut's sole causal power or of a new intentional act-type.

The affirmative research question is now tracked explicitly in **[workstream #14](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/14)**. [Issue #1](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/1) retains the Husserlian redundancy challenge as a **test** of the constructive account, not its sole organizing purpose. Husserl's strongest layered account has now been published as a charitable, section-based reconstruction, so its **published reconstruction** checkbox is complete. The separate claim that a living being contributes an irreducible *constitutive structure* to a conscious act remains unproved, as does the decisive discriminating comparison. The distinction between the life of an intended beneficiary and the nature of the photograph or model through which that being is encountered remains important.

The new plant observations in the November 7 note (ivy visibly climbing the walnut; provisional and researcher-crosschecked ivy, violet and lilyturf identifications) also warrant an additional limited milestone under [#3](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/3): a public account differentiates **co-presence, independent organismic life, actual ecological effects, possibly competing goods and appropriate action**, rather than saying everything is connected. This conceptual distinction is established; **a measured conflict of biological goods has not been demonstrated**.

**Board-stage accounting:** #1 remains appropriately in **Evidence review**; #3 and #4 remain **Investigating** while their strongest comparisons are incomplete. #7 has enough substantive revision/provenance work to justify **Investigating**, though changing a GitHub Projects column requires a separate verified board action. The new #14 should enter **Investigating**, not Public finding, until a longitudinal reconstruction and the rival explanation tests have been completed. A board's position describes the work's stage, not the truth or falsity of a philosophical thesis.

**Why this ordering matters:** the emerging argument has an affirmative achievement to defend even if Husserlian genetic phenomenology or Batesonian ecological learning explains some or all of its mechanisms. Reconstructing those accounts should improve, narrow, or challenge the positive proposal; the task is not to diminish the human subject or inflate nonhuman subjectivity. Vanessa Watts's specific Indigenous Place-Thought argument also challenges the privilege given to a human perceiver as the starting point, without being folded uncritically into European phenomenology.

## November 6: an improved discriminating case, not a new completed workstream

The published [November 6 sit, *When Another Life Changes the End*](https://ecologicalintentionality.org/sits/2026-11-06), records a change in a **planned weekend action** and the researcher's own interpretation of what the action was *for*. A planned repetition of leaf mowing had a tidiness purpose; after studying intact leaf litter as overwintering habitat, the plan became leaving those leaves whole. That revised intention **predated** the morning recording. October 28 already documents an enduring ecological concern, so the new distinction is between a **standing commitment** and the **operative purpose** governing this particular act.

This provides dated material for [#1](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/1) (Husserlian motivation and possible redundancy), [#6](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/6) (what the sit actually observes versus the earlier scientific investigation and intended action), [#5](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/5) (professed ecclesial commitments versus enacted ends), and [#7](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/7) (a public record preserving corrections and rivals). Stein's care for distinct creaturely goods continues to pressure [#3](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/3), but a conflict of actual organismic goods has not been established.

**Rival explanations must remain active.** Husserl's *Ideas II* §§55–56, existing theories of practical agency (including De Monticelli and Schütz as reconstructed by Barber), and Stein's attention to possible versus actual goods may already explain a changed immediate purpose. The claim that an independent organism's life **constitutes the ongoing conscious act** requires further argument. No field experiment, plant inventory, measured leaf-litter outcome, full primary-text comparison, or completed congregational case resulted from the sit.

**Roadmap accounting:** all seven foundational workstreams remain open; the six September–October qualified findings remain qualified. No criterion has been newly checked solely because the November 6 record is longer or more constructive. This dated update is part of the roadmap's methodological transparency, not a new research victory count. ## Preserve what failed, not only what survived

A useful public research record includes substantive counterarguments, discarded interpretations, and negative outcomes **with their reasons**. Treat every consequential revision as an accountable transition:

**Earlier claim → field observation or source → strongest objection/counterexample → decision to retain, narrow, withdraw, or suspend → remaining uncertainty → dated public record (when safe and warranted).**

The public issue is a navigable record of the question; its checkboxes indicate publicly evidenced deliverables, not philosophical proof. Update an issue only for work its criteria actually describe. A question may move between board statuses, gain a better formulation, or be superseded. Do not rewrite away the previous rationale merely to make the new position look inevitable. When a question no longer distinguishes anything, explain the reason publicly and close or mark it superseded rather than creating an appearance of steady cumulative success.

The underlying field journal, intimate encounters, consent-sensitive knowledge, exploratory drafts, and unpublished research remain private. A record of revision can be transparent without being exhaustive. Publication is a governed scholarly choice, not an automatic Git synchronization rule.

This is itself an unresolved methodological research problem, tracked in [issue #7](https://github.com/samharrelson/ecological-intentionality-research/issues/7); relevant comparisons with open notebooks, reflexive field research, and established provenance practices are still required.

## How to read the status of a research question

The public board groups questions by these research stages. The status indicates the stage of investigation, not how likely a theory is to be correct. Issue states are not philosophical verdicts.

- **Open question:** a problem is clearly stated, but work has not yet produced a provisional answer.
- **Investigating:** sources, field examples, and serious objections are being compared.
- **Evidence review:** an emerging response is being tested against its strongest rivals and possible defeaters.
- **Public finding:** a dated, qualified public analysis is ready to link and scrutinize.
- **Revised or rejected:** a formulation was narrowed, defeated, or superseded; the history of the change remains visible.

An issue can remain open even when public notes or partial findings are published. Closing an issue may mark a question as answered, revised, superseded, or no longer useful; closure is not proof of a theory.

## Evidence and source policy

Each issue should say what is being claimed, what an existing theory might already explain, and what observation or textual argument could challenge the candidate claim.

Field encounters are not retroactive illustrations. In reporting them I distinguish what was perceptually given, what was inferred, what was remembered or anticipated, what was interpreted through a concept, and what remained inaccessible. Biological, phenomenological, metaphysical, ethical, and theological claims have different warrants.

A source may be a **reading target**, a **selected-passage check**, or a **completed and page-verified reading**. Those statuses should not be blurred, and bibliographic or interpretive revisions are part of the public research record.

## Public and private boundaries

This is **not** a copy of my private doctoral research notebook or my task list. Public issues link to curated public notes and sources, not raw field recordings, family details, private correspondence, Zotero storage, or unpublished Comps material. The GitHub Project, when configured, will organize these issues; it will not serve as a publishing gate or decide what crosses the private/public boundary.

Readers are welcome to offer substantive references, counterarguments, and corrections through the linked public issues. Disagreement and failed hypotheses are meaningful findings when the evidence supports them.

The purpose of the roadmap is to make the inquiry more answerable to others, not to make it appear more settled.
